# JLPT Mistake Bank and Error Analysis Template (間違いバンク・誤答分析シート)

Simply taking quizzes and checking the score provides little long-term retention. Rapid score improvement occurs when you document, categorize, and systematically re-test your recurring mistakes using a **間違いバンク (*Machigai Bank* / Mistake Bank)**.

This reference card provides the diagnostic framework, the error logging schema, and the pre-analyzed "Hall of Fame" errors that trip up Japanese learners across JLPT levels.

---

## 1. The 5 Archetypes of Japanese Quiz Mistakes

When you get a question wrong in a quiz or mock exam, categorize the root cause into one of five archetypes:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             The 5 Mistake Archetypes (誤答の5類型)      │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Type 1: Phonetic & Furigana Traps (濁音・長音・促音)     │
│ Type 2: Particle Collocation Mismatches (助詞の接続)    │
│ Type 3: Nuance & Modality Overlaps (類似文法の混同)     │
│ Type 4: Beneficiary & Agency Inversion (授受・敬語の主語)│
│ Type 5: Reading Distractor Traps (同語トラップ・筆者の主張)│
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Mistake Archetype | Typical Symptom | How to Fix It |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Type 1: Phonetics** | Confusing voiced sounds (が/か), missing long vowels (お/おう), or small *tsu* (っ). | Drill writing words in pure Hiragana; pronounce words with exaggerated mora beats. |
| **Type 2: Particle Collocation** | Picking で instead of に, or を instead of に with specific verbs. | Learn verbs **paired with their particle as a single unit** (e.g. *densha ni noru*, not just *noru*). |
| **Type 3: Modality Overlap** | Choosing *hazu da* when the context demands *wake da*. | Create contrast pairs; identify whether the reason is objective expectation or logical deduction. |
| **Type 4: Agency Inversion** | Picking *oshiete ageta* when someone taught *you*. | Draw an arrow: Who acts? Who gains the favor? |
| **Type 5: Reading Trap** | Selecting an option because it copies words from the passage. | Cross out exact-word matches unless verified against the author's final opinion sentence. |

## 2. Standardized Mistake Bank Entry Template

Copy and maintain this schema in your study notes, notion database, or flashcard deck for every missed question:

```markdown
### [Mistake Card #001]
- **Date Logged**: 2026-11-03
- **Level & Section**: N3 Grammar (問題1)
- **Question Prompt**:
  昨日、図書館でこの本を（　　）。
  1. 借りました  2. 貸しました  3. 借りました  4. 買いました
- **What I Answered**: 2. 貸しました (Lent)
- **Correct Answer**: 1. 借りました (Borrowed)
- **Root Cause Archetype**: Type 4 (Agency / Directionality Confusion)
- **Why I Got It Wrong**:
  I confused 貸す (to lend to someone else) with 借りる (to borrow from someone/somewhere).
  Since I received the book from the library, it must be 借りる!
- **Core Rule / Mnemonic**:
  • 借りる (kariru) = "Borrow" (I take it in)
  • 貸す (kasu) = "Lend" (I give it out)
- **Review Checkpoint Schedule**:
  - [ ] Day 1 Re-test
  - [ ] Day 3 Re-test
  - [ ] Day 7 Re-test
  - [ ] Day 21 Mastered & Retired
```

## 3. "Hall of Fame" Recurring Quiz Traps Analyzed

These recurring traps appear with immense statistical regularity on Japanese quizzes:

### Trap 1: 勤める (*tsutomeru*) vs. 働く (*hataraku*)

```
❌ 会社で勤めています。(Using で with 勤める is WRONG!)
⭕ 会社に勤めています。(勤める takes particle に: marks affiliation)
⭕ 会社で働いています。(働く takes particle で: marks location of labor)
```

### Trap 2: 乗る (*noru*) vs. 降りる (*oriru*)

```
• Boarding a vehicle (Target of motion):
  ⭕ 電車に乗る (densha NI noru — get on the train)
• Exiting a vehicle (Point of detachment/departure):
  ⭕ 電車を降りる (densha O oriru — get off the train)
  ⚠️ Do not use から for routine transit exits on exams!
```

### Trap 3: 気がする vs. 気にする vs. 気になる

| Expression | Volition / Control | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **気がする** | Involuntary | Have a vague hunch / feeling | 雨が降る**気がする**。 (I have a feeling it'll rain.) |
| **気にする** | Conscious / Voluntary | Deliberately worry about / care | 失敗を**気にしないで**。 (Don't worry about mistakes.) |
| **気になる** | Spontaneous / Involuntary | Something weighs on mind / curious | 結果が**気になる**。 (I'm anxious about the results.) |

### Trap 4: 楽しむ (*tanoshimu*) vs. 楽しみにする (*tanoshimi ni suru*)
- **楽しむ (*tanoshimu*)**: Enjoying an event in the present or past:
  - *パーティーを楽しんだ。* (I enjoyed the party.)
- **楽しみにする (*tanoshimi ni suru*)**: Eagerly anticipating a future event:
  - *旅行を楽しみにしています。* (I am looking forward to the trip.)

### Trap 5: 治る (*naoru*) vs. 直る (*naoru*)
- **治る (Cure / Heal)**: For biological illnesses and physical injuries:
  - *風邪が治った。* (The cold healed.)
- **直る (Fix / Repair)**: For mechanical objects, clocks, computers, and errors:
  - *パソコンが直った。* (The computer got fixed.)

## 4. The 4-Interval Spaced Retirement Schedule

Do not retire an error from your Mistake Bank the first time you answer it correctly! Cognitive illusions create a false sense of mastery immediately after reading an explanation.

```
Error Logged ──► Retest Day 1 ──► Retest Day 3 ──► Retest Day 7 ──► Retest Day 21 ──► [RETIRED]
                   (If fail)       (If fail)       (If fail)       (If fail)
                      │               │               │               │
                      └───────────────┴───────────────┴───────────────┴──► Reset to Day 1
```

> [!TIP]
> Only archive an error card as **"Mastered"** once you have answered it correctly across **three distinct review sessions spanning at least three weeks**.
