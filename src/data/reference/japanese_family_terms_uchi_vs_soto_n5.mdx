# Japanese Family Terms: Your Own vs. Other People's (家族の呼び方)

In Japanese culture, how you refer to a family member depends entirely on **who you are speaking to**. This dynamic is governed by the sociological concept of **Uchi (内 — inside / in-group)** versus **Soto (外 — outside / out-group)**:

1. **When speaking to an outsider (Soto)** about your own family: You must use **humble, plain terms** without any honorific prefixes (*o-*) or suffixes (*-san*).
2. **When speaking about someone else's family (Soto)**: You must use **respectful, polite terms** with honorifics like *o-*, *go-*, or *-san / -sama*.
3. **When speaking directly to your own family at home**: You address your elders using the respectful forms (e.g., calling your mother *okāsan*).

## 2. Practical Dialogue Scenarios

### Scenario A: Talking to your teacher or coworker about your mother
Because the teacher is outside your family circle (*Soto*), you must lower your own family (*Uchi*):
- ✅ **母は会社員です。**  
  *Haha wa kaishain desu.*  
  (My mother is a company employee.)
- ❌ *お母さんは会社員です。* (Wrong: Never elevate your own mother when speaking to an outsider!)

### Scenario B: Asking a classmate about their father
Because the classmate's father is someone else's family (*Soto*), you must elevate him:
- ✅ **田中さんのお父さんは何歳ですか。**  
  *Tanaka-san no otōsan wa nan-sai desu ka?*  
  (How old is your father, Tanaka?)
- ❌ *田中さんの父は何歳ですか。* (Rude: *Chichi* is humble and should not refer to another's father!)

### Scenario C: Speaking directly to your mother at home
Inside the house, your parents are your hierarchical superiors:
- ✅ **「お母さん、朝ごはんできた？」**  
  *Okāsan, asagohan dekita?*  
  ("Mom, is breakfast ready?")
- ❌ *「母、朝ごはんできた？」* (Unnatural/bizarre to call your mom "Haha" to her face!)

## 3. Important Nuances: Husband & Wife Terms

In modern Japanese, terms for spouses carry specific traditional vs. gender-neutral nuances:

| Term | Register / Nuance | Usage Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **夫 (おっと / *otto*)** | Neutral / Modern standard | The preferred modern term when referring to your own husband. |
| **主人 (しゅじん / *shujin*)** | Traditional ("Master") | Still widely used by older generations; some modern speakers avoid its hierarchical literal meaning. |
| **妻 (つま / *tsuma*)** | Neutral / Legal standard | Official and standard term for your own wife. |
| **家内 (かない / *kanai*)** | Traditional ("Inside the house") | Commonly used by older men; avoid when modern egalitarian nuance is desired. |
| **奥さん (おくさん / *okusan*)** | Polite / Respectful | Standard when referring to someone else's wife (*Tanaka-san no okusan*). |
| **旦那さん (だんなさん / *dannasan*)** | Polite / Conversational | Common casual-polite term for someone else's husband. |

## 4. JLPT N5 Quick Exam Tips

> [!WARNING] The Pronunciation traps: Long Vowels in Grandparents & Extended Family!
> - **おじいさん (*ojīsan* — 4 beats)** = Grandfather
> - **おじさん (*ojisan* — 3 beats)** = Uncle / Middle-aged man
> - **おばあさん (*obāsan* — 4 beats)** = Grandmother
> - **おばさん (*obasan* — 3 beats)** = Aunt / Middle-aged woman
> 
> Shortening the long vowel completely transforms the family generation!
