# Honorific and Humble Verbs (敬語)

**敬語 (*keigo*)** adjusts language to social relationships and the situation. In workplace, service, and formal contexts, choosing the right verb often matters more than adding a polite ending.

## 1. Three broad levels

| Type | Japanese term | Whose action or state is elevated? | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Polite | 丁寧語 (*teineigo*) | Makes the sentence polite to the listener | 行きます |
| Honorific | 尊敬語 (*sonkeigo*) | The action of a respected person | 先生がいらっしゃいます |
| Humble | 謙譲語 (*kenjōgo*) | The speaker or speaker's in-group, in relation to someone respected | 私が参ります |

Do not use a humble verb to lower the action of the person you are honoring, or an honorific verb for your own action. **です／ます** is polite language, but it does not by itself turn a verb into honorific or humble language.

## 2. Essential special verbs

| Basic verb | Honorific (respect another person's action) | Humble (describe one's own side) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| いる / 行く / 来る | いらっしゃる | おる (いる); 参る (行く・来る) |
| 言う | おっしゃる | 申す / 申し上げる |
| する | なさる | いたす |
| 見る | ご覧になる | 拝見する |
| 食べる / 飲む | 召し上がる | いただく |
| 知っている | ご存じだ | 存じている (a person) / 存じ上げている (a person) |
| 聞く / 訪ねる | — | 伺う |
| 会う | — | お目にかかる |
| あげる | — | 差し上げる |
| くれる | くださる | — |
| もらう | — | いただく |

The dash indicates that the table is not giving a direct special-verb counterpart for that direction; use an appropriate respectful construction if needed.

### Examples in context

- 部長はもういらっしゃいました。 — The department head has already arrived.
- 私は後ほど参ります。 — I will come/go later (humble).
- 社長がおっしゃいました。 — The company president said so.
- 私からご説明いたします。 — I will explain (humble).
- こちらの資料をご覧ください。 — Please look at these materials.
- 私が資料を拝見しました。 — I looked at the materials (humble).
- どうぞ召し上がってください。 — Please eat/drink.
- 先生に伺います。 — I will ask the teacher / visit the teacher.

## 3. Productive honorific and humble patterns

For many verbs without a special counterpart, use a productive pattern.

### Honorific: お～になる

Put the verb in its ます-stem, remove ます, and place it between **お** and **になる**:

> 読みます → お読みになる  
> 社長はこの本をお読みになりました。  
> The company president read this book.

This pattern is generally used with native Japanese verb stems. Many verbs already have a special honorific form, such as いらっしゃる or 召し上がる; prefer the established form where appropriate.

### Humble: お～する / お～いたす

Use **お + ます-stem + する / いたす** for an action the speaker performs for or in relation to someone respected:

> 持ちます → お持ちします  
> 私がお荷物をお持ちします。  
> I will carry your bag (humble).

For many Sino-Japanese compounds, **ご～する / ご～いたす** is common:

> 説明する → ご説明します / ご説明いたします  
> 私がご説明いたします。  
> I will explain (humble).

These are patterns, not universal transformations. Avoid stacking them onto a verb that already has an established special form.

## 4. Special conjugations to remember

Some honorific verbs do not follow the expected pattern:

| Dictionary form | Polite form |
| :--- | :--- |
| いらっしゃる | いらっしゃいます |
| おっしゃる | おっしゃいます |
| なさる | なさいます |
| くださる | くださいます |

These verbs use **～います**, not *～ります*, in the polite non-past.

## 5. Polite language is not always keigo

**お** and **ご** also appear in ordinary polite vocabulary, such as お名前 (name) and ご家族 (family). These nouns do not automatically make a sentence honorific. Similarly, **お～ください** is a respectful request pattern: お待ちください (Please wait).

The form **お～になる** is not appropriate for every verb, and humble forms do not automatically make a statement respectful if the rest of the sentence is abrupt. Match the entire sentence to the context.

## 6. A safe selection process

1. Decide whose action you are describing: the other person or your own side.
2. If describing a respected person's action, use honorific language; if describing your own action toward them, use humble language.
3. Choose a special verb when one is established; otherwise consider お～になる or お～する / ご～する.
4. Add ordinary politeness as needed: いらっしゃいます, 参ります, ご説明いたします.
5. When unsure in a formal interaction, use a clear, neutral polite sentence rather than inventing a keigo form.

> **Perspective note:** “In-group” depends on whom you are speaking with. When speaking to a customer, for example, you generally do not elevate your own colleague's action, even if you would use respectful language for that colleague in another context.
