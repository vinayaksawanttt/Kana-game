# Hiragana & Katakana Master Charts (五十音 - Gojūon)

Japanese uses two phonetic syllabaries, collectively known as **Kana (仮名)**:
1. **Hiragana (ひらがな)**: Curved, cursive characters derived from ancient *man'yōgana* calligraphy. Used for native Japanese words, grammatical particles, auxiliary verbs, and okurigana (inflectional endings).
2. **Katakana (カタカナ)**: Angular, geometric characters derived from isolated fragments of kanji. Used for foreign loanwords (*gairaigo*), scientific terms, plant/animal species, onomatopoeia, and visual typography/emphasis.

Both syllabaries map to the exact same 46 basic sound units organized in the **Gojūon (五十音 / 50 Sounds)** grid.

> [!WARNING] Special Particle Pronunciation Rules (N5 Mandatory)
> When serving as grammatical particles, three Hiragana change pronunciation:
> - **は (*ha*)** → pronounced **wa** as the Topic / Contrast marker (*watashi **wa***).
> - **へ (*he*)** → pronounced **e** as the Directional marker (*Tōkyō **e***).
> - **を (*wo*)** → pronounced **o** as the Direct Object marker (*mizu **o***). を is *never* used in native words—it exists solely as a grammatical particle.

### Easily Confused Hiragana Pairs
- **さ (*sa*) vs. ち (*chi*)**: さ faces right (loops down-right); ち faces left (curves left-down).
- **れ (*re*) vs. わ (*wa*) vs. ね (*ne*)**:
  - **れ**: kicks outwards to the right at the tail.
  - **わ**: curves smoothly inward like a round hoop.
  - **ね**: terminates in a complete loop (pretzel curl) at the tail.
- **め (*me*) vs. ぬ (*nu*)**: め has a clean sweeping tail; ぬ loops into a small knot at the bottom.
- **ろ (*ro*) vs. る (*ru*)**: ろ ends cleanly; る loops inward with a small bottom knot.

## 3. The 4 Notorious Katakana Look-Alikes: シ vs ツ and ソ vs ン

This is the most common reading obstacle for beginners. Pay strict attention to **stroke origin and direction**:

```
[The Upward Group: Bottom-to-Top Sweep]
  シ (shi): Left strokes aligned vertically; final stroke sweeps UP from bottom-left.
  ン (n)  : Left stroke aligned vertically; final stroke sweeps UP from bottom-left.

[The Downward Group: Top-to-Bottom Strike]
  ツ (tsu): Top strokes aligned horizontally; final stroke strikes DOWN from top-right.
  ソ (so) : Top stroke aligned horizontally; final stroke strikes DOWN from top-right.
```

| Pair | Character | Stroke Breakdown | Mnemonic |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Shi vs. Tsu** | **シ (shi)** | 3 strokes: two left dots stacked vertically, third stroke sweeps **upwards from the bottom**. | "She" looks up at the sky. |
| | **ツ (tsu)** | 3 strokes: two top dots side-by-side horizontally, third stroke sweeps **downwards from the top**. | A "Tsunami" crashes down. |
| **So vs. N** | **ソ (so)** | 2 strokes: short top tick, long strike sweeping **downwards from top right**. | "Sewing" needle points down. |
| | **ン (n)** | 2 strokes: lower tick, long sweep moving **upwards from bottom left**. | Airplane taking off into the clouds. |

### Other Katakana Confusion Pairs:
- **ウ (*u*) vs. ワ (*wa*)**: ウ has a center vertical top tick; ワ starts directly with the left downward stroke.
- **ク (*ku*) vs. タ (*ta*)**: タ has an internal horizontal crossing stroke; ク is completely open.
- **コ (*ko*) vs. ユ (*yu*)**: コ has two parallel horizontal bars; ユ has a continuous box that extends past the right.

## 4. Fundamental Stroke Order Rules (筆順)

Following correct stroke order ensures clean legibility and balanced proportions:
1. **Top to bottom**: Characters are written from the upper portion downward (e.g., 三, 言).
2. **Left to right**: Vertical or diagonal elements on the left precede those on the right (e.g., 川, い).
3. **Horizontal before vertical**: When lines cross, horizontal usually comes first (e.g., 十, 十).
4. **Center before symmetrical sides**: (e.g., 小, 水).
5. **Enclosure before inside content**: (e.g., 日, 口, 四).
6. **Bottom closing stroke last**: (e.g., 目, 田, 国).
