# Japanese Vocabulary by JLPT Level: Complete Tier Guide (N5–N1)

Building Japanese vocabulary across the **JLPT (日本語能力試験)** involves distinct linguistic shifts:
- **Beginner (N5–N4)**: Focuses on concrete physical objects, everyday native verbs (*Yamato-kotoba*), sensory adjectives, and foundational conversation.
- **Intermediate (N3)**: Bridges into abstract ideas, social relationships, daily newspaper reporting, and high-frequency 2-kanji compounds (*Jukugo*).
- **Advanced (N2–N1)**: Encompasses professional business correspondence, legal statutes, scientific reporting, nuanced emotion descriptors, and literary expressions.

---

## Morphological Evolution: How Words Elevate Across Levels

Notice how a single conceptual domain elevates in register and formality as you progress from N5 to N1:

```
[Concept: "To Change / Alter"]
  N5: 変わる (kawaru - basic intransitive)
  N4: 変える (kaeru - basic transitive)
  N3: 変化する (henka suru - 2-kanji general compound)
  N2: 改める (aratameru - formal reform) / 変更する (henkō suru - schedule/plan change)
  N1: 変革する (henkaku suru - revolutionary reform) / 変遷する (hensen suru - historical transition)

[Concept: "Difficulty / Trouble"]
  N5: 大変 (taihen - tough/hard)
  N4: 困る (komaru - be troubled)
  N3: 苦労 (kurō - hardship/toil) / 困難 (konnan - difficulty)
  N2: 障害 (shōgai - obstacle/barrier) / 弊害 (heigai - adverse effect)
  N1: 難航する (nankō suru - hit rough waters/stagnate) / 逼迫する (hippaku suru - grow acute/strained)
```
