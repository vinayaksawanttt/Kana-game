# Okurigana Rules & Patterns (送り仮名の付け方)

**Okurigana (送り仮名)** are the hiragana characters attached to a kanji root to indicate grammatical inflections and disambiguate readings (e.g., distinguishing between **食べる** *ta-beru* and **食う** *ku-u*, or between **生じる** *shō-jiru* and **生きる** *i-kiru*).

Standard Japanese okurigana is codified by the Japanese Ministry of Education and Cabinet Directives (*Naikaku Kokuji*). Understanding these rules prevents costly errors on the JLPT orthography and reading sections.

---

## 1. Rules for Verbs (動詞の送り仮名)

### Rule 1: Godan (Group 1 / U-Verbs) — Start at the Conjugating Syllable
For standard Godan verbs, only the **final syllable**—the one that alternates across the 5 vowel rows (a, i, u, e, o) during conjugation—is placed outside the kanji as okurigana:
- **書く** (*ka-ku* → 書かない, 書きます, 書けば, 書こう) — only **く** is outside.
- **飲む** (*no-mu* → 飲まない, 飲みます, 飲めば) — only **む** is outside.
- **話す** (*hana-su* → 話さない, 話します) — only **す** is outside.
- **待つ** (*ma-tsu* → 待たない, 待ちます) — only **つ** is outside.

> [!WARNING] The Notorious JLPT Exception: 行う (*Okonau*)
> The verb **行う** (*okona-u* = to conduct/perform) only writes **う** outside as okurigana:
> - ✅ **行う** (*okona-u*)
> - ❌ *行なう* (Common spelling mistake!)

---

### Rule 2: Ichidan (Group 2 / Ru-Verbs) — The Last Two Syllables
For most Ichidan verbs, the syllable preceding **る** plus **る** itself sit outside the kanji as okurigana:
- **食べる** (*ta-beru*), **起きる** (*o-kiru*), **教える** (*oshi-eru*), **疲れる** (*tsuka-reru*).
- **開ける** (*a-keru*), **始める** (*haji-meru*), **集める** (*atsu-meru*).

#### Short-Stem Exception: Single-Syllable Roots
When the semantic root of an Ichidan verb consists of just a single vowel or consonant-vowel mora, only **る** goes outside:
- **見る** (*mi-ru*), **着る** (*ki-ru*), **寝る** (*ne-ru*), **出る** (*de-ru*), **居る** (*i-ru*), **似る** (*ni-ru*).

---

### Rule 3: Transitive and Intransitive Verb Pairs Sharing a Root
When a transitive verb and an intransitive verb derive from the same underlying root, the okurigana begins from the point where their stems diverge:
- **落とす** (*o-tosu* - to drop) ↔ **落ちる** (*o-chiru* - to fall) — okurigana begins at **と** / **ち**.
- **動かす** (*ugo-kasu* - to move something) ↔ **動く** (*ugo-ku* - to move) — okurigana begins at **か** / **く**.
- **増やす** (*fu-yasu* - to increase) ↔ **増える** (*fu-eru* - to increase) — okurigana begins at **や** / **え**.

## 2. Rules for Adjectives (形容詞の送り仮名)

### Rule 1: Standard i-Adjectives — Only い Outside
For basic adjectives whose stem does not derive from a verb, only the inflectional **い** sits outside the kanji:
- **高い** (*taka-i*), **安い** (*yasu-i*), **広い** (*hiro-i*), **長い** (*naga-i*), **重い** (*omo-i*), **白い** (*shiro-i*).

### Rule 2: "-shii" Adjectives — Always Write しい Outside
When an i-adjective terminates in the classical suffix **〜しい**, both characters are written as okurigana:
- **新しい** (*atara-shii*), **楽しい** (*tano-shii*), **難しい** (*muzuka-shii*), **嬉しい** (*ure-shii*), **正しい** (*tada-shii*), **美しい** (*utsuku-shii*).

### Rule 3: Adjectives Derived from Verbs — Retain the Derived Stem
If an adjective evolved historically from a verb root, the derivative okurigana syllables are retained outside the kanji:

| Adjective | Kanji & Okurigana | Reading | Historical Verb Derivation |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Bright** | **明るい** | あか・**るい** (*aka-rui*) | From the verb **明ける** (*a-keru*) |
| **Warm** | **暖かい / 温かい** | あたた・**かい** (*atata-kai*) | From the verb **暖まる** (*atata-maru*) |
| **Detailed** | **細かい** | こま・**かい** (*koma-kai*) | From the noun/stem **細やか** (*koma-yaka*) |
| **Soft** | **柔らかい** | やわ・**らかい** (*yawa-rakai*) | From the stem **和らぐ** (*yawa-ragu*) |
| **Big** | **大きい** | おお・**きい** (*ō-kii*) | Special fixed historical form |
| **Small** | **小さい** | ちい・**さい** (*chii-sai*) | Special fixed historical form |
| **Few / Scarce** | **少ない** | すく・**ない** (*suku-nai*) | Distinct from **少し** (*suko-shi*) |

## 3. Rules for na-Adjectives & Adverbs (な形容詞・副詞)

- **〜か / 〜やか / 〜らか Suffixes**:
  - **静か** (*shizu-ka* - quiet)
  - **豊か** (*yuta-ka* - abundant)
  - **穏やか** (*oda-yaka* - gentle/calm)
  - **鮮やか** (*aza-yaka* - vivid)
  - **明らか** (*aki-raka* - obvious)
- **High-Frequency Adverbs**:
  - **必ず** (*kanara-zu* - without fail)
  - **少し** (*suko-shi* - a little)
  - **再び** (*futata-bi* - once again)
  - **全く** (*matta-ku* - completely)
  - **最も** (*motto-mo* - most)

---

## 4. Compound Nouns & Nominalized Verb Stems (N3 Exam Focus)

When verbal stems compound into nouns, standard grammar rules suggest retaining okurigana. However, **official business and customary Japanese (*kan'yō*) permits dropping okurigana in established compound nouns**:

| Type | Formal Standard | Common Customary (*Kan'yō*) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reception Desk** | 受け付け (*uketsuke*) | **受付** (*uketsuke*) | Customary omission universally preferred on signs |
| **Revenue / Sales** | 売り上げ (*uriage*) | **売上** (*uriage*) | Universal in financial reports and accounting |
| **Level Crossing** | 踏み切り (*fumikiri*) | **踏切** (*fumikiri*) | Railway crossing |
| **Date** | 日付け (*hizuke*) | **日付** (*hizuke*) | Calendar date on forms |
| **TV Program** | 番組み (*bangumi*) | **番組** (*bangumi*) | Broadcast program |
| **Story / Tale** | 物語り (*monogatari*) | **物語** (*monogatari*) | Tale or narrative |
| **Case / Situation** | 場合い (*baai*) | **場合** (*baai*) | Case / circumstance |
| **Entrance** | 入り口 (*iri-guchi*) | **入口** (*iri-guchi*) | Both widely seen on public signage |
