# Japanese Age, Years, and Eras (年齢・年・元号)

Expressing age and years in Japanese involves specific counters and a dual calendar system. While the Gregorian Western calendar (西暦 / *seireki*) is standard in daily conversation and digital tools, **official government documents, resumes, driver's licenses, and formal bank applications in Japan frequently require the Japanese imperial era system (元号 / *gengō*)**.

---

## 1. Expressing Age (〜歳 / 〜才)

To state someone's age, append the counter **歳** (さい / *sai*) to the number. In informal handwritten Japanese or casual signage, the simplified character **才** is often used as an abbreviation.

### Core Age Table (1 to 100)

| Age | Kanji | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation Rules / Nuance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1 yr old** | 一歳 | いっさい | *issai* | **Phonetic contraction** (small っ, not *ichisai*) |
| **2 yrs old** | 二歳 | にさい | *nisai* | Regular |
| **3 yrs old** | 三歳 | さんさい | *sansai* | Regular |
| **4 yrs old** | 四歳 | よんさい | *yonsai* | Always *yon-*, never *shi-* |
| **5 yrs old** | 五歳 | ごさい | *gosai* | Regular |
| **6 yrs old** | 六歳 | ろくさい | *rokusai* | Regular |
| **7 yrs old** | 七歳 | ななさい / しちさい | *nanasai / shichisai* | *nanasai* is standard; *shichisai* is acceptable |
| **8 yrs old** | 八歳 | はっさい | *hassai* | **Phonetic contraction** (small っ, not *hachisai*) |
| **9 yrs old** | 九歳 | きゅうさい | *kyūsai* | Always *kyū-*, never *ku-* |
| **10 yrs old** | 十歳 | じゅっさい / じっさい | *jussai / jissai* | Both *jussai* (modern) and *jissai* (traditional) are correct |
| **11 yrs old** | 十一歳 | じゅういっさい | *jūissai* | Compounded: *jū* + *issai* |
| **18 yrs old** | 十八歳 | じゅうはっさい | *jūhassai* | Compounded: *jū* + *hassai* (legal adulthood in Japan since 2022) |
| **19 yrs old** | 十九歳 | じゅうきゅうさい | *jūkyūsai* | Regular compound |
| **20 yrs old** | **二十歳** | **はたち** / にじゅっさい | **hatachi** / *nijussai* | **Highly Irregular!** Traditional coming-of-age reading |
| **21 yrs old** | 二十一歳 | にじゅういっさい | *nijūissai* | Reverts to standard counting |
| **30 yrs old** | 三十歳 | さんじゅっさい | *sanjussai* | Regular tens contraction |
| **40 yrs old** | 四十歳 | よんじゅっさい | *yonjussai* | Regular tens contraction |
| **50 yrs old** | 五十歳 | ごじゅっさい | *gojussai* | Regular tens contraction |
| **60 yrs old** | 六十歳 | ろくじゅっさい | *rokujussai* | Also celebrated as **還暦** (*kanreki*) |
| **100 yrs old** | 百歳 | ひゃくさい | *hyakusai* | Century milestone |

> [!NOTE] 20 Years Old: はたち (*hatachi*) vs. にじゅっさい (*nijussai*)
> While *nijussai* is grammatically understood, **はたち (二十歳)** is by far the culturally preferred term. In Japan, age 20 marks the historical Coming-of-Age celebration (成人式 / *seijin-shiki*), even though the legal voting and adulthood age was revised to 18 in 2022.

### Asking Someone's Age
- **Casual / Friendly**: 何歳？ (*Nan-sai?*)
- **Standard Polite**: 何歳ですか。 (*Nan-sai desu ka?*)
- **Honorific / Formal (Addressing superiors or customers)**: おいくつですか。 (*O-ikutsu desu ka?*) / ご年齢をお伺いできますか。 (*Go-nenrei o o-ukagai dekimasu ka?*)

## 2. Counting Years & Durations (〜年 / 〜年間)

The counter **年** (ねん / *nen*) is used both for calendar years and for durations of years.

| Usage Type | Kanji | Hiragana / Reading | English Meaning | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Specific Year** | 2024年 | にせんにじゅうよねん (*ni-sen ni-jū yo-nen*) | The year 2024 | **Four is always よねん (*yonen*)**, not *yon-nen* or *shi-nen* |
| **What Year?** | 何年 | なんねん (*nan-nen*) | What year? / Which year? | Question word |
| **Year Duration** | 3年 / 3年間 | さんねん / さんねんかん (*san-nen / san-nen-kan*) | For 3 years | 間 (*kan*) emphasizes duration |
| **Half a Year** | 半年 | はんとし (*hantoshi*) | Half a year / 6 months | Native reading *hantoshi* |
| **1 Year & a Half** | 一年半 | いちねんはん (*ichi-nen-han*) | A year and a half | Follows the main number |
| **School Grade Year** | 一年生 | いちねんせい (*ichi-nen-sei*) | 1st-year student / Freshman | Adds 生 (*sei*) |

## 3. The Japanese Imperial Era System (元号 / Gengō)

In Japan, imperial era names begin on the accession of each new Emperor (*Tennō*). Official certificates, legal contracts, birthdates, and tax filings frequently use the era name instead of the Western year.

| Era Name | Kanji | Reading | Gregorian Period | Western Year Conversion Formula | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meiji** | **明治** | めいじ | 1868 – 1912 | **Meiji Year + 1867** | 明治30年 = 1897 |
| **Taishō** | **大正** | たいしょう | 1912 – 1926 | **Taishō Year + 1911** | 大正12年 = 1923 (Kanto Earthquake) |
| **Shōwa** | **昭和** | しょうわ | 1926 – 1989 | **Shōwa Year + 1925** | 昭和60年 = 1985 |
| **Heisei** | **平成** | へいせい | 1989 – 2019 | **Heisei Year + 1988** | 平成7年 = 1995 |
| **Reiwa** | **令和** | れいわ | 2019 – Present | **Reiwa Year + 2018** | 令和6年 = 2024 |

> [!IMPORTANT] Year One of Any Era is 元年 (*Gannen*)
> The first year of an era is **never** written as 1年 (*ichi-nen*). It is always designated as **元年 (がんねん / *gannen*)**:
> - 2019 = **令和元年** (*Reiwa Gannen*) from May 1 onward (prior to May 1 was **平成31年**).
> - 1989 = **平成元年** (*Heisei Gannen*).
> - 1926 = **昭和元年** (*Shōwa Gannen*).

## 4. Relative Time Expressions for Years

| English | Kanji | Hiragana | Romaji | Usage & Register |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Two Years Ago** | 一昨年 | おととし / いっさくねん | *ototoshi* (casual/polite) / *issakunen* (formal) | Everyday vs. business |
| **Last Year** | 去年 / 昨年 | きょねん / さくねん | *kyonen* (everyday) / *sakunen* (formal/written) | Avoid 去年 in formal speeches (去 means "to leave/pass away") |
| **This Year** | 今年 / 本年 | ことし / ほんねん | *kotoshi* (general) / *honnen* (business/formal) | "This fiscal year" = 今年度 (*konnendo*) |
| **Next Year** | 来年 | らいねん | *rainen* | Standard |
| **Year After Next** | 再来年 | さらいねん | *sarainen* | Standard |
| **Every Year** | 毎年 | まいとし / まいねん | *maitoshi / mainen* | Both readings are valid; *maitoshi* is more common in speech |
| **Annual / Yearly** | 年中 | ねんじゅう | *nenjū* | Year-round / all year long |
