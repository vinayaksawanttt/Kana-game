# Potential vs. Passive vs. Causative

These verb forms can look or sound similar, but their sentence roles and meanings differ:

| Form | Core meaning | Question it answers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Potential | can; be able to | Who can do what? |
| Passive | be acted on; experience an event | What happened to whom? |
| Causative | make or let someone do something | Who causes or permits whom to act? |

## 1. How to form them

| Verb group | Potential | Passive | Causative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Group 1: 書く | 書ける | 書かれる | 書かせる |
| Group 1: 買う | 買える | 買われる | 買わせる |
| Group 2: 食べる | 食べられる | 食べられる | 食べさせる |
| する | できる | される | させる |
| 来る (くる) | 来られる (こられる) | 来られる (こられる) | 来させる (こさせる) |

**Group 1:** potential changes the final kana to its え-row equivalent + る; passive uses the あ-row + れる; causative uses the あ-row + せる. For a final う, the passive and causative use わ: 買われる, 買わせる.

**Group 2:** remove る and add られる (potential/passive) or させる (causative). In casual speech, Group 2 potential forms are often shortened by dropping ら (食べれる), but られる is the standard form for formal writing.

## 2. Potential: ability and possibility

Potential sentences describe what someone can do. The ability-holder is often marked by **が**; **を** is also heard, especially in conversation:

> 私は漢字が読めます。  
> I can read kanji.
>
> 今日は早く帰れます。  
> I can go home early today.
>
> ここで写真を撮れますか。  
> Can I take photos here?

For Group 1 verbs, the potential is a separate る-verb: 読む → 読める → 読めます.

## 3. Passive: receiving or experiencing an action

The affected person or thing is commonly marked by **が** or **は**. The actor is often marked by **に**:

> 私は先生にほめられました。  
> I was praised by the teacher.
>
> 弟は犬に手をかまれた。  
> My younger brother was bitten on the hand by a dog.

Japanese also uses passive forms for an unwanted or troublesome experience, sometimes called the adversity passive:

> 雨に降られて、服がぬれました。  
> I got caught in the rain, and my clothes got wet.

The passive is not always equivalent in tone to an English passive. In many contexts, a natural Japanese active sentence is clearer.

## 4. Causative: make or let someone do something

The causative describes someone making or allowing another person to act. Context determines which meaning is intended:

> 先生は学生に作文を書かせました。  
> The teacher made / let the students write an essay.
>
> 母は子どもを公園で遊ばせました。  
> The mother let / made the child play in the park.

The person directed to act can be marked by **に** or **を**, depending on the verb, whether it is transitive, and the sentence structure. With an already transitive verb, **に** is common: 学生に作文を書かせる. With an intransitive verb, **を** is common: 子どもを走らせる. Usage varies; follow the construction in natural examples.

### Causative requests and permissions

**～させてください** asks permission to do something: 今日は早く帰らせてください (Please let me go home early today).

## 5. Causative-passive: be made to do

The causative-passive describes someone being made or pressured to do something:

> 私は先生に作文を書かされました。  
> I was made to write an essay by the teacher.

Group 1 commonly forms a shortened version with **～される** for many verbs: 書く → 書かされる. For verbs ending in す, avoid confusing the causative and causative-passive; for example, 話す → 話させる (make/let speak) → 話させられる (be made to speak).

## 6. Forms that look the same

### 食べられる: potential or passive

- この魚は食べられます。 — This fish can be eaten. (potential)
- 私は弟にケーキを食べられました。 — My younger brother ate my cake (to my dismay). (adversity passive)

The surrounding sentence and context tell you which meaning is intended.

### 来られる: potential or passive

- 明日、来られますか。 — Can you come tomorrow? (potential)
- 友達に家に来られました。 — A friend came to my house, to my inconvenience. (adversity passive)

### A useful reading strategy

Identify the subject and particles, then ask what the sentence is expressing: ability, an event affecting someone, or control over another person's action. Do not decide based on the verb ending alone.
