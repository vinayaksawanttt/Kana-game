# Casual vs Polite Speech in Japanese (タメ口 vs 丁寧語)

In Japanese, language changes dynamically based on the relationship between speaker and listener. Choosing the right register is not just a grammatical choice—it signals distance, respect, intimacy, and social awareness.

The two main registers encountered in everyday Japanese are:
1. **丁寧語 (*Teineigo* / Polite Speech)**: Characterized by the **〜です** and **〜ます** endings. Used with strangers, acquaintances, teachers, older individuals, and in general public life.
2. **くだけた表現 / タメ口 (*Kudaketa Hyōgen* / *Tameguchi* / Casual Speech)**: Characterized by the dictionary/plain form (辞書形・普通形). Used with close friends, immediate family members, peers of equal standing, and younger individuals.

---

## 1. Register Comparison Overview

| Feature | Polite (*Teineigo*) | Casual (*Tameguchi*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Verb Ending** | 〜ます / 〜ました / 〜ません | Plain (辞書形 / た形 / ない形) |
| **Copula ("To be")** | です / でした / ではありません | だ / だった / じゃない |
| **Questions** | Verb + **か** (e.g., 行きますか) | Plain form + **rising intonation ↗** or **の？** |
| **Particles (を, は, が)** | Clearly articulated | Frequently dropped in spoken conversation |
| **Social Distance** | Respectful, reserved, safe | Close, warm, relaxed, or intimate |
| **Default User Group** | Adults in society, work, strangers | Friends, classmates, family, children |

## 2. Grammatical Transformations: Polite to Casual

### Verbs (動詞)

| Tense / Form | Polite (*Masu*) | Casual (*Plain*) | Example (Polite ➔ Casual) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-past Affirmative** | 〜ます | Dictionary form (辞書形) | 食べます ➔ **食べる** |
| **Non-past Negative** | 〜ません | 〜ない形 | 食べません ➔ **食べない** |
| **Past Affirmative** | 〜ました | 〜た形 | 食べました ➔ **食べた** |
| **Past Negative** | 〜ませんでした | 〜なかった形 | 食べませんでした ➔ **食べなかった** |
| **Volitional ("Let's...")** | 〜ましょう | 〜よう / 〜おう | 行きましょう ➔ **行こう** |
| **Requests ("Please...")** | 〜てください | 〜て / 〜てね | 待ってください ➔ **待って / 待ってね** |

### The Copula and Nouns / な-Adjectives

| Tense / Form | Polite | Casual | Example (Polite ➔ Casual) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Affirmative** | です | **だ** (or dropped) | 学生です ➔ **学生だ** (or simply **学生**) |
| **Present Negative** | ではありません / じゃありません | **じゃない** | 暇じゃありません ➔ **暇じゃない** |
| **Past Affirmative** | でした | **だった** | 静かでした ➔ **静かだった** |
| **Past Negative** | ではありませんでした | **じゃなかった** | 有名じゃありませんでした ➔ **有名じゃなかった** |

> [!WARNING]
> In casual speech, women and young speakers often drop **だ** entirely at the end of a sentence (*綺麗だよ* or *綺麗* instead of *綺麗だ*), as ending with a bare **だ** can sound blunt or masculine.

### い-Adjectives

| Tense / Form | Polite | Casual (simply drop です) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Affirmative** | おいしいです | **おいしい** |
| **Present Negative** | おいしくないです / おいしくありません | **おいしくない** |
| **Past Affirmative** | おいしかったです | **おいしかった** |
| **Past Negative** | おいしくなかったです | **おいしくなかった** |

## 3. Asking Questions Casually

In polite speech, questions always end with the question particle **か (*ka*)**. In casual speech, using **か** after a plain form (e.g., *食べるか？*) sounds rough, blunt, or masculine. Instead, Japanese speakers use:

### 1. Rising Intonation (↗)
Simply pronounce the plain form with a rising pitch at the end:
- 明日、来る？ (*Ashita, kuru?* ↗) — Coming tomorrow?
- これ、おいしい？ (*Kore, oishii?* ↗) — Is this delicious?
- 今、暇？ (*Ima, hima?* ↗) — Are you free now? (Notice **だ** is dropped!).

### 2. The Explanatory Particle の (↗)
Adding **の** softens the question and invites an explanation:
- どこに行くの？ (*Doko ni iku no?* ↗) — Where are you going?
- 何を食べているの？ (*Nani o tabete iru no?* ➔ contracted: *何食べてんの？*) — What are you eating?
- どうしたの？ (*Dō shita no?* ↗) — What happened? / What's wrong?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> When attaching **の** to a noun or な-adjective in a casual question, replace **だ** with **な**:
> - ❌ *本当だの？*
> - ⭕ **本当なの？** (*Hontō na no?* ↗ — Is that true?)
> - ⭕ **休みの日なの？** (*Yasumi no hi na no?* ↗ — Is it a day off?)

## 4. Particle Omission Rules in Casual Japanese

Spoken casual Japanese naturally drops unstressed structural particles when context makes the meaning obvious.

| Particle | Dropped in Casual? | Example: Full Polite | Example: Spoken Casual |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **を (*o*)** (Direct Object) | **Very Common** | 水**を**飲みます。 | 水飲む？ (*Mizu nomu?*) |
| **は (*wa*)** (Topic) | **Very Common** | これ**は**何ですか。 | これ何？ (*Kore nani?*) |
| **が (*ga*)** (Subject) | **Common** | 時間**が**ありません。 | 時間ない。 (*Jikan nai.*) |
| **へ (*e*)** (Direction) | **Common** | どこ**へ**行きますか。 | どこ行くの？ (*Doko iku no?*) |
| **に (*ni*)** (Time / Target) | **Selective** | 明日**に**会いましょう。 | 明日会おう。 (*Ashita aō.*) |
| **で (*de*)** (Location / By means) | **Rarely Dropped** | 電車**で**行く。 | 電車**で**行く。 (Preserved for clarity) |
| **と (*to*)** (With / And) | **Never Dropped** | 友だち**と**話す。 | 友だち**と**話す。 (Crucial meaning) |
| **から / まで** (From / Until) | **Never Dropped** | 3時**から**始まる。 | 3時**から**始まる。 (Preserved) |

## 5. Casual Sentence-Ending Tags and Particles

Casual conversation uses sentence enders to build camaraderie and emotional nuance:

```
・ ね (ne)        ──► "Right?" / "Isn't it?" (Seeking shared agreement)
・ よ (yo)        ──► "You know!" / "I tell you!" (Sharing new information)
・ よね (yone)    ──► "Right, as we both know?" (Gentle confirmation)
・ じゃん (jan)   ──► "Isn't it?!" (Contraction of じゃないか)
・ な / なあ (nā) ──► "Hmm..." / "I wonder..." (Internal monologue / wistful)
・ さ (sa)        ──► "Like / You know" (Conversational rhythm filler)
```

### Examples:
- これ、すごくいい**じゃん**！ (*Kore, sugoku ii jan!* — Hey, this is really great!)
- 明日は雨降る**かもね**。 (*Ashita wa ame furu kamo ne.* — It might rain tomorrow, huh.)
- それは違う**と思うよ**。 (*Sore wa chigau to omou yo.* — I think that's different, you know.)

## 6. Social Rules: When to Use Which Register?

```
               ┌───────────────────────────────┐
               │    Who are you speaking to?   │
               └──────────────┬────────────────┘
                              │
             ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
             ▼                                 ▼
    [Out-Group / Higher Status]       [In-Group / Equal or Junior]
     • Strangers                       • Close friends
     • Boss / Teachers                 • Family members
     • Clients / Customers             • Classmates of same age
     • Older acquaintances             • Children / Pets
             │                                 │
             ▼                                 ▼
      丁寧語 (Teineigo)                 タメ口 (Tameguchi)
      です / ます forms                 Plain / Casual forms
```

### Transitioning to Casual Speech (*Tameguchi*)
Speaking politely to an intimate friend creates unnatural emotional distance (*yosoyosashii*). Conversely, speaking casually to an elder or superior is rude (*tameguchi o kiku*). 

When acquaintances become friends, a senior or peer will often initiate the switch:
- **敬語はやめましょう。** (*Keigo wa yamemashō.* — Let's drop polite speech.)
- **タメ口でいいよ！** (*Tameguchi de ii yo!* — Casual speech is fine!)
- **敬語じゃなくていいからね。** (*Keigo janakute ii kara ne.* — You don't need to speak politely.)
