# JLPT Format, Scoring, and Timing Guide (JLPT試験構成・時間配分)

The **Japanese Language Proficiency Test (日本語能力試験 — *JLPT*)** is the globally standardized criterion-referenced examination evaluating Japanese language proficiency among non-native speakers. Administered worldwide by the Japan Educational Exchanges and Services (JEES) and the Japan Foundation, the test spans five competency levels from **N5 (introductory)** to **N1 (advanced academic and professional)**.

Success on the JLPT requires not only vocabulary and grammatical knowledge, but also precise time management, an understanding of section structures, and mastery of scaled scoring dynamics.

---

## 1. Test Architecture by Level (試験構成)

The exam divides its questions into three primary knowledge domains:
1. **言語知識 (文字・語彙・文法)** — Language Knowledge: Characters, Vocabulary & Grammar
2. **読解** — Reading Comprehension
3. **聴解** — Listening Comprehension

### Test Block Partitioning
Depending on the level, the test day is divided into either **two or three separate test blocks** separated by designated rest intervals:

```
N5 & N4: 3 Distinct Test Blocks
┌──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┐
│  Test Block 1:       │  Test Block 2:       │  Test Block 3:       │
│  Language Knowledge  │  Language Knowledge  │  Listening           │
│  (Vocabulary)        │  (Grammar) & Reading │  (聴解)              │
│  (文字・語彙)        │  (文法・読解)        │                      │
└──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┘

N3, N2 & N1: 2 Distinct Test Blocks
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────┐
│  Test Block 1:                              │  Test Block 2:       │
│  Language Knowledge (Vocab/Grammar)         │  Listening           │
│  & Reading Comprehension                    │  (聴解)              │
│  (言語知識［文字・語彙・文法］・読解)       │                      │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────┘
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> For **N3, N2, and N1**, Language Knowledge and Reading are combined into a single, unified test block. You are given a single countdown timer and test booklet covering both sections. Managing how many minutes you spend on vocabulary vs. long reading passages is entirely up to you.

## 3. Recommended Pacing Breakdown (時間配分の目安)

Running out of time during the Reading section is the most common reason examinees fail N2 and N1. Below is the ideal pacing model for each level.

### N1 Pacing Strategy (110 Minutes Total)
- **Vocabulary (文字・語彙)**: ~15–18 minutes (Kanji readings, context, paraphrases, word usage).
- **Grammar (文法)**: ~18–20 minutes (Sentences, star-sentence unscrambling, passage grammar).
- **Short & Medium Reading (短文・中文)**: ~30 minutes (Quick thematic extracts and main points).
- **Long Reading & Integrated (長文・総合理解・論説文)**: ~30 minutes (In-depth analysis and comparative texts).
- **Information Retrieval (情報検索)**: ~8 minutes (Catalog/flyer scanning).
- **Review & Buffer**: ~4 minutes.

### N2 Pacing Strategy (105 Minutes Total)
- **Vocabulary (文字・語彙)**: ~15 minutes.
- **Grammar (文法)**: ~15–18 minutes.
- **Short Reading (短文)**: ~15 minutes (5 passages × 3 min each).
- **Medium Reading (中文)**: ~25 minutes (3 passages × 8 min each).
- **Integrated & Long Reading (総合理解・長文)**: ~20 minutes.
- **Information Retrieval (情報検索)**: ~7 minutes.
- **Review & Buffer**: ~3 minutes.

### N3 Pacing Strategy (Block 2: 70 Minutes for Grammar & Reading)
- **Grammar Sentences & Stars (文法形式・並べ替え)**: ~15 minutes.
- **Passage Grammar (文章の文法)**: ~8 minutes.
- **Short Reading (短文 - 4 passages)**: ~15 minutes.
- **Medium Reading (中文 - 6 questions)**: ~20 minutes.
- **Information Retrieval (情報検索)**: ~8 minutes.
- **Buffer**: ~4 minutes.

### N4 & N5 Pacing Strategy
- **Vocabulary Block**: Pace through questions at ~45 seconds per question. Never get stuck on unknown kanji—make an educated guess and keep moving.
- **Grammar & Reading Block**: Dedicate the first 40% of the block to grammar questions, leaving the remaining 60% for short reading comprehension and signs/notices.

## 4. Scoring System: Scaled Scores (得点等化: Item Response Theory)

The JLPT does **not** calculate raw percentages or count points per question. Instead, it utilizes **Item Response Theory (IRT)** to generate "scaled scores" (*scaled scoring*).

```
Raw Answers Sheet ──► Statistical Difficulty Model ──► Scaled Score (0–180)
```

- Each test administration undergoes statistical calibration so that getting a scaled score of 100 on one exam reflects the exact same ability as 100 on an exam from five years prior.
- If a question proves abnormally easy for test-takers globally, its scaled weight is lower; if a question discriminates proficiency effectively, it carries standard scaled value.
- **Guessing penalty**: There is **no negative marking** on the JLPT. Never leave an answer oval blank!

## 5. Passing Thresholds and Sectional Minimums (合格基準点)

To pass any JLPT level, an examinee must satisfy **two independent conditions simultaneously**:
1. **Overall Pass Mark (総合得点の合格点)**: Attain the overall total score threshold out of 180.
2. **Sectional Benchmarks (各得点区分の基準点)**: Attain the minimum passing score in *every individual section*.

> [!WARNING]
> If you score 60/60 in Reading and 60/60 in Vocabulary, but score 18/60 in Listening (below the 19-point sectional cutoff), **you will fail the entire examination**, even though your total score is 138/180!

### Passing Score Matrix

| Level | Total Score | Minimum Overall Passing Score | Section 1 Minimum (Language Knowledge) | Section 2 Minimum (Reading) | Section 3 Minimum (Listening) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **N1** | 180 | **100 / 180** (55.5%) | **19 / 60** | **19 / 60** | **19 / 60** |
| **N2** | 180 | **90 / 180** (50.0%) | **19 / 60** | **19 / 60** | **19 / 60** |
| **N3** | 180 | **95 / 180** (52.7%) | **19 / 60** | **19 / 60** | **19 / 60** |
| **N4** | 180 | **90 / 180** (50.0%) | Combined Knowledge & Reading: **38 / 120** | ── | **19 / 60** |
| **N5** | 180 | **80 / 180** (44.4%) | Combined Knowledge & Reading: **38 / 120** | ── | **19 / 60** |

## 6. Exam Day Rules and Practical Advice

- **Pencils Only**: You must use a medium-soft lead pencil (**HB or No. 2**) and a clean eraser. Mechanical pencils are permitted in many test centers, but ink pens or gel pens will void the optical mark reader (OMR).
- **Watches**: Only standard analog or simple digital wristwatches without alarms, calculators, or smart features are permitted. Wall clocks in the exam room are not guaranteed.
- **No Writing During Instructions**: Never open your test booklet until the proctor announces *"Hajimete kudasai"* (始めてください). Doing so will trigger a yellow warning card (*chūi*) or immediate disqualification (*shikkaku*).
- **Listening Section Strictness**: In the listening block, you must bubble your answers on the answer sheet **as the recording plays**. There is zero extra time given at the end to transfer marks; the moment the audio ends, all pencils must be put down instantly.
