# JLPT Reading Strategies and Key Connectors (読解解法と接続語)

The Reading section (**読解 — *Dokkai***) evaluates your capacity to comprehend factual details, track logical arguments, resolve abstract references, and determine the author's underlying thesis (**筆者の主張 — *Hissha no Shuchō***). 

Regardless of whether you are reading an N5 notice or an N1 philosophical essay, successful reading depends on structural analysis rather than passive word-by-word translation.

---

## 1. Passage Types Across Levels (問題形式)

| Passage Type | Japanese Name | Typical Length | JLPT Levels | Core Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Short Passages** | 短文 (*Tambun*) | 150–350 chars | N5–N1 | Direct factual comprehension, single main point, or practical message. |
| **Medium Passages** | 中文 (*Chūbun*) | 450–600 chars | N3–N1 | Tracking causal development, contrastive viewpoints, and reasons. |
| **Long Passages** | 長文 (*Chōbun*) | 800–1,000 chars | N2–N1 | Complex essays (*Ronsetsubun*), philosophical arguments, human relations. |
| **Comparative Reading** | 総合理解 (*Sōgō Rikai*) | 2 related texts | N2–N1 | Comparing Author A and Author B on the same topic (agreements & disagreements). |
| **Opinion / Thesis** | 主張理解 (*Shuchō Rikai*) | ~1,000 chars | N1 | Uncovering the author's ultimate philosophical or intellectual thesis. |
| **Information Retrieval** | 情報検索 (*Jōhō Kensaku*) | Visual flyer / table | N5–N1 | Locating specific conditions, exceptions, prices, dates, and qualifications. |

## 2. Core Reading Methodology: The 4-Step Strategy

```
Step 1: Read Question Stem First (設問の先読み)
        ──► Identify what you are looking for (Reason? Main point? Underline meaning?)
        ──► ⚠️ Do NOT read all 4 options yet (avoids confusing distractor bias).

Step 2: Anchor the Underlined Portion (傍線部への着目)
        ──► If question asks "Why is [X]?", look immediately BEFORE and AFTER the line.
        ──► The direct justification almost always sits within 1–2 sentences of the underline.

Step 3: Track Discourse Connectors (接続語のマーク)
        ──► Circle every contrast (しかし), summary (つまり), and result (したがって).

Step 4: Locate the Author's Ending (文末の主張表現)
        ──► Scan the final paragraph for explicit opinion markers (〜べきだ, 〜のではないか).
```

## 3. The Power of Connectors: Discourse Markers (接続語)

Discourse connectors reveal the logical blueprint of any Japanese passage. Master these four families of keywords:

### A. The Pivot / Contrast Family (逆接: Gyakusetsu)
> [!IMPORTANT]
> The sentence appearing **immediately after** a contrast connector almost always holds the author's true stance!

| Connector | Romaji | Meaning | Tactical Importance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **しかし** | *shikashi* | However / But | Standard pivot. The text before it is often general consensus; the text after is the author's counter-argument. |
| **だが / けれども** | *daga / keredomo* | But / And yet | Common transitions in essay writing. |
| **ところが** | *tokoroga* | However / Unexpectedly | Introduces an unexpected reality that shatters a prior premise. |
| **むしろ** | *mushiro* | Rather / On the contrary | Signals where the author's true priority lies. |
| **にもかかわらず** | *ni mo kakawarazu* | Despite / In spite of | Highlights a counter-intuitive phenomenon. |
| **それに対して** | *sore ni taishite* | In contrast to that | Compares two opposing subjects or phenomena. |

### B. The Rephrasing / Summary Family (換言・要約: Kangen / Yōyaku)
> [!TIP]
> When you see a summary connector, **the author is summarizing their complex point in clear, simple words**. This is often the exact answer to the comprehension question!

| Connector | Romaji | Meaning | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **つまり** | *tsumari* | In other words / In short | Distills preceding paragraphs into a core takeaway. |
| **要するに** | *yōsuru ni* | In summary / Essentially | Strips away examples to state the fundamental point. |
| **すなわち** | *sunawachi* | Namely / That is to say | Formal, academic restatement. |
| **言い換えれば** | *iikaereba* | In other words / Put differently | Clarifies an abstract assertion with simpler phrasing. |

### C. Cause and Consequence (因果関係: Inga Kankei)

| Connector | Romaji | Meaning | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **したがって** | *shitagatte* | Therefore / Accordingly | Formal, logical deduction leading directly to conclusion. |
| **その結果** | *sono kekka* | As a result | Highlights the tangible consequence of an event. |
| **なぜなら / というのも** | *nazenara / to iu no mo* | The reason is / Because | Immediately supplies the explanatory evidence for an assertion. |
| **それゆえに** | *sore yue ni* | For that reason / Hence | High-level N1 academic cause marker. |

### D. Addition and Progression (累加・展開: Ruika / Tenkai)

| Connector | Romaji | Meaning | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **さらに** | *sarani* | Furthermore / Moreover | Adds a secondary, reinforcing argument. |
| **しかも / その上** | *shikamo / sono ue* | What's more / On top of that | Intensifies the narrative. |
| **一方（で）** | *ippō (de)* | On the other hand / Meanwhile | Introduces a parallel perspective or simultaneous event. |

## 4. Pinpointing the Author's Thesis (筆者の主張)

Japanese authors frequently employ a stylistic structure known as **一般論の否定 (Refutation of General Consensus)**:

```
Step 1: Introduce general belief:   「一般に、〇〇と思われがちである。」(People tend to think X...)
Step 2: Pivot with contrast:        「しかし、本当にそうだろうか。」(However, is that really so?)
Step 3: Deliver the author's stance: 「実は、〇〇なのではないだろうか。」(In fact, isn't it really Y?)
```

### High-Probability Sentence Endings (文末表現)
Whenever you see these endings in the final paragraph, **underline them immediately**:
- **〜のではないだろうか / 〜のではないか** — *"Is it not the case that...?"* (The author's polite, assertive opinion).
- **〜べきである / 〜べきではない** — *"One should / should not..."* (Normative imperative).
- **〜にほかならない** — *"It is nothing other than..."* (Strong emphatic conclusion).
- **〜と考えられる / 〜と思われる** — *"It can be considered that..."* (Academic deduction).
- **〜に違いない** — *"There is no doubt that..."* (Firm conviction).

## 5. Resolving Demonstratives (指示語: Kore, Sore, Are)

When a question asks: **「これ / それ / その理由は何を指しているか」** (What does "this/that" refer to?):

1. **The Proximity Rule**: In 90% of cases, the referent is located in the **1 to 2 sentences immediately preceding** the demonstrative.
2. **Cohesion**:
   - **これ (*kore*) / この (*kono*)**: Refers to a concept just introduced by the writer, or something physically/psychologically close.
   - **それ (*sore*) / その (*sono*)**: Refers to a topic mentioned in the prior clause or sentence.
   - **あれ (*are*) / あの (*ano*)**: Refers to mutually shared knowledge between author and reader.
3. **Verification**: Substitute each candidate answer back into the sentence where the demonstrative appeared. If the sentence flows with grammatical and logical clarity, you have found the answer.

## 6. Information Retrieval Tactics (情報検索: Jōhō Kensaku)

The final reading question of every JLPT level presents a practical document (a seminar announcement, hotel brochure, delivery guideline, or library policy).

```
   [Do NOT read the whole brochure line-by-line!]
                        │
                        ▼
   Step 1: Read the specific condition prompt carefully:
           • Who is applying? (Student, employee, senior?)
           • What date/time? (Weekend vs. weekday?)
           • What are their constraints? (Budget, cancellation, group size?)
                        │
                        ▼
   Step 2: Scan the document headings to find the relevant section.
                        │
                        ▼
   Step 3: Read the FINE PRINT and ASTERISKS:
           • ※ ただし... (Exception: However...)
           • 割引対象外 (Excluded from discounts)
           • 事前申し込みが必要 (Advance registration required)
```

> [!WARNING]
> The correct answer in Information Retrieval almost always pivots on an **exception clause** (e.g., notes preceded by `※`, `（注）`, or `ただし`). Always check the bottom footnotes before picking an option!
