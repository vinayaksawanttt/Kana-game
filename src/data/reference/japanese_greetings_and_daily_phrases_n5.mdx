# Japanese Greetings and Daily Phrases (挨拶・日常会話)

In Japan, daily greetings and set expressions—collectively called **挨拶 (*aisatsu*)**—are far more than mere social niceties; they establish mutual trust, acknowledge social roles, and maintain group harmony (*wa*). Many expressions do not have 1:1 English translations because they reflect specific cultural actions (such as leaving home, finishing work, or eating a meal).

---

## 1. Time-of-Day Greetings (時間の挨拶)

Japanese time-based greetings shift according to the hour and the level of formality.

| Japanese | Furigana / Romaji | English Meaning | Register / Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **おはようございます** | おはようございます (*ohayō gozaimasu*) | Good morning | Polite / Standard (used until ~10:00–11:00 AM) |
| **おはよう** | おはよう (*ohayō*) | Morning! | Casual (family, friends, juniors) |
| **こんにちは** | こんにちは (*konnichiwa*) | Hello / Good afternoon | Polite / Neutral (mid-morning until dusk) |
| **こんばんは** | こんばんは (*konbanwa*) | Good evening | Polite / Neutral (after dark) |
| **おやすみなさい** | おやすみなさい (*oyasuminasai*) | Good night | Polite (before bed or leaving at night) |
| **おやすみ** | おやすみ (*oyasumi*) | Night night / Sleep well | Casual (friends, family, children) |

> [!NOTE]
> The morning greeting **おはようございます** is also traditionally used in retail, restaurant, entertainment, and shift-work environments upon meeting a coworker for the first time that day, even if it is 3:00 PM or 9:00 PM!

> [!WARNING]
> Remember the spelling rule: **こんにちは** and **こんばんは** end with the hiragana **は (*ha*)**, pronounced as **わ (*wa*)**, because they originated as topic phrases (*今日・今晩は...*).

## 2. Partings and Farewells (別れの言葉)

Saying goodbye depends heavily on whether you expect to see the person again soon, their status relative to you, and whether someone is leaving work.

| Japanese | Furigana / Romaji | Meaning | Usage Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **さようなら** | さようなら (*sayōnara*) | Goodbye / Farewell | Heavy connotation of finality or long absence; spoken by school children to teachers. Rarely used between close coworkers. |
| **じゃあね / またね** | じゃあね / またね (*jā ne / mata ne*) | See you! | Casual between friends and peers. |
| **また明日 / また来週** | またあした / またらいしゅう (*mata ashita / mata raishū*) | See you tomorrow / See you next week | Casual to semi-polite. |
| **失礼します** | しつれいします (*shitsurei shimasu*) | Excuse me / Pardon me | Formal departure; used when hanging up the phone, leaving a superior's room, or exiting the office. |
| **失礼しました** | しつれいしました (*shitsurei shimashita*) | Excuse me (past) | Spoken right after an encounter concludes. |
| **お先に失礼します** | おさきにしつれいします (*osaki ni shitsurei shimasu*) | Pardon me for leaving before you | Spoken when leaving work while colleagues are still working. |
| **お疲れ様でした** | おつかれさまでした (*otsukaresama deshita*) | Thank you for your hard work | The standard reply when a colleague says *osaki ni shitsurei shimasu*. |

## 3. Leaving and Returning Home (出かける時・帰宅の挨拶)

Japanese homes and traditional workplaces use set pairs of greetings when someone leaves or returns.

```
Person Leaving:   行ってきます (itte kimasu)  ──► "I'm heading out (and will return)!"
Person Staying:   行ってらっしゃい (itte rasshai) ◄── "Take care / Have a good day!"

Person Returning: ただいま (tadaima)          ──► "I'm back!"
Person at Home:   お帰りなさい (okaerinasai)   ◄── "Welcome home!"
```

| Phrase | Romaji | Meaning | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **行ってきます** | *itte kimasu* | I'm off / See you later | Literally: "I will go and come back." |
| **行ってらっしゃい** | *itterasshai* | Take care / Go and come back | Often shortened to *itterasshai* (polite) or *itte kina* (casual). |
| **ただいま（戻りました）** | *tadaima (modorimashita)* | I'm back / I have returned | Short for *tadaima modorimashita* ("I have returned just now"). |
| **お帰りなさい** | *okaerinasai* | Welcome home | Casual: **お帰り (*okaeri*)**. |

## 4. Dining Expressions (食事の挨拶)

Expressing reverence for the life sacrificed, the farmer, the cook, and the host is an essential ritual at every meal.

### Before Eating
- **いただきます (*itadakimasu*)**
  - Kanji origin: 頂きます / 戴きます (humble verb "to receive from above").
  - Literally: *"I humbly receive."*
  - Gesture: Place palms together in front of the chest, bow slightly, and say the phrase before picking up chopsticks.

### After Eating
- **ごちそうさまでした (*gochisōsama deshita*)**
  - Kanji origin: ご馳走様でした (*chisō* originally meant "running around" to gather ingredients and prepare food for guests).
  - Meaning: *"Thank you for the wonderful feast / meal."*
  - Casual variant: **ごちそうさま (*gochisōsama*)**.

> [!TIP]
> When dining at a restaurant in Japan, it is customary to say **ごちそうさまでした** to the chef or cashier when exiting.

## 5. Gratitude and Apologies (感謝とお詫び)

In Japanese culture, gratitude (*kansha*) and apology (*owabi*) overlap significantly. Saying "I am sorry" often functions as "Thank you for troubling yourself on my behalf."

| Expression | Formality | Nuance & Usage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ありがとうございます** (*arigatō gozaimasu*) | Polite | Standard, versatile "Thank you very much." |
| **ありがとうございました** (*arigatō gozaimashita*) | Polite (Past) | Used when the completed favor or transaction is over (e.g., leaving a shop or ending a presentation). |
| **ありがとう** (*arigatō*) | Casual | "Thanks" — only for friends, family, and younger people. |
| **どうも** (*dōmo*) | Flexible | Versatile shortener: "Thanks", "Hello", or "Excuse me". Combine as *dōmo arigatō gozaimasu* for high politeness. |
| **すみません** (*sumimasen*) | Standard | 1) "Excuse me" (calling a waiter) <br/> 2) "I'm sorry" (minor inconvenience) <br/> 3) "Thank you for the trouble". |
| **ごめんなさい** (*gomen nasai*) | Familiar | Direct personal apology. Used with close peers and family; not suitable in business hierarchy. |
| **ごめん** (*gomen*) / **ごめんね** (*gomen ne*) | Casual | "Sorry!" between friends. |
| **申し訳ありません** (*mōshiwake arimasen*) | Formal | Deep business apology: *"There is no excuse for my actions."* |
| **どういたしまして** (*dō itashimashite*) | Standard | "You're welcome." (Avoid saying this to business superiors; use *tondemo gozaimasen* or *o-yaku ni tatete kōei desu* instead). |

## 6. First Meetings and Introductions (初対面の挨拶)

When meeting someone for the first time, stick strictly to this established formula:

```
Step 1:  初めまして (Hajimemashite) ────────── "Nice to meet you / How do you do?"
Step 2:  [Name] です / と申します ─────────── "I am [Name] / My name is [Name]."
Step 3:  よろしくお願いします ────────────── "Please treat me favorably."
Reply:   こちらこそ、よろしくお願いします ── "Likewise, please treat me favorably!"
```

| Japanese | Romaji | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **初めまして** | *hajimemashite* | How do you do? (From 始める, beginning a new relationship) |
| **田中と申します** | *Tanaka to mōshimasu* | My name is Tanaka (humble). |
| **よろしくお願いします** | *yoroshiku onegai shimasu* | Please treat me favorably / I look forward to working with you. |
| **どうぞよろしくお願いします** | *dōzo yoroshiku onegai shimasu* | More polite variant. |
| **よろしく** | *yoroshiku* | Casual variant for peers/friends. |
| **こちらこそ** | *kochira koso* | "It is I who should say so / Likewise!" |

## 7. Consideration, Health, and Encouragement (気遣い・励まし)

| Expression | Romaji | Meaning | Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **お元気ですか** | *o-genki desu ka* | How are you? / Are you well? | Used when you haven't seen someone for a while (not a daily "how are you"). |
| **お大事に** | *o-daiji ni* | Take care / Get well soon | Spoken to sick or injured individuals. |
| **気をつけて** | *ki o tsukete* | Take care / Be careful | Spoken to someone leaving on a trip or walking home in bad weather. |
| **頑張ってください** | *ganbatte kudasai* | Do your best! / Good luck! | Encouragement for a test, sport, or task. |
| **頑張って** | *ganbatte* | Hang in there! / Go for it! | Casual encouragement. |
| **無理をしないでね** | *muri o shinai de ne* | Don't push yourself too hard | Warm, caring encouragement. |
| **おめでとうございます** | *omedetō gozaimasu* | Congratulations! | Birthdays, New Year, weddings, graduations. |
