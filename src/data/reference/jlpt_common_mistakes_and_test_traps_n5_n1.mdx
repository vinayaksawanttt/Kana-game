# JLPT Common Mistakes and Test Traps by Level (レベル別頻出の落とし穴)

Test designers at the Japan Foundation deliberately craft multiple-choice options with common learner pitfalls in mind. Understanding these recurring traps transforms confusing questions into predictable patterns.

Below is an exhaustive breakdown of the classic linguistic and tactical traps from **N5 through N1**.

---

## 1. N5 Traps & Pitfalls (入門レベルの落とし穴)

### A. The "Looks Like an い-Adjective" Trap
Several common adjectives end with the sound **い (*-i*)**, but are grammatically **な-adjectives**:
- ❌ 部屋が*綺麗い*です。
- ⭕ 部屋が**綺麗です** (*kirei desu* — な-adjective).
- ❌ 有名い人
- ⭕ **有名な人** (*yūmei na hito* — な-adjective).
- Others: 嫌い (*kirai* — hate/dislike), 幸い (*saiwai* — fortunate).

### B. Conjugation of いい (*Good*)
The adjective **いい (*ii*)** conjugates from its classical stem **よい (*yoi*)**:
- ❌ *いくない* / *いかった*
- ⭕ **よくない** (*yoku nai* — not good)
- ⭕ **よかった** (*yokatta* — was good)

### C. Confusing Time and Date Counters
- 1st of the month: **ついたち (*tsuitachi*)** vs. 1 day duration: **いちにち (*ichinichi*)**.
- 4th day: **よっか (*yokka*)** vs. 8th day: **ようか (*yōka*)** vs. 14th day: **じゅうよっか (*jūyokka*)** vs. 20th day: **はつか (*hatsuka*)**.
- 20 years old: **はたち (*hatachi*)** (not *nijū-sai*).

## 2. N4 Traps & Pitfalls (初級後半の落とし穴)

### A. Giving and Receiving Verbs (授受動詞: Ageru, Kureru, Morau)
The most frequently tested grammar point on JLPT N4:

```
[Giver] は [Recipient] に あげる        ──► Giver gives to someone else (NOT to "me")
[Giver] は [私に] くれる              ──► Giver gives to ME (or my in-group family)
[Recipient/Me] は [Giver] に/から もらう ──► Recipient receives from Giver
```

> [!WARNING]
> You can **never** say: ❌ *田中さんは私に本をあげました。*
> Because someone gave to *me*, you MUST use **くれました**:
> ⭕ **田中さんは私に本をくれました。** (*Tanaka-san wa watashi ni hon o kuremashita.*)

### B. Passive vs. Causative vs. Causative-Passive
- **Passive (受身: 〜られる)**: The subject is affected by someone else's action.
  - 私は先生に**褒められました**。 (*I was praised by the teacher.*)
- **Causative (使役: 〜させる)**: The subject makes or lets someone do something.
  - 母は私に野菜を**食べさせました**。 (*Mother made me eat vegetables.*)
- **Causative-Passive (使役受身: 〜させられる)**: The subject is **forced** against their will to do an action.
  - 私は無理やりお酒を**飲まされました**（飲ませられました）。 (*I was forced to drink alcohol.*)

### C. Transitive vs. Intransitive Verb Pairs (他動詞と自動詞)
- **他動詞 (Transitive)**: Requires human agency + particle **を**:
  - ドア**を**開ける (*doa o akeru* — I open the door).
- **自動詞 (Intransitive)**: State/natural occurrence + particle **が**:
  - ドア**が**開く (*doa ga aku* — The door opens).

## 3. N3 Traps & Pitfalls (中級の落とし穴)

### A. The "Modal Nuance" Trap: わけだ vs はずだ vs ことだ

| Grammar Point | Core Function | Distinctive Trigger |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **〜わけだ (*wake da*)** | Logical / Natural consequence | *"Naturally, it follows that..."* (explaining why a fact occurred). |
| **〜はずだ (*hazu da*)** | Objective rational expectation | *"According to expectations, it should be..."* (can be violated by reality). |
| **〜ことだ (*koto da*)** | Direct practical advice / warning | *"The best thing to do is..."* (e.g., *早く寝ることだ*). |
| **〜わけがない (*wake ga nai*)** | Logical impossibility | *"There is absolutely no logical way that..."* |

### B. Conjecture & Hearsay: そうだ vs ようだ vs らしい

| Form | How it functions | Clue |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Verb Stem + そうだ** | Visual appearance / Imminent event | 雨が**降りそうだ**。(It looks like it will rain soon!) |
| **Plain Form + そうだ** | Hearsay (*"I heard that..."*) | 明日は雨が**降るそうだ**。(I heard it will rain tomorrow.) |
| **〜ようだ / 〜みたいだ** | Sensory conjecture / Metaphor | 誰もいない**ようだ**。(It seems like nobody is here.) |
| **〜らしい** | Characteristic essence / Reliable rumor | 男**らしい** (manly / acts like a true man); 来年値上げする**らしい** (reliable rumor). |

## 4. N2 Traps & Pitfalls (中上級の落とし穴)

### A. The "Quoted Opinion" Reading Trap (引用と筆者の見解)
In N2 reading passages, authors frequently spend an entire paragraph describing a theory they disagree with:
> *「ある学者は、SNSが人間関係を希薄にすると主張している。...」*
> *(A certain scholar argues that social media dilutes human relationships...)*

- **The Trap**: The question asks, *"What does the author think about SNS?"*
- **The Distractor**: Option 1 says: *"It dilutes human relationships."* (It matches the text word-for-word, but it is the **scholar's** opinion, not the author's!).
- **The Reality**: In paragraph 2, the author writes: *「しかし、私はそうは思わない。」* (However, I do not think so).

### B. Double Negatives & Compulsory Action
N2 questions love multi-layered negative auxiliary forms:
- **〜ざるを得ない (*zaru o enai*)**: *"Cannot help but do..."* (forced by unavoidable circumstances: *退職せざるを得ない*).
- **〜ずにはいられない (*zu ni wa irarenai*)**: *"Cannot help but do out of overwhelming emotion"* (internal urge: *笑わずにはいられない*).
- **〜ないわけにはいかない (*nai wake ni wa ikanai*)**: *"Cannot avoid doing due to social obligation"* (e.g., attending a boss's funeral).

## 5. N1 Traps & Pitfalls (上級の落とし穴)

### A. Archaic Literary Grammar Remnants (文語的表現)
N1 tests grammar derived from Classical Japanese (古文):
- **〜たるもの (*taru mono*)**: *"In the capacity of / as a..."* (demands high moral duty: *医師たるもの、患者の命を最優先にすべきだ*).
- **〜であれ (*de are*)**: *"Even if / whether it be..."* (replaces *であっても*).
- **〜んばかりに (*n bakari ni*)**: *"As if on the verge of..."* (replaces *〜しそうなほど*).
- **〜ずにはおかない (*zu ni wa okanai*)**: *"Will inevitably cause / will definitely do..."* (dramatic guarantee: *見る者を感動させずにはおかない*).

### B. Immediate Succession Nuance Traps (「〜するとすぐ」の微妙な違い)

| Form | Constraint / Nuance |
| :--- | :--- |
| **〜や否や (*ya ina ya*)** | Factual immediate sequence; **cannot end with a request, order, or volitional action**. |
| **〜が早いか (*ga hayai ka*)** | The subject performs the second action almost simultaneously. |
| **〜なり (*nari*)** | The **same subject** performs an unexpected action immediately following the first. |
| **〜そばから (*soba kara*)** | Repeated cycle: as soon as you do X, it is undone (e.g., cleaning up toys, but kids immediately mess them up again). |

## 6. Universal Exam Day Traps (全レベル共通の罠)

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Trap 1: The "Exact Word Match" Trap (同語トラップ)    │
│  • A multiple-choice option uses the EXACT identical   │
│    kanji and words from the passage.                   │
│  • Beware! Often a distractor. The CORRECT answer      │
│    is almost always a PARAPHRASE (言い換え).            │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Trap 2: The "Personal Common Sense" Trap (常識の罠)   │
│  • Selecting an answer because it is "obviously true   │
│    in real life," even though the passage never said it│
│  • Base your answer STRICTLY on what is written.       │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Trap 3: Optical Mark Sheet (OMR) Row Displacement    │
│  • You skip question #14, but bubble your answer for   │
│    question #15 into row #14 on the answer sheet.      │
│  • Always check question numbers every 5 questions!    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
