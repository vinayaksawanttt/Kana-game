# Mastering Keigo: Honorific, Humble, and Polite Forms (敬語体系)

**敬語 (*Keigo*)** is the systematic honorific language of Japanese. It is not merely an optional layer of extra politeness; in business, corporate society, professional communications, and formal ceremonies, using appropriate keigo is mandatory for functioning effectively.

In 2007, the Japanese Agency for Cultural Affairs (文化庁) codified keigo into **5 distinct categories**, superseding the older three-category model.

---

## 1. The 5 Modern Categories of Keigo

```
                          ┌─────────────────────────────┐
                          │    Japanese Keigo System    │
                          └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                                         │
     ┌───────────────────┬───────────────┴───────────────┬───────────────────┐
     ▼                   ▼                               ▼                   ▼
1. 尊敬語            2. 謙譲語 I                     3. 謙譲語 II (丁重語)   4. 丁寧語 & 5. 美化語
(Sonkeigo)         (Kenjōgo I)                     (Teichōgo)          (Teineigo & Bikago)
Elevates the       Lowers speaker in relation      Courteous speech    Polite sentence endings
listener's action  to an elevated recipient       to listener         & beautified nouns
```

| Category | Japanese Name | Core Function | Action Performed By | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Respectful Language** | **尊敬語** (*Sonkeigo*) | Elevates the status of the person performing the action (clients, superiors, teachers). | The other person (相手 / 目上) | 社長が**お帰りになります**。 |
| **2. Humble Language I** | **謙譲語 I** (*Kenjōgo I*) | Lowers the speaker's action directed toward an elevated recipient. | Speaker / In-group (自分 / ウチ) | 私が資料を**お持ちします**。 |
| **3. Courteous Language II** | **謙譲語 II (丁重語)** (*Teichōgo*) | Speaks courteously about the speaker's own action, honoring the listener generally without a direct recipient. | Speaker / In-group (自分 / ウチ) | 明日、東京へ**参ります**。 |
| **4. Polite Language** | **丁寧語** (*Teineigo*) | Standard polite speech directed at the listener; ends in です / ます. | Anyone | 今日は暑い**ですね**。 |
| **5. Beautified Language** | **美化語** (*Bikago*) | Adds aesthetic refinement and elegance to nouns using **お** or **ご**. | Objects / Concepts | **お**茶、**ご**飯、**お**金 |

## 3. Regular Keigo Derivation Formulas

When a verb does not have a dedicated irregular keigo equivalent, use the systematic derivation formulas:

### A. Regular Sonkeigo (尊敬語: Respectful Forms)

#### 1. The Standard Pattern: お / ご + Verb Stem + になる
- 読む ➔ **お読みになります** (*o-yomi ni narimasu*)
- 待つ ➔ **お待ちになります** (*o-machi ni narimasu*)
- 連絡する ➔ **ご連絡になります** (*go-renraku ni narimasu*)

#### 2. The Respectful Passive Form: Verb + れる / られる
- 先生は昨日、本を**読まれました**。 (*Sensei wa kinō, hon o yomaremashita.*)
- 部長は何時に**来られますか**？ (*Buchō wa nanji ni koraremasu ka?*)

> [!NOTE]
> The passive form is slightly less formal than *お〜になる*, but safer and very common in semi-formal business settings.

#### 3. Respectful Requests: お / ご + Verb Stem + ください
- **お座りください** (*o-suwari kudasai* — Please have a seat.)
- **ご確認ください** (*go-kakunin kudasai* — Please confirm/check.)

---

### B. Regular Kenjōgo I (謙譲語 I: Humble Forms)

#### 1. The Standard Pattern: お / ご + Verb Stem + する / いたす
- 運ぶ ➔ 私が**お運びします** (*watashi ga o-hakobi shimasu* — I will carry it.)
- 届ける ➔ 後ほど**お届けいたします** (*nochihodo o-todoke itashimasu* — I will deliver it later.)
- 案内する ➔ 会場を**ご案内します** (*kaijō o go-annai shimasu* — I will guide you around the venue.)

#### 2. The Elevated Humble Pattern: お / ご + Verb Stem + 申し上げる
- **お詫び申し上げます** (*o-wabi mōshiagemasu* — I offer my sincere apologies.)
- **心よりお礼申し上げます** (*kokoro yori o-rei mōshiagemasu* — I express my heartfelt gratitude.)

## 4. In-Group vs. Out-Group (内と外: Uchi vs. Soto)

The golden rule of business keigo is that **the speaker's entire company is the In-Group (*Uchi*)**, and **the client or external party is the Out-Group (*Soto*)**.

Even if someone is your superior (e.g., your company president, 社長), **you must lower them when speaking to an outside client**:

```
Scenario: Client calls asking for President Tanaka.

❌ INCORRECT (Honoring your own boss to a client):
「田中社長はいらっしゃいません。」 (Using Sonkeigo for your boss to an outsider!)

⭕ CORRECT (Uchi-Soto Rule):
「田中はあいにく席を外しております。」
(Drop the title 社長, use bare family name Tanaka, and use humble/neutral polite verb!)
```

| Situation | Who is being spoken to? | Reference to President Tanaka |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Internal Office** | Talking to another coworker inside the company | 田中**社長**はいらっしゃいますか？ |
| **External Client** | Talking to a customer or business partner | 社長の**田中**は、ただいま外出しております。 |

## 5. Beautified Language (美化語: Bikago)

Bikago attaches the prefixes **お (*o-*)** or **ご (*go-*)** to make the language elegant and refined.

- **お (*o-*)**: Prefixed to native Japanese words (和語 - *wago*):
  - お茶 (*o-cha* — tea), お水 (*o-mizu* — water), お金 (*o-kane* — money), お箸 (*o-hashi* — chopsticks), お手洗い (*o-tearai* — restroom).
- **ご (*go-*)**: Prefixed to Sino-Japanese words (漢語 - *kango*):
  - ご飯 (*go-han* — rice/meal), ご住所 (*go-jūsho* — address), ご意見 (*go-iken* — opinion), ご家族 (*go-kazoku* — family).

## 6. Common Keigo Pitfalls & Errors

### 1. Double Honorifics (二重敬語 - *Nijū Keigo*)
Using two honorific transformations on the same verb is grammatically excessive and incorrect in standard Japanese:
- ❌ **おっしゃられました** (おっしゃる [Sonkeigo] + られる [Sonkeigo]) ➔ ⭕ **おっしゃいました**
- ❌ **お召し上がりになられました** ➔ ⭕ **召し上がりました**
- ❌ **ご覧になられました** ➔ ⭕ **ご覧になりました**

### 2. Confusing Kenjōgo for Sonkeigo
Never use a humble verb to describe a client's or teacher's action:
- ❌ 社長が**申しました**。 (Humble verb used for the boss!) ➔ ⭕ 社長が**おっしゃいました**。
- ❌ 先生が**参りました**。 (Humble verb used for the teacher!) ➔ ⭕ 先生が**いらっしゃいました**。

### 3. Customer Service "Baito Keigo" (バイト敬語)
Common ungrammatical phrases adopted in retail and restaurants that should be avoided in professional Japanese:
- ❌ 1000円**から**お預かりします。 ➔ ⭕ 1000円**をお預かりいたします**。
- ❌ こちらがメニュー**になります**。 ➔ ⭕ こちらがメニュー**でございます**。
- ❌ ご注文は以上で**よろしかったでしょうか**？ ➔ ⭕ ご注文は以上で**よろしいでしょうか**？
