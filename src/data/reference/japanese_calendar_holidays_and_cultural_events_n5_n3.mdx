# Japanese Calendar, National Holidays, and Cultural Events (日本の暦・祝日・年中行事)

In Japan, daily life, business operations, and school schedules harmonize around an intricate calendar blending modern Western solar dates, national public holidays (**国民の祝日 — *Kokumin no Shukujitsu***), the imperial era system (**元号 — *Gengō***), and ancient seasonal festivals (**年中行事 — *Nenjū Gyōji***).

---

## 1. The Japanese Imperial Era System (元号: Gengō)

In official government documents, driver's licenses, bank forms, and tax records, years are designated by the reigning Emperor's era name rather than the Western Common Era (西暦 — *seireki*).

```
Recent Japanese Eras:
• 令和 (Reiwa):  May 1, 2019 – Present    (Emperor Naruhito)
• 平成 (Heisei): Jan 8, 1989 – Apr 30, 2019 (Emperor Akihito)
• 昭和 (Shōwa):  Dec 25, 1926 – Jan 7, 1989 (Emperor Hirohito)
```

### Quick Era Conversion Formulas
- **Reiwa (令和)**: Reiwa Year = Western Year - 2018
  - Example: 2026 - 2018 = 令和8年 (Reiwa 8).
- **First Year Rule**: The first year of any era is called **元年 (*Gannen*)**, never "Year 1":
  - 2019 = **令和元年** (*Reiwa Gannen*).

## 2. Japan's 16 National Public Holidays (国民の祝日)

Japan celebrates **16 official national holidays** annually. Under the **振替休日 (*Furikae Kyūjitsu* / Substitute Holiday)** law, if a national holiday falls on a Sunday, the following Monday becomes a paid day off.

| Date | Japanese Holiday | Furigana / Romaji | Significance & Cultural Customs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Jan 1** | **元日** | がんじつ (*Ganjitsu*) | New Year's Day. Visiting shrines (*Hatsumōde*), eating *Osechi* cuisine. |
| **2nd Mon of Jan** | **成人の日** | せいじんのひ (*Seijin no Hi*) | Coming of Age Day. Honoring youth turning 18/20; women wear elaborate *Furisode* kimonos. |
| **Feb 11** | **建国記念の日** | けんこくきねんのひ (*Kenkoku Kinen no Hi*) | National Foundation Day. Commemorating the accession of mythical first Emperor Jimmu. |
| **Feb 23** | **天皇誕生日** | てんのうたんじょうび (*Tennō Tanjōbi*) | The Emperor's Birthday (Emperor Naruhito, born 1960). Imperial palace public greetings. |
| **Mar 20/21** | **春分の日** | しゅんぶんのひ (*Shunbun no Hi*) | Vernal Equinox Day. Nature reverence, visiting family graves during *Higan* (彼岸). |
| **Apr 29** | **昭和の日** | しょうわのひ (*Shōwa no Hi*) | Showa Day. Reflecting on the tumultuous Showa Era. First day of **Golden Week**. |
| **May 3** | **憲法記念日** | けんぽうきねんび (*Kempō Kinenbi*) | Constitution Memorial Day. Commemorating the 1947 post-war democratic constitution. |
| **May 4** | **みどりの日** | みどりのひ (*Midori no Hi*) | Greenery Day. Appreciating nature and the environment. |
| **May 5** | **こどもの日** | こどものひ (*Kodomo no Hi*) | Children's Day. Flying carp streamers (*Koinobori*), eating *Kashiwa-mochi*. |
| **3rd Mon of Jul** | **海の日** | うみのひ (*Umi no Hi*) | Marine Day. Giving thanks to the ocean and maritime prosperity. |
| **Aug 11** | **山の日** | やまのひ (*Yama no Hi*) | Mountain Day. Appreciating mountains and hiking. Precursor to *O-bon*. |
| **3rd Mon of Sep** | **敬老の日** | けいろうのひ (*Keirō no Hi*) | Respect for the Aged Day. Honoring elderly citizens and longevity. |
| **Sep 22/23** | **秋分の日** | しゅうぶんのひ (*Shūbun no Hi*) | Autumnal Equinox Day. Honoring ancestors and departed family souls. |
| **2nd Mon of Oct** | **スポーツの日** | スポーツのひ (*Supōtsu no Hi*) | Sports Day. Commemorating the 1964 Tokyo Olympics; school sports days (*Undōkai*). |
| **Nov 3** | **文化の日** | ぶんかのひ (*Bunka no Hi*) | Culture Day. Promoting peace, freedom, arts, and Order of Culture awards. |
| **Nov 23** | **勤労感謝の日** | きんろうかんしゃのひ (*Kinrō Kansha no Hi*) | Labor Thanksgiving Day. Honoring workers, production, and gratitude for labor. |

> [!NOTE]
> The **Happy Monday System (ハッピーマンデー制度)** deliberately relocated four public holidays (Coming of Age Day, Marine Day, Respect for the Aged Day, and Sports Day) to fixed Mondays to guarantee regular three-day holiday weekends throughout the year.

## 3. Major Holiday Clusters (大型連休)

### 1. お正月 (*O-shōgatsu* — New Year Period: Dec 29 – Jan 3)
The most sacred family holiday in Japan. Most businesses, clinics, and government offices shut down:
- **初詣 (*Hatsumōde*)**: The year's first shrine or temple visit to pray for health and fortune.
- **おせち料理 (*Osechi Ryōri*)**: Multi-tiered lacquered boxes filled with symbolic preserved foods.
- **お年玉 (*Otoshidama*)**: Monetary gifts in small decorative envelopes given to children.

### 2. ゴールデンウィーク (*Gōruden Wīku* — Golden Week: Apr 29 – May 5)
A dense cluster of four national holidays within seven days. Millions of Japanese travel domestically and abroad; bullet trains (*Shinkansen*) and flights operate at maximum capacity.

### 3. お盆 (*O-bon* — Ancestor Spirit Homecoming: Mid-August)
While not an official statutory public holiday, most Japanese companies grant 4–6 days of consecutive summer leave around **August 13–16**:
- **迎え火 (*Mukaebi*) & 送り火 (*Okuribi*)**: Welcome fires lit to guide ancestral spirits home, and send-off fires (e.g., Kyoto's famous *Gozan no Okuribi* / Daimonji) to guide them back.
- **盆踊り (*Bon Odori*)**: Traditional outdoor community circle dancing around wooden towers.

## 4. Traditional Seasonal & Cultural Events (年中行事)

```
🌸 Spring:  節分 (Feb 3) ➔ 雛祭り (Mar 3) ➔ お花見 (Late Mar/Apr)
☀️ Summer:  七夕 (Jul 7) ➔ 花火大会 (Jul/Aug) ➔ 夏祭り (Matsuri)
🍁 Autumn:  お月見 (Sep) ➔ 七五三 (Nov 15)
❄️ Winter:  クリスマス (Dec 25) ➔ 大晦日 (Dec 31) ➔ 初日の出 (Jan 1)
```

| Event | Date | Core Customs & Food |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **節分** (*Setsubun*) | Feb 3 | Tossing roasted soybeans to expel evil spirits: *「鬼は外、福は内！」* (Demons out, fortune in!). Eating an unbroken **恵方巻 (*Ehōmaki*)** sushi roll facing the year's auspicious direction in complete silence. |
| **雛祭り** (*Hinamatsuri*) | Mar 3 | Girls' Doll Festival. Displaying ornamental imperial court dolls (*Hina-ningyō*); eating *Chirashizushi* and *Hina-arare* sweet rice crackers to pray for young girls' health. |
| **お花見** (*O-hanami*) | Late Mar – Early Apr | Cherry blossom viewing picnics beneath blooming *sakura* trees with coworkers and friends. |
| **七夕** (*Tanabata*) | July 7 | Star Festival celebrating the annual reunion of deities Orihime and Hikoboshi. Writing personal wishes on colorful paper strips (**短冊 — *tanzaku***) tied to bamboo branches. |
| **花火大会** (*Hanabi Taikai*) | July – August | Spectacular summer riverbank fireworks displays; attendees wear casual cotton **浴衣 (*Yukata*)** and eat street food at festival stalls (*Yatai*). |
| **お月見** (*O-tsukimi*) | September / October | Harvest moon viewing. Offering round white rice dumplings (**月見団子 — *tsukimi dango***) and silver pampas grass (*Susuki*) to give thanks for autumn agricultural yields. |
| **七五三** (*Shichi-Go-San*) | November 15 | Rite-of-passage festival celebrating children aged 3, 5, and 7 at local Shinto shrines; children receive long red-and-white candies (**千歳飴 — *chitose-ame***) symbolizing longevity. |
| **大晦日** (*Ōmisoka*) | December 31 | New Year's Eve. Eating long buckwheat **年越しそば (*Toshikoshi Soba*)** symbolizing crossing over into the new year and longevity. Buddhist temples ring temple bells **108 times** (**除夜の鐘 — *Joya no Kane***) to cleanse worldly earthly desires. |
