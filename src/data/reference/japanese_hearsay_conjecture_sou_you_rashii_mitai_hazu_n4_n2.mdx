# Japanese Conjecture, Evidence & Hearsay

In Japanese, a speaker rarely presents unverified information as an absolute fact. Instead, the language requires selecting a specific grammatical ending that honestly reflects **the source and reliability of the evidence**—whether it is direct visual appearance, secondhand rumor, sensory intuition, or logical deduction.

---

| Pattern | Level | Source of Evidence | Core Meaning | Formula Key |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **〜そうだ (Appearance)** | N4 | Immediate visual observation | *Looks like / About to...* | Verb [ます-stem] / Adj stem + そうだ |
| **〜そうだ (Hearsay)** | N4 | Third-party report / Media | *I heard that... / Reportedly* | **Plain Form** + そうだ |
| **〜ようだ** | N3 | Direct sensory clues (sound, sight, smell) | *It appears that / Seems like* | Plain Form + ようだ (Noun + **の**) |

| **〜はずだ** | N3 | Logical deduction from known premises | *Should be / Ought to be* | Plain Form + はずだ (Noun + **の**) |

## 1. そうだ: The Two Completely Distinct Patterns

The single syllable **そうだ** has two entirely unrelated grammatical identities depending on what form precedes it:

```
Verb ます-stem / Adj stem  +  そうだ  ──►  APPEARANCE ("Looks like...")
Plain Form (Dictionary)    +  そうだ  ──►  HEARSAY ("I heard that...")
```

### A. Visual Appearance & Imminence (Stem + そうだ)
Based on what you observe right in front of your eyes:
- このケーキは**美味しそうだ**。  
  *Kono kēki wa oishisō da.*  
  (This cake **looks delicious**.)
- 雨が**降りそうだ**。  
  *Ame ga furisō da.*  
  (It **looks like it's about to rain**.)
- Special Irregular Negatives:
  - いい (*good*) → **よさそうだ** (*looks good*)
  - ない (*not*) → **なさそうだ** (*doesn't look like...*)

### B. Secondhand Hearsay & Reports (Plain Form + そうだ)
Transmits information learned from an external source (news, friends, books):
- 天気予報によると、明日は**雨が降るそうだ**。  
  *Tenki yohō ni yoru to, ashita wa ame ga furu sō da.*  
  (According to the weather forecast, **I heard that it will rain** tomorrow.)
- 田中さんは来月**結婚するそうだ**。  
  *Tanaka-san wa raigetsu kekkon suru sō da.*  
  (I heard that Tanaka is getting married next month.)

## 2. ようだ (*yō da*) vs. みたいだ (*mitai da*): Sensory Inference & Simile

Both patterns describe an inference based on the speaker's **five senses** (hearing a noise, seeing footprints, feeling a draft) or draw a **poetic comparison (simile)**:

### 1. Inferences from Sensory Clues
- 隣の部屋から物音がする。誰か**いるようだ** / **いるみたいだ**。  
  *Tonari no heya kara monooto ga suru. Dareka iru yō da / iru mitai da.*  
  (I hear a sound next door. It seems someone is in there.)
- 外は風が強くて**寒いようだ** / **寒いみたいだ**。  
  *Soto wa kaze ga tsuyokute samui yō da / samui mitai da.*  
  (The wind is strong outside; it seems cold out.)

### 2. Figurative Simile ("Just like...")
- 彼女はまるで天使**のようだ**。 (Formal / Written)  
  *Kanojo wa marude tenshi no yō da.*  
  (She is just like an angel.)
- 彼女はまるで天使**みたいだ**。 (Conversational)  
  *Kanojo wa marude tenshi mitai da.*

### Connection Difference: Noun Linking
- **ようだ**: Noun + **の** + ようだ (天使**の**ようだ)
- **みたいだ**: Noun attaches **directly** (天使みたいだ)

## 3. らしい (*rashii*): External Reports & Prototypicality

**らしい** operates in two specialized semantic dimensions:

### 1. Secondhand Rumor-Based Inference ("Apparently...")
The speaker draws a conclusion based on external chatter, rumors, or unconfirmed gossip:
- 噂によると、あの二人は**付き合っているらしい**。  
  *Uwasa ni yoru to, ano futari wa tsukiatte iru rashii.*  
  (According to rumors, those two are apparently dating.)
- 明日は雨**らしい**よ。  
  *Ashita wa ame rashii yo.*  
  (Apparently it's going to rain tomorrow.)

### 2. Prototypicality ("True to character / Befitting of...")
When appended directly to a noun, **らしい** means that an entity embodies all the ideal or typical qualities of that category:
- 彼は**男らしい**人だ。 (*He is a truly manly person.*)
- 今日はポカポカして、**春らしい**天気ですね。 (*Today is balmy and truly spring-like weather.*)
- 学生なら、もっと**学生らしく**勉強しなさい。 (*If you are a student, study properly like a student should!*)

---

## 4. はずだ (*hazu da*): Logical Deduction & Expectation

Unlike the other patterns, **はずだ** does not rely on visual guesswork or casual rumors; it reflects **ironclad logical deduction based on verified premises**:

```
[Known Premise] ──► Therefore, [Conclusion] はずだ ("It ought to / must logically be")
```

### Examples
- 彼は日本に10年間住んでいたから、日本語が**話せるはずです**。  
  *Kare wa Nihon ni jūnenkan sunde ita kara, Nihongo ga hanaseru hazu desu.*  
  (Because he lived in Japan for 10 years, he **ought to be able to speak** Japanese.)
- 鍵はさっきカバンに入れたから、ここにある**はずだ**。  
  *Kagi wa sakki kaban ni ireta kara, koko ni aru hazu da.*  
  (Since I put the key in my bag earlier, it **must logically be** right here.)

### Strong Negative Conviction: 〜はずがない ("There is no way that...")
- まだ練習していないのだから、上手にできる**はずがない**。  
  *Mada renbshū shite inai no dakara, jōzu ni dekiru hazu ga nai.*  
  (Since you haven't practiced yet, there is **no way you can do it well**!)

## 5. Master Scenario Comparison: "Tanaka is Sick" (田中さんは病気だ)

Observe how changing the ending transforms the epistemological source:

| Japanese Sentence | Meaning & Psychological Source of Evidence |
| :--- | :--- |
| 田中さんは**病気になりそうだ**。 | Direct Observation: He looks feverish and pale right now; he appears about to fall sick. |
| 田中さんは**病気だそうだ**。 | Pure Hearsay: Someone told me that Tanaka is sick. |
| 田中さんは**病気のようだ / 病気みたいだ**。 | Sensory Inference: His voice sounds hoarse and he's coughing; it seems he is sick. |
| 田中さんは**病気らしい**。 | External Rumor: Word going around the office is that he is sick. |
| 田中さんは**病気のはずだ**。 | Logical Deduction: He never misses a single day of work, so he logically must be sick. |
