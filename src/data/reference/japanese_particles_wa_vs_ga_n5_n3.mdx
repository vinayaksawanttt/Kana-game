# Japanese Particles: は (*wa*) vs. が (*ga*)

The distinction between **は** and **が** is famous as one of the most challenging concepts for Japanese learners. In English, both often translate to the grammatical subject ("is", "does"), but in Japanese, they govern entirely different communicative functions:

- **は (Topic Marker)**: Sets the conversational backdrop or theme (*"Speaking of X..."*). The focus falls on the **comment following は**.
- **が (Subject / Identifier Marker)**: Marks the specific actor or grammatical agent. The focus falls on the **word preceding が**.

```
[Topic] は ────► [WHAT FOLLOWS IS THE FOCUS]
  "As for Tanaka, he is a doctor."  (Tanaka wa isha desu.)

[WHAT PRECEDES IS THE FOCUS] ◄──── が [Subject]
  "Tanaka is the one who is a doctor."  (Tanaka ga isha desu.)
```

---

## 1. Rule 1: Old / Known Information (は) vs. New Information (が)

A fundamental principle of Japanese discourse is that **は** frames known, established topics, whereas **が** introduces new entities into the conversation.

### The Classic Folktale Pattern
Notice how characters are introduced in Japanese storytelling:

> 昔々、ある所に、おじいさんとおばあさん**が**住んでいました。 (*Introduction of new characters: が*)  
> おじいさん**は**山へ柴刈りに、おばあさん**は**川へ洗濯に行きました。 (*Now established as topics: は*)

- **First mention (New information)**: Mark with **が**.
- **Subsequent mentions (Established topic)**: Mark with **は**.

---

## 2. Rule 2: Question Words (Wh-Questions)

The placement of question words (**誰** *who*, **何** *what*, **どこ** *where*, **どれ** *which*) dictates the particle:

### A. Question word is the Subject ──► Always use が
A question word represents entirely unknown information; it can never be a topic (topics must be known context):
- **誰が**来ましたか。 (*Dare ga kimashita ka.* — **Who** came?)
  - Answer mirrors with **が**: 田中さん**が**来ました。 (*Tanaka came.*)
- **何が**起きましたか。 (*Nan ga okimashita ka.* — **What** happened?)
- **どれが**あなたの鍵ですか。 (*Dore ga anata no kagi desu ka.* — **Which one** is your key?)

### B. Question word is the Predicate ──► Topic uses は
When the entity being asked about is known, and the unknown part is the predicate, use **は**:
- 田中さん**は**どこですか。 (*Where is Tanaka?*)
- あなたの趣味**は**何ですか。 (*What is your hobby?*)

---

## 3. Rule 3: Phenomenon vs. Exhaustive Selection vs. Contrast

### 1. Neutral Sensory Observation (Phenomenon が)
When describing a scene unfolding before your eyes (weather, natural sights, sudden occurrences) without personal commentary, use **が**:
- あ、雨**が**降ってきた！ (*Ah, rain has started falling!*)
- 空**が**青い。 (*The sky is blue [an immediate visual observation].*)
- バス**が**来ましたよ。 (*The bus has arrived!*)

### 2. Exhaustive Listing ("X and X alone") with が
When answering a question of identity, **が** singles out the subject to the exclusion of others:
- 私**が**やります。 (*I am the one who will do it [not anyone else].*)
- これ**が**探していた本です。 (*This [and no other] is the book I was looking for.*)

### 3. Contrastive は
When comparing two elements, or implying that a statement does not apply to other things, **は** replaces or supplements other particles:
- 昼はお茶**は**飲みますが、コーヒー**は**飲みません。  
  (*During the day, I drink green tea, but [in contrast] I don't drink coffee.*)
- ひらがな**は**書けますが、漢字**は**書けません。  
  (*I can write hiragana, but [by contrast] I cannot write kanji.*)

---

## 4. Rule 4: Subordinate Clauses Must Use が

In complex sentences, any subject residing inside a **subordinate clause** (relative clauses, time clauses, conditional clauses, reason clauses) is marked by **が**, almost never by **は**:

### 1. Relative Clauses (Noun-Modifying Clauses)
- [私**が**作った] ケーキはおいしいです。  
  *Watashi ga tsukutta kēki wa oishii desu.*  
  (The cake [that **I** made] is delicious.)  
  *(Never: \*私が作ったケーキ...)*

### 2. Time & Conditional Clauses (とき, 前に, たら, ば)
- [友達**が**来た] とき、料理をしていました。  
  *Tomodachi ga kita toki, ryōri o shite imashita.*  
  (When my friend arrived, I was cooking.)
- [雨**が**降ったら]、試合は中止です。  
  *Ame ga futtara, shiai wa chūshi desu.*  
  (If it rains, the game is canceled.)

### 3. Reason Clauses (から, ので)
- [バス**が**遅れた] ので、遅刻しました。  
  *Basu ga okureta node, chikoku shimashita.*  
  (Because the bus was late, I arrived tardy.)

---

## 5. Rule 5: Predicates of Emotion, Ability, Desire & Existence

A special class of Japanese verbs and adjectives takes **が** to mark the object of emotion, understanding, ability, or existence:

| Category | Typical Words | Structure | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Likes / Dislikes** | 好き (*suki*), 嫌い (*kirai*) | [Noun] が 好き/嫌い | 私は猫**が**好きです。<br/>(*I like cats.*) |
| **Skill / Competence** | 上手 (*jōzu*), 下手 (*heta*), 得意 (*tokui*) | [Noun] が 上手/下手 | 田中さんは歌**が**上手です。<br/>(*Tanaka is good at singing.*) |
| **Comprehension** | 分かる (*wakaru*) | [Noun] が 分かる | 日本語**が**分かります。<br/>(*I understand Japanese.*) |
| **Potential / Ability** | できる (*dekiru*), Potential verbs | [Noun] が できる/読める | 漢字**が**読めます。<br/>(*I can read kanji.*) |
| **Desire** | 欲しい (*hoshii*), 〜たい (*-tai*) | [Noun] が 欲しい/食べたい | 新しいパソコン**が**欲しい。<br/>(*I want a new computer.*) |
| **Existence** | ある (*aru*), いる (*iru*) | [Noun] が ある/いる | 庭に犬**が**います。<br/>(*There is a dog in the garden.*) |

## 6. The "Double Subject" Construction: [Topic] は [Attribute] が [Predicate]

Japanese frequently uses **は** to establish an overarching domain, and **が** to identify the specific feature being described:

```
[Broad Topic] は + [Specific Feature] が + [Quality / State]
```

### Classic Examples
1. **象は鼻が長い。**  
   *Zō wa hana ga nagai.*  
   (As for the elephant, its trunk is long.)
2. **日本は物価が高い。**  
   *Nihon wa bukka ga takai.*  
   (As for Japan, commodity prices are high.)
3. **私は頭が痛い。**  
   *Watashi wa atama ga itai.*  
   (As for me, my head hurts. / I have a headache.)
4. **マリアさんは髪が長いです。**  
   *Maria-san wa kami ga nagai desu.*  
   (As for Maria, her hair is long.)

## Summary Decision Tree

```
Are you asking a question with a question word as subject (Who/What)?
  └─► YES ──► Use が (誰が来ますか？)

Is the noun inside a subordinate clause (before とき, から, たら, or modifying a noun)?
  └─► YES ──► Use が (雨が降ったとき / 私が買った本)

Is it an immediate sensory observation of an event happening right now?
  └─► YES ──► Use が (あ、バスが来た！)

Is it an object of 好き, 嫌い, 分かる, できる, 欲しい, ある, いる?
  └─► YES ──► Use が (犬が好き / 英語が分かる)

Are you contrasting two things ("X is true, but Y is not")?
  └─► YES ──► Use は (お茶は飲むが、酒は飲まない)

Are you setting the general topic or theme of the sentence ("As for X...")?
  └─► YES ──► Use は (私は学生です / 田中さんは優しい)
```
