# Japanese い-Adjectives and な-Adjectives

Japanese adjectives describe qualities, states, and properties of nouns. Unlike English adjectives, which remain uninflected (e.g., *tall*, *was tall*, *not tall*), Japanese adjectives conjugate directly to reflect **tense** (present/past), **polarity** (affirmative/negative), and **politeness** (plain/polite).

All Japanese adjectives belong to one of two structural classes:

1. **い-Adjectives (形容詞 / *keiyōshi*)**: True adjectives whose dictionary forms invariably end in the hiragana **い** (e.g., 高い, 暑い, 新しい). They conjugate by altering their final **い**.
2. **な-Adjectives (形容動詞 / *keiyōdōshi*)**: Adjectival nouns that behave like nouns when standing alone, but attach **な** when modifying a noun directly (e.g., 静かな, 元気な, きれいな). They conjugate using the copula (**だ / です**).

---

## 1. Modifying Nouns Directly (Attributive Position)

When an adjective is placed immediately before a noun to describe it, the two classes behave differently:

| Adjective Type | Rule | Example | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **い-Adjective** | Keep dictionary form ending in **い** + Noun | **高い**本 (*takai hon*) | an expensive book |
| | | **新しい**車 (*atarashii kuruma*) | a new car |
| **な-Adjective** | Add **な** to the stem + Noun | **静かな**部屋 (*shizuka na heya*) | a quiet room |
| | | **元気な**子ども (*genki na kodomo*) | an energetic child |

> [!NOTE]
> The dictionary or base form of a な-adjective is simply its stem without な (e.g., 静か, 元気, 有名). You only append **な** when directly linking it to a following noun.

---

## 2. い-Adjectives (形容詞) Conjugation

To conjugate an い-adjective, take the dictionary form, drop the final **い** to find the **stem**, and attach the appropriate suffix.

### Formation Rules

- **Stem**: Drop the final **〜い** (e.g., 高い → **高〜**; 寒い → **寒〜**).
- **Negative (〜くない)**: Stem + **くない**
- **Past Affirmative (〜かった)**: Stem + **かった**
- **Past Negative (〜くなかった)**: Stem + **くなかった**
- **Connecting Form (〜くて)**: Stem + **くて**
- **Adverbial Form (〜く)**: Stem + **く** (modifies verbs)

### Plain vs. Polite Paradigm: 高い (*takai*, expensive / high)

| Form | Plain (Casual) | Polite (Desu) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-past Affirmative** | 高い (*takai*) | 高いです (*takai desu*) | is expensive |
| **Non-past Negative** | 高くない (*takakunai*) | 高くないです (*takakunai desu*)<br/>高くありません (*takaku arimasen*) | is not expensive |
| **Past Affirmative** | 高かった (*takakatta*) | 高かったです (*takakatta desu*) | was expensive |
| **Past Negative** | 高くなかった (*takakunakatta*) | 高くなかったです (*takakunakatta desu*)<br/>高くありませんでした (*takaku arimasendeshita*) | was not expensive |

> [!WARNING]
> **Never attach でした directly to an い-adjective!**  
> Incorrect: \*高いでした (\*takai deshita)  
> Correct: **高かったです** (*takakatta desu*)  
> In polite い-adjective sentences, **です** acts solely as a politeness marker; tense and polarity are carried entirely by the adjective's own inflection (**〜かった / 〜くない**).

### The Essential Irregular Exception: いい / よい (*good*)

The common word for "good" is pronounced **いい** (*ii*) in the plain affirmative non-past form. However, **all other conjugations are built from the formal stem よい (*yoi*)**:

| Form | Plain | Polite |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-past Affirmative** | **いい** / **よい** | いいです / よいです |
| **Non-past Negative** | **よくない** (*not* \*いくない) | よくないです / よくありません |
| **Past Affirmative** | **よかった** (*not* \*いった) | よかったです |
| **Past Negative** | **よくなかった** (*not* \*いくなかった) | よくなかったです / よくありませんでした |
| **Connecting (て-form)** | **よくて** (*not* \*いくて) | — |
| **Adverbial** | **よく** (well / often) | — |

Compound adjectives ending in いい also follow this rule:
- かっこいい (*stylish/cool*) → かっこ**よくない**, かっこ**よかった**
- 頭がいい (*smart*) → 頭が**よくない**, 頭が**よかった**

## 3. な-Adjectives (形容動詞) Conjugation

A な-adjective stem behaves grammatically like a noun when ending a predicate sentence. It takes the auxiliary copula **だ** (plain) or **です** (polite).

### Plain vs. Polite Paradigm: 静か (*shizuka*, quiet)

| Form | Plain (Casual) | Polite (Desu) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-past Affirmative** | 静か**だ** (*shizuka da*) | 静か**です** (*shizuka desu*) | is quiet |
| **Non-past Negative** | 静か**じゃない** (*shizuka ja nai*)<br/>静か**ではない** (*shizuka de wa nai*) | 静か**じゃないです** (*shizuka ja nai desu*)<br/>静か**じゃありません** (*shizuka ja arimasen*)<br/>静か**ではありません** (*formal*) | is not quiet |
| **Past Affirmative** | 静か**だった** (*shizuka datta*) | 静か**でした** (*shizuka deshita*) | was quiet |
| **Past Negative** | 静か**じゃなかった** (*shizuka ja nakatta*)<br/>静か**ではなかった** (*shizuka de wa nakatta*) | 静か**じゃなかったです** (*shizuka ja nakatta desu*)<br/>静か**じゃありませんでした** (*shizuka ja arimasendeshita*)<br/>静か**ではありませんでした** (*formal*) | was not quiet |

> [!NOTE]
> **では** (*de wa*) is more formal and standard in written Japanese, while **じゃ** (*ja*) is standard in everyday conversation.

## 4. Connecting Adjectives: The て-Form

When chaining multiple adjectives together ("is cheap **and** delicious", "is quiet **and** clean"), use the adjective's **て-form**:

### い-Adjectives: Drop 〜い, add 〜くて

- 安い (*cheap*) + 美味しい (*delicious*)  
  → この店は**安くて**美味しいです。 (*Kono mise wa yasukute oishii desu.* - This shop is cheap and delicious.)
- 広い (*spacious*) + 明るい (*bright*)  
  → 部屋は**広くて**明るいです。 (*Heya wa hirokute akarui desu.* - The room is spacious and bright.)

### な-Adjectives: Attach で to the stem

- 静か (*quiet*) + きれい (*pretty/clean*)  
  → 図書館は**静かで**きれいです。 (*Toshokan wa shizuka de kirei desu.* - The library is quiet and clean.)
- 親切 (*kind*) + 元気 (*cheerful*)  
  → 田中さんは**親切で**元気な人です。 (*Tanaka-san wa shinsetsu de genki na hito desu.* - Mr. Tanaka is a kind and cheerful person.)

### Mixing い- and な-Adjectives

You can freely mix both classes. The ending of the first adjective determines whether you use **〜くて** or **〜で**:
- **な-adj first**: この町は**静かで**、とても**いい**ところです。 (*This town is quiet and a very nice place.*)
- **い-adj first**: この部屋は**広くて**、**きれい**です。 (*This room is spacious and clean.*)

## 5. Adverbial Forms: Modifying Verbs

To turn an adjective into an adverb describing **how** an action happens (e.g., *speak quietly*, *wake up early*):

| Adjective Class | Transformation | Example Adjective | Adverbial Form | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **い-Adjective** | Drop **い** → add **く** | 早い (*early*) | **早く** (*hayaku*) | 毎朝、**早く**起きます。<br/>(*I wake up early every morning.*) |
| | | 美味しい (*delicious*) | **美味しく** (*oishiku*) | 料理を**美味しく**作りました。<br/>(*I made the dish deliciously.*) |
| **な-Adjective** | Add **に** to stem | 静か (*quiet*) | **静かに** (*shizuka ni*) | 図書館では**静かに**話してください。<br/>(*Please speak quietly in the library.*) |
| | | 上手 (*skillful*) | **上手に** (*jōzu ni*) | 日本語を**上手に**話します。<br/>(*Speaks Japanese skillfully.*) |

### State Change: 〜くなる / 〜になる (To become...)

Combining the adverbial form with the verb **なる** (*naru*, to become) expresses a change of state:
- **い-adjective**: 寒**く**なる (*samuku naru*, become cold) → 冬になると、寒**くなります**。 (*When winter arrives, it gets cold.*)
- **な-adjective**: 元気**に**なる (*genki ni naru*, recover / become healthy) → 薬を飲んで、元気**になりました**。 (*I took medicine and became well.*)

## 6. The "Looks like an い-Adjective" Trap

A few very frequent な-adjectives end with the syllable **い** in their dictionary form. Learners frequently mistake them for い-adjectives, causing conjugation errors:

| Word | Kanji & Reading | True Classification | Correct Noun Modification | Common Mistake to Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **きれい** | 綺麗 (*kirei*) | **な-adjective** | きれい**な**部屋 | \*きれいくない (Incorrect!) → **きれいじゃない** |
| **有名** | ゆうめい (*yūmei*) | **な-adjective** | 有名**な**人 | \*有名くない (Incorrect!) → **有名じゃない** |
| **嫌い** | きらい (*kirai*) | **な-adjective** | 嫌い**な**食べ物 | \*嫌いくない (Incorrect!) → **嫌いじゃない** |
| **得意** | とくい (*tokui*) | **な-adjective** | 得意**な**スポーツ | \*得意くない (Incorrect!) → **得意じゃない** |
| **苦手** | にがて (*nigate*) | **な-adjective** | 苦手**な**科目 | \*苦手くない (Incorrect!) → **苦手じゃない** |
| **幸い** | さいわい (*saiwai*) | **な-adjective** | 幸い**な**ことに | \*幸いくない (Incorrect!) → **幸いじゃない** |

> [!TIP]
> Notice the kanji spelling: 綺麗 (*kire-i*), 有名 (*yū-me-i*), 嫌い (*ki-ra-i*). In Sino-Japanese words, the final *i* sound is part of the kanji reading itself (like 銘 *mei* or 麗 *rei*), rather than trailing okurigana. If a word ends in **〜ei** (like *kirei*, *yūmei*), it is virtually always a **な-adjective**.
