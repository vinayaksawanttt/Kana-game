# Business Japanese Phrases and Etiquette (ビジネス日本語)

Working in a Japanese company or interacting professionally with Japanese clients requires a mastery of **ビジネス日本語 (*Bijinesu Nihongo*)**. Workplace Japanese relies on established formulaic expressions, empathetic hedging phrases known as **クッション言葉 (*cushion words*)**, and strict adherence to internal and external hierarchies.

---

## 1. Daily Workplace Greetings: In-Office Etiquette

Greeting coworkers properly is fundamental to teamwork (*team play*) and mutual respect in the office.

### The Big Workplace Rule: お疲れ様 vs ご苦労様

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  お疲れ様です (Otsukaresama desu)                      │
│  • Used by anyone to peers, superiors, or subordinates. │
│  • The safest, standard office greeting.                │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  ご苦労様です (Gokurōsama desu)                        │
│  • Strictly used by SUPERIORS speaking to SUBORDINATES. │
│  • ⚠️ NEVER say this to your boss, senior, or client!   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Situation | Spoken Phrase | Romaji | Meaning & Nuance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arriving at work** | **おはようございます** | *ohayō gozaimasu* | "Good morning" (even if reporting for an afternoon shift). |
| **Leaving desk for meeting** | **行ってまいります** | *itte mairimasu* | "I am heading out (to a meeting/visit)." |
| **Coworkers reply** | **行ってらっしゃいませ** | *itterasshaimase* | "Please take care / Have a good visit." |
| **Returning to desk** | **ただいま戻りました** | *tadaima modorimashita* | "I have returned." |
| **Coworkers reply** | **お帰りなさいませ** | *okaerinasaimase* | "Welcome back." |
| **Leaving for the day** | **お先に失礼します** | *osaki ni shitsurei shimasu* | "Excuse me for leaving ahead of you." |
| **Remaining staff reply** | **お疲れ様でした** | *otsukaresama deshita* | "Thank you for your hard work today." |

## 2. Cushion Words (クッション言葉: Kusshon Kotoba)

In Japanese business communication, making a direct request or refusal sounds abrasive. Speakers prepend **cushion words** to soften the impact, demonstrate empathy, and signal consideration before delivering a request, decline, or inquiry.

| Cushion Expression | Romaji | Literal Meaning | Best Used When... |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **恐れ入りますが** | *osoreirimasu ga* | "I am filled with awe/trepidation, but..." | Asking for a favor, confirmation, or information. |
| **お手数をおかけしますが** | *otesū o okake shimasu ga* | "It causes you troublesome effort, but..." | Requesting a task that takes time or physical effort. |
| **ご面倒をおかけいたしますが** | *gomendō o okake itashimasu ga* | "It is an inconvenience, but..." | Asking someone to double-check or redo something. |
| **差し支えなければ** | *sashitsukae nakereba* | "If there is no hindrance / if it's convenient..." | Asking a sensitive or personal question (e.g., schedule, contact info). |
| **あいにくですが** | *ainiku desu ga* | "Unfortunately / Unluckily, but..." | Declining a request or delivering bad news. |
| **申し訳ございませんが** | *mōshiwake gozaimasen ga* | "There is no excuse, but..." | Making a difficult request or refusing an offer. |

### Practical Examples in Action:
- **恐れ入りますが**、もう一度お名前を伺えますでしょうか。
  *(Osoreirimasu ga, mō ichido o-namae o ukaemasu deshō ka.)*
  — Excuse me, but could I please have your name once more?
- **お手数をおかけしますが**、添付書類をご確認いただけますか。
  *(Otesū o okake shimasu ga, tempu shorui o go-kakunin itadakemasu ka.)*
  — I apologize for the trouble, but could you please verify the attached documents?
- **差し支えなければ**、ご連絡先を教えていただけますでしょうか。
  *(Sashitsukae nakereba, go-renrakusaki o oshiete itadakemasu deshō ka.)*
  — If you don't mind, could you share your contact details with us?

## 3. Affirmation and Agreement: 承知しました vs 了解しました

When receiving an instruction from a client or manager:

| Phrase | Appropriate for Superiors/Clients? | Correct Usage & Nuance |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **承知いたしました** (*shōchi itashimashita*) | ⭕ **Gold Standard** | Formal, respectful humble acceptance. The best business choice. |
| **かしこまりました** (*kashikomarimashita*) | ⭕ **Gold Standard** | Polite acknowledgment, widely used with clients and customers. |
| **了解しました** (*ryōkai shimashita*) | ❌ **Forbidden to superiors** | Means "comprehended/roger". Implies equal or senior authority granting approval. |
| **分かりました** (*wakarimashita*) | ⚠️ Avoid | Too casual for clients and senior management. |

## 4. Business Meeting and Client Interaction Phrases

| Situation | Japanese Phrase | English Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Universal client opening** | **いつも大変お世話になっております。** | Thank you very much for your continuous kind support. |
| **First-time meeting a company** | **平素は格別のご高配を賜り、厚く御礼申し上げます。** | We express our deepest gratitude for your exceptional patronage (written/formal). |
| **Receiving a business card** | **頂戴いたします。よろしくお願い申し上げます。** | I gratefully receive your card. I look forward to working with you. |
| **Proposing an idea** | **〜の件につきまして、ご提案申し上げます。** | Regarding the matter of..., I would like to submit a proposal. |
| **Polite refusal / rejection** | **誠に遺憾ではございますが、今回は見送らせていただきます。** | It is truly regrettable, but we must pass on this opportunity this time. |
| **Deferring for consideration** | **社内で持ち帰り、検討させていただきます。** | I will take this back internally and we will deliberate on it. |
| **Apologizing for an oversight** | **こちらの不手際で、大変ご迷惑をおかけいたしました。** | Due to an oversight on our part, we have caused you tremendous inconvenience. |

## 5. The Golden Rule of Workplace Communication: 報連相 (Hō-Ren-Sō)

Japanese companies place foundational emphasis on **報連相 (*Hō-Ren-Sō*)**, an acronym for the three pillars of teamwork:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  報 (Hō)  = 報告 (Hōkoku)  ──► Reporting progress/outcomes│
│  連 (Ren) = 連絡 (Renraku) ──► Communicating factual info │
│  相 (Sō)  = 相談 (Sōdan)   ──► Consulting before deciding  │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Essential Hō-Ren-Sō Phrases:
- **ご報告がございます。** (*Go-hōkoku ga gozaimasu.* — I have a report to deliver.)
- **〇〇の件について、進捗状況をご報告いたします。** (*...shinchoku jōkyō o go-hōkoku itashimasu.* — I would like to report the progress status on...)
- **今、少々お時間よろしいでしょうか。ご相談したい件がございます。** (*Ima, shōshō o-jikan yoroshii deshō ka. Go-sōdan shitai ken ga gozaimasu.* — Do you have a moment right now? There is a matter I would like to consult you about.)

## 6. Business Etiquette: The Business Card Exchange (名刺交換: Meishi Kōkan)

Exchanging business cards (*meishi*) is treated as handling the person's own face and honor:

1. **Both Hands**: Hold your card with both hands by the corners, ensuring no text is covered.
2. **Lower Position**: Present your card lower than the other person's card if you are lower in rank.
3. **Say Aloud**:
   > 「株式会社〇〇の[姓]と申します。どうぞよろしくお願い申し上げます。」
4. **Receiving**:
   > 「頂戴いたします。」 (*Chōdai itashimasu.*)
5. **Seated Meeting**: Place their card neatly on top of your card case on the table directly in front of you. Never write on their card or immediately stuff it into your pocket!
