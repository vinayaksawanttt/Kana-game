# Japanese Basic Particles (助詞 / Joshi)

In Japanese, **particles (助詞 / *joshi*)** are postpositional grammatical markers attached directly to nouns, verbs, adjectives, or clauses. They define the grammatical relationship between words—specifying who is doing what, where, when, why, and how.

Unlike English, which relies primarily on rigid Subject-Verb-Object word order, Japanese word order is relatively flexible because **particles clearly state the grammatical role of every constituent phrase**.

---

## Master Particle Quick Reference

| Particle | Pronunciation | Primary Function | Core English Equivalent | Example Sentence |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **は** | *wa* | Topic of sentence / Contrast | "As for...", "Speaking of..." | 私は学生です。 (*I am a student.*) |
| **が** | *ga* | Grammatical subject / New information | Subject marker ("the one who...") | 猫がいます。 (*There is a cat.*) |
| **を** | *o* (*wo*) | Direct object of transitive verb | Marks the recipient of action | 水を飲みます。 (*I drink water.*) |
| **に** | *ni* | Specific time / Destination / Indirect object / Static location | "At", "on", "to", "in" | 7時に起きます。 (*I wake up at 7:00.*) |
| **で** | *de* | Location of action / Means / Tool / Cause | "At" (doing), "by", "with" | 図書館で勉強します。 (*I study at the library.*) |
| **へ** | *e* (*he*) | Direction of movement towards a destination | "Toward", "heading to" | 日本へ行きます。 (*I am heading to Japan.*) |
| **と** | *to* | Exhaustive listing / Companion ("with") | "And" (nouns only), "with" | パンと卵 (*bread and eggs*); 友人と話す (*talk with a friend*) |
| **も** | *mo* | Inclusion / Addition (replaces は, が, を) | "Also", "too", "as well" | 私も行きます。 (*I will go too.*) |
| **の** | *no* | Possession / Noun modification / Apposition | "'s", "of", "belonging to" | 私の本 (*my book*); 日本の車 (*Japanese car*) |

## 1. は (*wa*) — The Topic Marker

Although written with the hiragana **は** (*ha*), it is always pronounced **wa** when functioning as a particle.

- **Primary Role**: Introduces the **topic** (the shared contextual theme being discussed): *"Speaking of X, [comment]."*
- **Contrastive Role**: Highlights a contrast between two elements.

```
[Topic] は + [Comment / Description]
```

### Examples
1. **私は田中です。**  
   *Watashi wa Tanaka desu.*  
   (Speaking of myself, I am Tanaka.)
2. **今日はお肉を食べますが、魚は食べません。**  
   *Kyō wa oniku o tabemasu ga, sakana wa tabemasen.*  
   (Today I will eat meat, but [in contrast] as for fish, I won't eat it.)

---

## 2. が (*ga*) — The Subject & Identifier Marker

- **Primary Role**: Marks the **grammatical subject** carrying out the action or being described.
- **Specific Identifier**: Identifies *who* or *which* specific entity fulfills the predicate.
- **Verbs of Existence & Perception**: Obligatory with **ある / いる** (existence), **好き / 嫌い** (likes/dislikes), **上手 / 下手** (skills), and **分かる** (understanding).

### Examples
1. **雨が降っています。**  
   *Ame ga futte imasu.*  
   (Rain is falling. [Natural phenomenon])
2. **誰が来ましたか。 — 田中さんが来ました。**  
   *Dare ga kimashita ka. — Tanaka-san ga kimashita.*  
   (Who came? — Mr. Tanaka came. [Specific identification])
3. **猫が好きです。**  
   *Neko ga suki desu.*  
   (I like cats.)
4. **机の上に本があります。**  
   *Tsukue no ue ni hon ga arimasu.*  
   (There is a book on the desk.)

---

## 3. を (*o*) — The Direct Object Marker

Written with the unique hiragana **を** (*wo*), pronounced simply **o**.

- **Primary Role**: Marks the **direct object** receiving a transitive verb's action.
- **Motion Through Space**: Marks the space traversed, crossed, or departed from with motion verbs (e.g., 渡る, 歩く, 出る).

### Examples
1. **りんごを食べます。**  
   *Ringo o tabemasu.*  
   (I eat an apple.)
2. **本を読みました。**  
   *Hon o yomimashita.*  
   (I read a book.)
3. **橋を渡ります。**  
   *Hashi o watarimasu.*  
   (I cross over the bridge. [Space traversed])
4. **毎朝、7時に家を出ます。**  
   *Maiasa, shichiji ni ie o demasu.*  
   (Every morning, I leave the house at 7:00. [Point of departure])

---

## 4. に (*ni*) — Target, Specific Time & Static Location

The most versatile N5 particle, **に** indicates pinpoint focus:

1. **Specific Time**: Marks clock times, days, and months that have numbers (do not use with relative words like 今日 or 毎日).
   - **7時に起きます。** (*I wake up at 7:00.*)
   - **月曜日にテストがあります。** (*There is a test on Monday.*)
2. **Static Location of Existence**: Used with **ある / いる / 住む / 泊まる**.
   - **東京に住んでいます。** (*I live in Tokyo.*)
   - **教室に先生がいます。** (*The teacher is in the classroom.*)
3. **Destination of Movement**: Target destination for **行く / 来る / 帰る**.
   - **学校に行きます。** (*I go to school.*)
4. **Indirect Object / Recipient**: Marks to whom an action is directed.
   - **友達に手紙を書きます。** (*I write a letter to a friend.*)

---

## 5. で (*de*) — Location of Action & Means/Tool

While **に** marks static presence, **で** marks dynamic activity and instruments:

1. **Location where an Action Happens**:
   - **カフェでコーヒーを飲みます。** (*I drink coffee at a cafe.*)
   - **公園で走ります。** (*I run in the park.*)
2. **Means of Transport, Tool, or Medium**:
   - **電車で会社へ行きます。** (*I go to work by train.*)
   - **箸でご飯を食べます。** (*I eat rice with chopsticks.*)
   - **日本語で話してください。** (*Please speak in Japanese.*)
3. **Total / Scope / Boundary**:
   - **全部で千円です。** (*It is 1,000 yen in total.*)

---

## 6. へ (*e*) — Directional Movement

Written with **へ** (*he*), pronounced **e**.

- **Primary Role**: Emphasizes the **direction or vector** of movement toward a destination ("heading toward").
- **Contrast with に**: **に** emphasizes the point of arrival; **へ** emphasizes the orientation toward the destination.
- **Noun Modification with の**: Can combine as **〜への** (e.g., 日本への旅行 = *a trip to Japan*). Note that \*〜にの is ungrammatical!

### Examples
1. **西へ向かって進みます。**  
   *Nishi e mukatte susumimasu.*  
   (Proceed heading toward the west.)
2. **明日、京都へ行きます。**  
   *Ashita, Kyōto e ikimasu.*  
   (Tomorrow, I am heading to Kyoto.)

---

## 7. と (*to*) — Complete Listing & Accompaniment

1. **Exhaustive "And" (Nouns Only)**: Lists every item completely (contrast with **や** which implies an incomplete list).
   - **机の上にペンとノートがあります。** (*There are a pen and a notebook [and nothing else] on the desk.*)
2. **Accompaniment ("With")**:
   - **家族と日本へ来ました。** (*I came to Japan with my family.*)
3. **Reciprocal Action**: Used with verbs like 会う (meet), 結婚する (marry), 話す (talk).
   - **先生と話しました。** (*I spoke with the teacher.*)

---

## 8. も (*mo*) — Inclusive Addition ("Also / Too")

- **Primary Role**: Replaces **は**, **が**, and **を** to mean "also", "too", or "neither".
- **Combined with other particles**: Appends after **に**, **で**, **と**, **から**, etc. (e.g., にも, でも, とも).
- **Double も (も...も)**: Expresses "both A and B" or "neither A nor B".

### Examples
1. **私も学生です。**  
   *Watashi mo gakusei desu.*  
   (I am also a student. [Replaces は])
2. **お茶も飲みます。**  
   *Ocha mo nomimasu.*  
   (I will drink tea as well. [Replaces を])
3. **日本へも行きたいです。**  
   *Nihon e mo ikitai desu.*  
   (I want to go to Japan too. [Appends after へ])
4. **犬も猫も好きです。**  
   *Inu mo neko mo suki desu.*  
   (I like both dogs and cats.)

---

## 9. の (*no*) — Possession & Noun-to-Noun Linker

In Japanese, two nouns can virtually never be placed side-by-side without a connector. **の** bridges them:

1. **Possession**:
   - **私の傘** (*my umbrella*)
2. **Origin / Category / Material**:
   - **日本の車** (*Japanese car*)
   - **木綿のシャツ** (*cotton shirt*)
3. **Apposition (Identity)**:
   - **友達の田中さん** (*my friend Tanaka*)
4. **Pronoun Replacement ("the one")**:
   - **この赤いのは私のです。** (*This red one is mine.*)

## Summary Checklist for N5 Particles

```
Who is speaking / Topic?  ──► は (wa)
Who specifically acts?    ──► が (ga)
What is acted upon?       ──► を (o)
At what exact time?       ──► に (ni)
In what static place?     ──► に (ni)
Where does action occur?  ──► で (de)
By what tool or vehicle?  ──► で (de)
Toward which direction?   ──► へ (e)
With whom or what total?  ──► と (to)
In addition / Also?       ──► も (mo)
Belonging to what noun?   ──► の (no)
```
