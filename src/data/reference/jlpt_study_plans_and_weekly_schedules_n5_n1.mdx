# JLPT Study Plans and Weekly Schedules (学習計画と週間スケジュール)

Preparing for the JLPT requires structured consistency rather than last-minute cramming. Because the test evaluates intuitive language recognition and processing speed under tight time constraints, lasting success relies on **spaced repetition, deliberate grammar review, daily auditory exposure, and timed simulation**.

---

## 2. The 4-Phase Preparation Roadmap (4段階の学習ロードマップ)

A disciplined 6-month preparation cycle divides into four structured phases:

```
Month 1–2: Phase 1 (Foundation)      ──► Kanji & Core Vocabulary (SRS / Flashcards)
Month 3–4: Phase 2 (Structure)       ──► Grammar Patterns & Sentence Mechanics
Month 5:   Phase 3 (Immersion & Drill)──► Reading Speed Drills & Audio Question Types
Month 6:   Phase 4 (Simulation)      ──► Full Timed Mocks & Error Analysis Logging
```

### Phase 1: Knowledge Foundation (Month 1–2)
- **Goal**: Lock in required Kanji and Vocabulary.
- **Tools**: Spaced Repetition Systems (Anki / FSRS / Kana-game), Kanji radical breakdown sheets.
- **Rule**: Minimum 15–25 new words/kanji per day with 100% daily review completion.

### Phase 2: Grammar & Sentence Structure (Month 3–4)
- **Goal**: Internalize grammar formulas, nuances, and star-sentence word order.
- **Rule**: Do not just read grammar entries; write **two original example sentences** for every grammar point to cement active recall.

### Phase 3: Input Immersion & Skill Drills (Month 5)
- **Goal**: Develop speed in Reading (*Dokkai*) and real-time comprehension in Listening (*Chōkai*).
- **Activities**:
  - Read Japanese news (NHK News Web Easy for N4/N3; standard Asahi/Mainichi/Nikkei for N2/N1).
  - Listen to natural podcasts (Nihongo con Teppei, Japanese with Shun, News in Slow Japanese).
  - Complete 20-minute timed reading question drills.

### Phase 4: Full Simulation & Error Analysis (Month 6)
- **Goal**: Build psychological stamina and eliminate timing mistakes.
- **Action**: Take at least **3 to 4 complete, timed official practice exams** under exact test-day conditions.
- **Error Analysis**: Maintain a dedicated **間違いノート (*Machigai Nōto* / Error Log)**.

## 3. Weekly Schedule Templates (週間スケジュール例)

### Template A: The Balanced Busy Schedule (1 Hour / Day — ~8 hrs/week)
Ideal for working professionals and university students:

| Day | Focus Area | Detailed Activities | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Monday** | Kanji & Vocabulary | Daily SRS reviews + 20 new kanji/vocab cards. | 45 min |
| **Tuesday** | Grammar Focus | Study 3 new grammar points; write 2 sample sentences each. | 60 min |
| **Wednesday** | Reading Sprint | 1 Short Reading passage + 1 Medium passage under timed clock. | 60 min |
| **Thursday** | Listening Practice | 15 min SRS reviews + 30 min listening drills (Task & Key Point). | 45 min |
| **Friday** | Quick Response & Vocab | 20 Quick Response audio questions + vocab review. | 45 min |
| **Saturday** | Long Reading & Analysis | 1 Long Reading text; dissect unknown grammar and connectors. | 90 min |
| **Sunday** | Weekly Error Review | Review the week's Error Log; re-test missed questions; rest. | 60 min |

## 4. The Error Analysis Notebook (間違いノート: Machigai Nōto)

Simply taking mock tests and checking the score is ineffective. High scorers analyze **why** an error happened:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│           Error Log Entry Example (ミス分析例)          │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Test: N2 July 2023 Mock 1, Reading Question #12        │
│ Correct Answer: 3                                      │
│ My Chosen Answer: 1                                    │
│                                                        │
│ [Why I got it wrong]:                                  │
│ I chose Option 1 because it contained the exact words   │
│ 「地球温暖化の影響」 from paragraph 2.                  │
│ However, that was a cited theory refuted in line 14:   │
│ 「しかし、これは誤解にすぎない」.                     │
│ Option 3 was the true paraphrase of the author's       │
│ final sentence ending in 「〜べきではないか」.         │
│                                                        │
│ [Actionable Rule to Remember]:                         │
│ Beware of identical-word matches in N2 reading! Check  │
│ whether the sentence connects to しかし or べきだ.     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> [!TIP]
> Categorize every missed question into one of three buckets:
> 1. **Vocabulary Deficit** (Did not know the word/kanji).
> 2. **Grammar Formula Misread** (Confused pattern with another form).
> 3. **Tactical Trap** (Fell for a distractor or ran out of time).
