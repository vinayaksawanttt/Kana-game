# Japanese Proverbs & Set Phrases: Kotowaza (ことわざ)

**Kotowaza (ことわざ / 諺)** are traditional Japanese proverbs and aphorisms that embody centuries of agrarian life lessons, Buddhist karmic philosophy, samurai honor codes, and classical folklore.

On the **JLPT N2 and N1**, proverbs frequently appear in reading comprehension essays, listening monologues, and essay questions as literary metaphors for human behavior and societal decision-making.

---

## 1. Perseverance, Effort, Patience & Time (努力・忍耐・時間)

| Japanese Proverb | Hiragana & Romaji | Literal Meaning | English Equivalent & Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **石の上にも三年** | いしのうえにもさんねん (*ishi no ue ni mo san-nen*) | Sitting on a cold rock for three years makes it warm | **Perseverance prevails / Patience wears out stones.** Remaining dedicated to a difficult pursuit for three years will eventually yield fruit. |
| **塵も積もれば山となる** | ちりもつもればやまとなる (*chiri mo tsumoreba yama to naru*) | If even specks of dust accumulate, they become a mountain | **Many a mickle makes a muckle / Small efforts compound into greatness.** Even small daily study habits create massive mastery over time. |
| **継続は力なり** | けいぞくはちからなり (*keizoku wa chikara nari*) | Continuity is strength | **Persistence is power.** The ability to continue without giving up is the ultimate determinant of success. |
| **雨降って地固まる** | あめふってじかたまる (*ame futte ji katamaru*) | After rainfall, the soil hardens | **Adversity strengthens the foundation / What doesn't kill you makes you stronger.** Hardships and conflicts solidify relationships. |
| **光陰矢の如し** | こういんやのごとし (*kōin ya no gotoshi*) | Light and shade (time) are like an arrow | **Time flies like an arrow.** Life passes swiftly and cannot be retrieved. |
| **蒔かぬ種は生えぬ** | まかぬたねははえぬ (*makanu tane wa haenu*) | A seed that is not sown will never sprout | **Nothing ventured, nothing gained / No pain, no gain.** Without taking the initiative, success is impossible. |

## 2. Caution, Foresight & Prevention (用心・準備・油断)

| Japanese Proverb | Hiragana & Romaji | Literal Imagery | Practical Meaning & Usage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **転ばぬ先の杖** | ころばぬさきのつえ (*korobanu saki no tsue*) | A walking cane before stumbling | **Look before you leap / An ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure.** Preparing countermeasures in advance before disaster strikes. |
| **備えあれば患いなし** | そなえあればうれいなし (*sonae areba urei nashi*) | If prepared, there is no grief | **Better safe than sorry / Forewarned is forearmed.** Complete emergency preparation eliminates anxiety. |
| **猿も木から落ちる** | さるもきからおちる (*saru mo ki kara ochiru*) | Even monkeys fall from trees | **Even masters make mistakes / Homer nods.** Nobody is infallible, no matter how skilled they are. |
| **河童の川流れ** | かっぱのかわながれ (*kappa no kawa-nagare*) | Even a mythical Kappa gets washed away in the river | Identical to *Saru mo ki kara ochiru* — a swimming master can still drown. |
| **弘法にも筆の誤り** | こうぼうにもふでのあやまり (*kōbō ni mo fude no ayamari*) | Even Kōbō Daishi makes brush errors | Even Japan's greatest calligrapher made mistakes; perfection is impossible. |
| **口は災いの元** | くちはわざわいのもと (*kuchi wa wazawai no moto*) | The mouth is the source of calamity | **Out of the mouth comes evil / Discretion is the better part of valor.** Careless speech causes ruin. |
| **触らぬ神に祟りなし**| さわらぬかみにたたりなし (*sawaranu kami ni tatari nashi*) | An untouched deity brings no curse | **Let sleeping dogs lie.** Avoid meddling in volatile affairs where you have nothing to gain. |

## 3. Human Nature, Inevitability & Blind Spots (人間性・宿命・皮肉)

| Japanese Proverb | Hiragana & Romaji | Metaphorical Meaning | English Equivalent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **覆水盆に返らず** | ふくすいぼんにかえらず (*fukusui bon ni kaerazu*) | Spilled water cannot return to the tray | **It is no use crying over spilled milk.** Once an action is done or a word is spoken, it cannot be undone. |
| **井の中の蛙大海を知らず**| いのなかのかわずたいかいをしらず (*i no naka no kawazu taikai o shirazu*) | A frog in a well knows nothing of the great ocean | **Narrow-minded provincialism.** A person with limited perspective who assumes their small experience encompasses the entire universe. |
| **能ある鷹は爪を隠す** | のうあるたかはつめをかくす (*nō aru taka wa tsume o kakusu*) | A skilled hawk conceals its sharp talons | **Still waters run deep / A truly talented person does not brag.** True masters remain humble until the critical moment. |
| **馬の耳に念仏** | うまのみみにねんぶつ (*uma no mimi ni nenbutsu*) | Chanting Buddhist prayers into a horse's ear | **Preaching to deaf ears / Pearls before swine.** Offering profound wisdom to someone incapable of appreciating it. |
| **猫に小判** | ねこにこばん (*neko ni koban*) | Giving gold coins (*koban*) to a cat | Identical to *Pearls before swine* — giving valuable things to someone who cannot value them. |
| **豚に真珠** | ぶたにしんじゅ (*buta ni shinju*) | Pearls to a swine | Direct Japanese translation of the biblical idiom. |
| **蛙の子は蛙** | かえるのこはかえる (*kaeru no ko wa kaeru*) | The child of a frog is a frog | **Like father, like son / The apple doesn't fall far from the tree.** |
| **二兎を追う者は一兎をも得ず**| にとをおうものは いっとをもえず (*nito o ou mono wa itto o mo ezu*) | One who chases two rabbits catches neither | **If you run after two hares, you will catch neither.** Pursuing two contradictory goals at once leads to total failure. |

## 4. Societal Conduct & Interpersonal Ethics (社会・処世術)

| Japanese Proverb | Hiragana & Romaji | Cultural Context | Meaning & Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **郷に入っては郷に従え** | ごうにいってはごうにしたがえ (*gō ni itte wa gō ni shitagae*) | When entering a village, obey the village customs | **When in Rome, do as the Romans do.** Respect local etiquette, cultures, and workplace norms when in a new environment. |
| **立つ鳥跡を濁さず** | たつとりあとをにごさず (*tatsu tori ato o nigosazu*) | A bird taking flight does not muddy the pond | **Leave things in immaculate order when departing.** When resigning from a job or moving out of an apartment, leave clean ties without mess. |
| **類は友を呼ぶ** | るいはともをよぶ (*rui wa tomo o yobu*) | Similar kinds summon friends | **Birds of a feather flock together.** People of similar character naturally congregate. |
| **聞くは一時の恥、聞かぬは一生の恥** | きくはいっときのはじ、きかぬはいっしょうのはじ (*kiku wa ittoki no haji, kikanu wa isshō no haji*) | Asking is a moment's shame; not asking is a lifetime's shame | **Better to ask and risk looking foolish for a moment than remain ignorant forever.** Ask questions proactively when learning. |
| **情けは人のためならず**| なさけはひとのためならず (*nasake wa hito no tame narazu*) | Kindness is not merely for the sake of others | **What goes around comes around / Kindness is its own reward.** Doing good to others ultimately circles back to benefit yourself. |
| **良薬は口に苦し** | りょうやくはくちににがし (*ryōyaku wa kuchi ni nigashi*) | Good medicine tastes bitter in the mouth | **Constructive criticism is hard to swallow, but heals.** Candid, stinging feedback is the most beneficial for growth. |
