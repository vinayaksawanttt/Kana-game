# Japanese Emergency, Health, and Medical Vocabulary (緊急時・医療・症状の表現)

When facing emergencies, natural disasters, or sudden medical illnesses in Japan, being able to clearly communicate symptoms, contact first responders, and understand hospital protocols is critical for safety.

---

## 1. Japan's National Emergency Numbers (緊急通報番号)

All emergency numbers in Japan can be dialed free of charge from any smartphone, landline, or public telephone (press the red emergency button on public payphones):

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  • 110: 警察 (Police) ──► Traffic accidents, crime, theft│
│  • 119: 救急・消防    ──► Medical emergencies & fires   │
│  • 118: 海上保安庁   ──► Maritime accidents & coast guard│
│  • 171: 災害用伝言   ──► Disaster emergency voice dial │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> [!WARNING]
> Remember: In Japan, **119** is for **both ambulances and fires**. When the dispatcher answers, they will immediately ask:
> **「火事ですか、救急ですか？」** (*Kaji desu ka, kyūkyū desu ka?* — Is it a fire, or a medical emergency?)
> - Reply: **「救急です！」** (*Kyūkyū desu!* — Medical emergency / ambulance!)
> - Reply: **「火事です！」** (*Kaji desu!* — Fire!)

## 2. Immediate Crisis and Distress Phrases (緊急時の叫び)

| Japanese Phrase | Furigana / Romaji | English Meaning | Situation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **助けて！** | たすけて！ (*Tasukete!*) | Help! | Direct cry for immediate assistance. |
| **警察を呼んでください！** | けいさつをよんでください！ (*Keisatsu o yonde kudasai!*) | Please call the police! | Crime, assault, theft, or hit-and-run. |
| **救急車を呼んでください！** | きゅうきゅうしゃをよんでください！ | Please call an ambulance! | Severe injury, collapse, or heart attack. |
| **火事だ！** | かじだ！ (*Kaji da!*) | Fire! | Discovering a fire. |
| **泥棒！** | どろぼう！ (*Dorobō!*) | Thief! | Theft / pickpocketing. |
| **痴漢です！** | ちかんです！ (*Chikan desu!*) | Molester / G Roper! | Unwanted touching on trains/stations. |
| **事故に遭いました。** | じこにあいました。 (*Jiko ni aimashita.*) | I was involved in an accident. | Traffic collisions. |
| **パスポートを無くしました。** | パスポートをなくしました。 | I lost my passport. | Reporting lost items at a police box (**交番 — *Kōban***). |

## 3. Natural Disaster Vocabulary & Protocol (地震・災害)

Japan lies along the Pacific Ring of Fire and experiences frequent seismic activity. Standard emergency alerts follow strict alert scales:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│              Earthquake Safety Protocol (地震)         │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. 頭を守る (Protect your head with bag or helmet)     │
│ 2. 丈夫な机の下に隠れる (Duck under a sturdy table)    │
│ 3. ドアを開けて避難路を確保する (Open doors before jam)│
│ 4. 揺れが収まるまで待つ (Wait until shaking stops)     │
│ 5. 避難所へ移動する (Evacuate to designated shelter)   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Japanese Term | Furigana / Romaji | Meaning & Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **地震** | じしん (*jishin*) | Earthquake |
| **震度** | しんど (*shindo*) | Japanese seismic intensity scale measuring ground shaking at a location (0 to 7). |
| **津波警報** | つなみけいほう (*tsunami keihō*) | Tsunami Warning (evacuate immediately to high ground!). |
| **余震** | よしん (*yoshin*) | Aftershocks |
| **避難所** | ひなんじょ (*hinanjo*) | Designated evacuation shelter (local public schools, community gymnasiums). |
| **非常口** | ひじょうぐち (*hijōguchi*) | Emergency Exit |
| **警戒レベル** | けいかいレベル (*keikai reberu*) | Alert Level 1 to 5 (Level 4: Evacuate; Level 5: Life-threatening disaster underway). |

## 4. Hospital & Clinic Departments (病院の診療科目)

In Japan, primary medical care is handled by specialized local clinics (**医院・クリニック — *iin / kurinikku***), while major university hospitals (**総合病院 — *sōgō byōin***) require referrals or extra fees:

| Department | Kanji & Furigana | Conditions Treated |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Internal Medicine** | **内科** (*naika*) | Fevers, colds, stomach aches, flu, high blood pressure. |
| **Surgery** | **外科** (*geka*) | Cuts, physical wounds, fractures, operations. |
| **Orthopedics** | **整形外科** (*seikei geka*) | Bone breaks, sprains, joint pain, back injuries. |
| **Ophthalmology** | **眼科** (*ganka*) | Eye infections, vision issues, conjunctivitis. |
| **ENT (Ear, Nose, Throat)** | **耳鼻科 / 耳鼻咽喉科** (*jibika*) | Sinusitis, earaches, throat inflammation, seasonal allergies (*kafunshō*). |
| **Dermatology** | **皮膚科** (*hifuka*) | Rashes, eczema, hives, severe burns, bug bites. |
| **Dentistry** | **歯科** (*shika*) | Cavities, broken teeth, toothaches. |
| **Pediatrics** | **小児科** (*shōnika*) | Infant and child medical care. |

### Hospital Paperwork Keywords
- **保険証 (*Hokenshō*)**: National Health Insurance card (foreigners must present passport + insurance policy).
- **問診票 (*Monshinkyō*)**: Medical questionnaire filled out in the waiting room regarding symptoms, medical history, and allergies.
- **処方箋 (*Shohōsen*)**: Prescription paper taken to an independent dispensing pharmacy (**調剤薬局 — *chōzai yakkyoku***) nearby.

## 5. Communicating Symptoms (症状の伝え方)

Use the structural frame: **[Body Part] + が痛い (*...ga itai* — my ... hurts)**:
- **頭が痛い** (*atama ga itai* — headache)
- **喉が痛い** (*nodo ga itai* — sore throat)
- **胃が痛い / お腹が痛い** (*i ga itai / o-naka ga itai* — stomachache)
- **歯が痛い** (*ha ga itai* — toothache)

| Symptom Phrase | Romaji | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **熱があります（38.5度あります）。** | *Netsu ga arimasu.* | I have a fever (I have a 38.5°C fever). |
| **咳と痰が出ます。** | *Seki to tan ga demasu.* | I have a cough and phlegm. |
| **鼻水とくしゃみが出ます。** | *Hanamizu to kushami ga demasu.* | I have a runny nose and sneezing. |
| **吐き気がします / 吐きました。** | *Hakike ga shimasu / hakimashita.* | I feel nauseous / I threw up. |
| **下痢をしています。** | *Geri o shite imasu.* | I have diarrhea. |
| **めまいがします。** | *Memai ga shimasu.* | I feel dizzy / the room is spinning. |
| **寒気がします。** | *Samuke ga shimasu.* | I have the chills / shivering. |
| **息苦しいです。** | *Ikigurushii desu.* | I have difficulty breathing / shortness of breath. |
| **ペニシリンにアレルギーがあります。** | *Penishirin ni arerugī ga arimasu.* | I am allergic to penicillin. |
| **妊娠しています。** | *Ninshin shite imasu.* | I am pregnant. |

## 6. Pain Onomatopoeia: Explaining Pain to Japanese Doctors

Japanese medicine relies heavily on onomatopoeia to pinpoint the exact physiological sensation of pain:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             Pain and Sensation Onomatopoeia            │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ズキズキ (Zuki-zuki):   Throbbing / Pulsing pain       │
│ キリキリ (Kiri-kiri):   Sharp, stabbing stomach cramp  │
│ チクチク (Chiku-chiku): Prickly / Needling sting        │
│ ヒリヒリ (Hiri-hiri):   Burning sting (sunburn, scrape)│
│ ガンガン (Gan-gan):     Splitting, pounding headache   │
│ ムカムカ (Muka-muka):   Queasy / Nauseous stomach      │
│ フラフラ (Fura-fura):   Dizzy, unsteady on your feet   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Doctor Examination Dialogue:
- **医師**: *「どのような痛みですか？」* (*Dono yō na itami desu ka?* — What kind of pain is it?)
- **患者**: *「頭が**ズキズキ**痛みます。」* (*Atama ga zuki-zuki itamimasu.* — My head has a throbbing, pulsing pain.)
- **患者**: *「胃が**キリキリ**して、胸が**ムカムカ**します。」* (*I ga kiri-kiri shite, mune ga muka-muka shimasu.* — My stomach has sharp cramps and I feel queasy.)
