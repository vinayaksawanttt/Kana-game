# Giving and Receiving Verbs

Japanese distinguishes not only the direction of a gift but also the speaker's point of view. The same event can be described with **あげる**, **くれる**, or **もらう**.

## 1. The three basic viewpoints

| Verb | Subject / viewpoint | Typical pattern | Core meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| あげる | The giver | Giver は receiver に thing を あげる | give, usually away from the speaker's side |
| くれる | The giver; recipient is speaker or speaker's in-group | Giver は (me/us) に thing を くれる | give to me/us |
| もらう | The receiver | Receiver は giver に / から thing を もらう | receive, get |

### あげる: giving away from the speaker's side

> 私は友達に本をあげました。  
> I gave a book to my friend.

あげる can also describe someone giving to another person when the speaker does not frame the recipient as “me/us”:

> 田中さんは山田さんに花をあげました。  
> Tanaka gave flowers to Yamada.

### くれる: someone gives to me or my in-group

> 友達が私に本をくれました。  
> My friend gave me a book.

The recipient is often omitted when it is understood: 友達が本をくれました (My friend gave me a book).

### もらう: the receiver's perspective

> 私は友達に本をもらいました。  
> I received a book from my friend.

The giver can be marked with **に** or **から**. から is especially natural when emphasizing the source: 先生から手紙をもらいました (I got a letter from my teacher).

## 2. Giving and receiving actions: ～てあげる, ～てくれる, ～てもらう

Attach these verbs to another verb's て-form to describe doing an action as a favor or receiving someone's help:

| Pattern | Viewpoint | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ～てあげる | I/someone does something for another person | 妹に宿題を教えてあげました。 |
| ～てくれる | Someone does something for me/us | 友達が駅まで送ってくれました。 |
| ～てもらう | I/we receive the benefit of someone's action | 友達に駅まで送ってもらいました。 |

> 先生が説明してくれました。  
> The teacher explained it for me/us.
>
> 先生に説明してもらいました。  
> I had the teacher explain it for me.

The person who performs the action is commonly marked by **に** with ～てもらう. In ～てくれる, that person is usually the subject marked by **が** or **は**.

### Courtesy and social nuance

～てあげる can sound as if the speaker is emphasizing their own kindness, especially when speaking to a superior or someone who has not requested help. In those situations, describe the action directly or choose a more considerate expression. ～てくれる and ～てもらう also express a benefit, so they are not neutral replacements for every action.

## 3. Polite, honorific, and humble counterparts

| Basic form | Polite/honorific/humble counterpart | Direction and use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| あげる | 差し上げる (*sashiageru*) | Humble giving to a superior or valued recipient |
| くれる | くださる (*kudasaru*) | Honorific: a superior gives to me/us |
| もらう | いただく (*itadaku*) | Humble: receive from a superior |

Examples:

- 先生に資料を差し上げました。 — I gave the materials to the teacher (humble).
- 先生が資料をくださいました。 — The teacher gave me/us the materials (honorific).
- 先生に資料をいただきました。 — I received the materials from the teacher (humble).

The polite forms are **差し上げます**, **くださいます**, and **いただきます**. Note the special conjugation **くださいます** (not *くださります*).

### Honorific and humble action-giving forms

- **～てくださる**: a respected person does something for me/us.  
  先生が作文を直してくださいました。 — The teacher kindly corrected my essay.
- **～ていただく**: I humbly receive someone's action.  
  先生に作文を直していただきました。 — I had my essay corrected by the teacher.
- **～て差し上げる**: I humbly do something for a superior. Use with care; it may still sound as though you are announcing a favor.  
  お荷物をお持ちして差し上げます can sound presumptuous; offer help with お持ちしましょうか instead.

## 4. A reliable choice sequence

1. Is the receiver the speaker or the speaker's in-group? Consider **くれる** if the giver is the subject, or **もらう** if the receiver is the subject.
2. Is the gift moving away from the speaker's side? **あげる** is the basic choice.
3. Is the recipient socially superior? Use an appropriate honorific or humble form rather than assuming the basic form fits.
4. For an action, put the action in て-form before the giving/receiving verb.

> **Culture note:** These verbs encode a social viewpoint, but they do not replace the ordinary rules of politeness. Choose both the correct direction and the appropriate level of respect.
