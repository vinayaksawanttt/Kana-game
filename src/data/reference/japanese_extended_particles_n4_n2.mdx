# Japanese Extended Particles (取り立て助詞 / Toritate Joshi)

Beyond the basic case-marking particles (**は, が, を, に, で**), Japanese relies heavily on **focal and limiting particles (取り立て助詞 / *toritate-joshi*)**. These particles do not merely show grammatical roles—they impart subtle speaker perspectives: boundaries, exclusivity, comparative baselines, subjective insufficiency, extreme examples, and emphatic resolve.

---

## Master Overview Matrix

| Particle | Level | Core Meaning | Polarity / Ending | Nuance & Function |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **から** | N5–N4 | From (starting point / origin) | Any | Marks spatial, temporal, or material start |
| **まで** | N5–N4 | Until / Up to (boundary limit) | Any | Marks the terminating boundary of an action |
| **より** | N5–N4 | Than (comparative baseline) | Any | Establishes the standard against which comparison is made |
| **だけ** | N5–N4 | Only / Just (objective boundary) | Affirmative or Negative | Neutral, objective limitation ("no more than this") |
| **しか** | N4 | Only / Nothing but (insufficient) | **Always Negative (〜ない)** | Subjective scarcity, dissatisfaction ("regrettably only") |
| **ばかり** | N3 | Nothing but / Just recently | Any | Preponderance, excessive repetition, or recent completion |
| **さえ** | N3–N2 | Even / If only | Any | Highlights an extreme example to imply all others |
| **こそ** | N3–N2 | Precisely / The very / For sure | Affirmative | Strong deliberate spotlighting or emphatic resolve |

## 1. Boundaries: から (*from*) and まで (*until / to*)

### から (*Origin / Starting Point*)
Marks where or when an action, state, or journey begins:
- **時間**: 授業は9時**から**始まります。 (*Class starts from 9:00.*)
- **場所**: 東京**から**大阪まで新幹線で行きます。 (*I go from Tokyo to Osaka by Shinkansen.*)
- **原料 / 材料**: 酒は米**から**作られます。 (*Sake is made from rice.*)

### まで (*Terminating Boundary*)
Marks the endpoint up to which an ongoing action or condition continues:
- 会議は5時**まで**続きました。 (*The meeting continued until 5:00.*)
- 駅**まで**歩きましょう。 (*Let's walk as far as the station.*)

### Critical Distinction: まで vs. までに
- **まで** (Until): Action continues continuously until the deadline.
  - 5時**まで**勉強します。 (*I will study continuously until 5:00.*)
- **までに** (By / Before): Action must be finished at or before a single cutoff point.
  - 5時**までに**レポートを出してください。 (*Please submit the report by [no later than] 5:00.*)

## 2. Comparative Baseline: より (*than*)

Used to establish the benchmark in comparison structures:

```
[A] は [B] より [Adjective] です。  ──►  A is more [Adjective] than B.
```

- 新幹線は飛行機**より**安いです。  
  *Shinkansen wa hikōki yori yasui desu.*  
  (The Shinkansen is cheaper **than** an airplane.)
- 言うは行う**より**易し。  
  *Iu wa okonau yori yasashi.*  
  (Saying is easier **than** doing. [Proverb])

### Adverbial Use in Formal Japanese (N3–N2)
When placed directly before an adjective or adverb without a comparison noun, **より** means "even more / further":
- **より良い**社会を目指します。 (*We aim for an even better society.*)
- **より速く**走る。 (*To run even faster.*)

## 3. Limits & Exclusivity: だけ vs. しか

Both translate to "only" in English, but their grammar and psychological subtext are fundamentally different:

### だけ (*Neutral, Objective Limitation*)
- Can be paired with **affirmative** or **negative** predicates.
- Purely factual: states an exact quantity with zero emotional judgment.
- Replaces **を** and **が**, but can stack on top of **に**, **で**, **から** (e.g., だけに, だけでは).

### しか (*Negative, Subjective Scarcity*)
- **Must strictly pair with a negative verb (〜ない / 〜ません)**.
- Expresses that the quantity is **insufficient, regrettable, or surprisingly low**.
- Directly absorbs **が** and **を** (e.g., 水しか飲まない, never \*水をしか).

### Side-by-Side Comparison

| Context | Japanese Sentence | Speaker's Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **だけ** (Neutral) | 千円**だけ**あります。<br/>*Sen-en dake arimasu.* | "I have 1,000 yen (an objective fact; no complaint)." |
| **しか** (Scarcity) | 千円**しか**ありません。<br/>*Sen-en shika arimasen.* | "I **only** have 1,000 yen (and it's not enough; I wish I had more)." |

- 日曜日**だけ**休みます。 (*I take only Sunday off [as a matter of schedule].*)
- 日曜日**しか**休めません。 (*I can only rest on Sunday [and I am overworked].*)

## 4. Preponderance & Recency: ばかり (*bakari*)

**ばかり** has three primary structural uses across N4 to N3:

### 1. Noun + ばかり ("Nothing but / Only")
Emphasizes an excessive, lopsided amount of something (often accompanied by slight disapproval):
- 彼は肉**ばかり**食べて、野菜を全然食べない。  
  *Kare wa niku bakari tabete, yasai o zenzen tabenai.*  
  (He eats nothing but meat and eats no vegetables at all.)

### 2. Verb て-form + ばかりいる ("Doing nothing but...")
Expresses that someone does the same action over and over without doing anything else productive:
- 弟は勉強しないで、ゲームをし**てばかりいる**。  
  *Otōto wa benkyō shinaide, gēmu o shite bakari iru.*  
  (My younger brother doesn't study and does nothing but play games.)

### 3. Verb た-form + ばかり ("Just recently completed")
Indicates an action was completed very recently according to the speaker's subjective perception:
- 先週、日本に来**たばかり**です。  
  *Senshū, Nihon ni kita bakari desu.*  
  (I just arrived in Japan last week.)

> [!NOTE]
> Contrast **〜たばかり** with **〜たところ**:
> - **たところ**: Objectively immediate (seconds or minutes ago: *just this second*).
> - **たばかり**: Subjectively fresh, even if days, weeks, or months have elapsed.

## 5. Extreme Case Emphasis: さえ (*sae*)

**さえ** highlights an extreme, unexpected example to imply that all ordinary cases naturally apply:

### 1. "Even X" (Extreme Example)
- この漢字は日本人で**さえ**読めない。  
  *Kono kanji wa Nihonjin de sae yomenai.*  
  (Even Japanese people cannot read this kanji. [Implication: It's natural you can't either.])
- 忙しすぎて、昼ごはんを食べる時間**さえ**なかった。  
  *Isogashisugite, hirugohan o taberu jikan sae nakatta.*  
  (I was so busy I didn't even have time to eat lunch.)

### 2. Essential Condition: 〜さえ〜ば ("If only / As long as...")
Highlights the single necessary condition that guarantees a result:

```
[Noun] + さえ + [Verb ば-form]
[Verb stem] + さえ + すれば
```

- 体**さえ**健康なら**ば**、何でもできる。  
  *Karada sae kenkō naraba, nan demo dekiru.*  
  (As long as one's body is healthy, one can achieve anything.)
- 薬を飲み**さえ**すれば、治ります。  
  *Kusuri o nomi sae sureba, naorimasu.*  
  (If you just take the medicine, you will recover.)

---

## 6. Purposeful Focusing: こそ (*koso*)

**こそ** shines an intense, deliberate spotlight on a specific element to emphasize firm resolve, singular cause, or mutual courtesy.

### 1. Resolute Focus ("This very... / For sure!")
- 今年**こそ**JLPT N2に合格するぞ！  
  *Kotoshi koso JLPT N2 ni gōkaku suru zo!*  
  (This year, for sure, I will pass the JLPT N2!)
- これ**こそ**私が長年探していた本だ。  
  *Kore koso watashi ga naganen sagashite ita hon da.*  
  (This is the very book I have been searching for all these years.)

### 2. Singular Causality: 〜からこそ ("Precisely because...")
Rejects accidental correlation to state the true, singular underlying reason:
- あなたのことを心配している**からこそ**、注意するのです。  
  *Anata no koto o shinpai shite iru kara koso, chūi suru no desu.*  
  (It is precisely because I care about you that I am warning you.)

### 3. Reciprocal Politeness
- こちら**こそ**、よろしくお願いします。  
  *Kochira koso, yoroshiku onegai shimasu.*  
  (It is I [rather than you] who should ask for your favor!)

---

## Summary Differentiation: だけ vs. しか vs. ばかり vs. さえ vs. こそ

```
Only coffee (neutral fact):           コーヒーだけ飲む (drinks only coffee)
Only coffee (sadly, no tea/food):     コーヒーしか飲まない (drinks nothing but coffee)
Constantly drinking coffee (excess):  コーヒーばかり飲んでいる (does nothing but drink coffee)
Even coffee (refusing all food/drink):コーヒーさえ飲めない (can't even drink coffee)
Precisely coffee (not tea or water):  コーヒーこそ飲むべきだ (coffee is the very thing to drink)
```
