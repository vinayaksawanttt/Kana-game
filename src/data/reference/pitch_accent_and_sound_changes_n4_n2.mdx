# Pitch Accent Basics & Morphophonemic Sound Changes (高低アクセント・音便・連濁)

To achieve natural fluency and accurate comprehension at the intermediate and advanced levels (JLPT N3–N1), learners must understand the underlying phonological machinery of Japanese:
1. **Pitch Accent (高低アクセント / *kōtei akusento*)**: Musical pitch contours across morae.
2. **Morphophonemic Sound Changes (連濁・促音化・音便)**: Systematic shifts that occur when morphemes and kanji compound together.

---

## 1. Pitch Accent Basics (高低アクセント)

Unlike English (which uses **stress accent**—making a vowel louder, longer, and higher in pitch) or Mandarin Chinese (which applies tonal contours to every isolated syllable), Japanese uses **pitch accent**.

Every mora in standard Tokyo Japanese is perceived as either **High (H)** or **Low (L)** pitch, governed by two inviolable phonological axioms:

> [!IMPORTANT] The Two Fundamental Rules of Tokyo Pitch Accent
> 1. **Rule 1 (The Initial Step Change)**: The pitch of the 1st and 2nd morae must **always differ** (either Low-High `L-H` or High-Low `H-L`). They can never both be Low or both be High.
> 2. **Rule 2 (No Resurrected Drop)**: Once pitch drops from High to Low within a word or phrase, it **can never rise again** within that same phonological unit.

---

## 2. The Four Tokyo Pitch Accent Patterns

Illustrated with a 3-mora word followed by the subject particle **が (*ga*)**:

```
1. 平板 (Heiban - Flat)       :  L ── H ── H ── [H]  (Particle stays HIGH)
2. 頭高 (Atamadaka - Head)    :  H ── L ── L ── [L]  (Drop happens after 1st mora)
3. 中高 (Nakadaka - Middle)   :  L ── H ── L ── [L]  (Drop happens inside the word)
4. 尾高 (Odaka - Tail)        :  L ── H ── H ── [L]  (Particle drops to LOW!)
```

| Pattern Name | Kernel (Downstep) | Pitch Contour (+ が) | Example Word | Reading & Meaning | Pitch Pattern |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. 平板 (Heiban)** | None (0) | `L - H - H - (H)` | **桜** (さくら) | *sakura* (Cherry blossom) | さ(L) く(H) ら(H) が(H) |
| **2. 頭高 (Atamadaka)** | 1st mora [1] | `H - L - L - (L)` | **命** (いのち) | *inochi* (Life) | い(H) の(L) ち(L) が(L) |
| **3. 中高 (Nakadaka)** | Middle [2 or 3] | `L - H - L - (L)` | **心** (こころ) | *kokoro* (Heart/Mind) | こ(L) こ(H) ろ(L) が(L) |
| **4. 尾高 (Odaka)** | Final mora [N] | `L - H - H - (L)` | **男** (おとこ) | *otoko* (Man) | お(L) と(H) こ(H) **が(L)** |

> [!WARNING] The Critical Distinction: Heiban vs. Odaka
> When spoken in isolation, **平板 (Heiban)** and **尾高 (Odaka)** sound completely identical (both sound like Low $\rightarrow$ High).  
> The difference **only appears when a particle (が, を, に, の) is attached**:
> - Heiban: **ともだち** が (*to-mo-da-chi ga* — particle stays High: `L-H-H-H-H`)
> - Odaka: **いもうと** が (*i-mō-to ga* — particle falls Low: `L-H-H-H-L`)

## 4. Morphophonemic Sound Changes (音便・連濁)

When words, kanji, or morphemes combine into compounds, predictable phonetic rules smooth out articulation.

### A. Rendaku (連濁 / Sequential Voicing)
When two native words (**kun'yomi**) form a compound, the initial unvoiced consonant of the **second word** voices:
- $k \rightarrow g \qquad s \rightarrow z \qquad t \rightarrow d \qquad h \rightarrow b$
- 例: 手 (*te* - hand) + 紙 (*kami* - paper) $\rightarrow$ **手紙 (てがみ / *tegami*)**
- 例: 星 (*hoshi* - star) + 空 (*sora* - sky) $\rightarrow$ **星空 (ほしぞら / *hoshizora*)**
- 例: 人 (*hito* - person) + 人 (*hito*) $\rightarrow$ **人々 (ひとびと / *hitobito*)**

#### The 3 Rules That Block Rendaku (JLPT Intermediate/Advanced):
1. **Lyman's Law (ライマンの法則)**: If the second element **already contains a voiced obstruent ($g, z, d, b$) anywhere**, Rendaku is blocked!
   - 例: 春 (*haru*) + 風 (**かぜ** / *kaze* — already has *z*) $\rightarrow$ **春風 (はるかぜ / *harukaze*, NOT \*harugaze)**.
   - 例: 大 (*ō*) + トカゲ (*tokage* — already has *g*) $\rightarrow$ **大トカゲ (おおとかげ / *ōtokage*)**.
2. **Coordinate Compounds (並列複合語 / Dvandva)**: When the compound means "A **and** B" rather than "A modifies B", Rendaku is blocked:
   - 例: **山川 (やまかわ / *yamakawa*)** = "mountains and rivers" (No Rendaku).
   - Compare with **山川 (やまがわ / *yamagawa*)** = "a mountain river" (Rendaku applied).
3. **Foreign Loanwords (*Gairaigo*) & Onomatopoeia**: Generally resist Rendaku:
   - 例: ガラス + コップ $\rightarrow$ ガラスコップ (*garasu koppu*, NOT \*goppu).

### B. Consonant Gemination (促音化 / Sokuonka) in Kanji Compounds
When two Sino-Japanese (**on'yomi**) kanji combine, if Kanji 1 ends in **つ (*tsu*)** or **く (*ku*)** and Kanji 2 starts with an unvoiced consonant (**k, s, t, h**):
1. The ending shrinks into a **small っ (geminate pause)**.
2. If Kanji 2 starts with **H**, it mutates into a **P (Handakuten)**!

| First Kanji | + Second Kanji | Resulting Compound | Phonological Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 学 (**がく** / *gaku*) | + 校 (**こう** / *kō*) | **学校 (がっこう / *gakkō*)** | く + K $\rightarrow$ **っK** |
| 一 (**いち** / *ichi*) | + 歳 (**さい** / *sai*) | **一歳 (いっさい / *issai*)** | ち + S $\rightarrow$ **っS** |
| 出 (**しゅつ** / *shutsu*) | + 発 (**はつ** / *hatsu*) | **出発 (しゅっぱつ / *shuppatsu*)** | つ + H $\rightarrow$ **っP** |
| 日 (**にち** / *nichi*) | + 本 (**ほん** / *hon*) | **日本 (にっぽん / *nippon*)** | ち + H $\rightarrow$ **っP** |

### C. Verb Conjugation Euphonic Shifts (音便 / Onbin)
In classical Japanese verb morphology, Godan (Group 1) verbs underwent 4 historical euphony changes before **〜て** and **〜た**:

| Onbin Type | Description | Godan Endings | Example Conjugation |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1. イ音便 (I-onbin)** | Drops consonant into **い** | **く, ぐ** | 書く (*kaku*) $\rightarrow$ **書いて (*kaite*)**; 泳ぐ (*oyogu*) $\rightarrow$ **泳いで (*oyoide*)** |
| **2. 促音便 (Sokuonbin)** | Contracts into small **っ** | **う, つ, る** | 買う (*kau*) $\rightarrow$ **買って (*katte*)**; 待つ (*matsu*) $\rightarrow$ **待って (*matte*)**; 帰る $\rightarrow$ **帰って** |
| **3. 撥音便 (Hatsuonbin)** | Contracts into nasal **ん** | **む, ぶ, ぬ** | 飲む (*nomu*) $\rightarrow$ **飲んで (*nonde*)**; 呼ぶ (*yobu*) $\rightarrow$ **呼んで (*yonde*)**; 死ぬ $\rightarrow$ **死んで** |
| **4. ウ音便 (U-onbin)** | Drops consonant into **う** | Classical Adjectives & Keigo | ありがたく $\rightarrow$ **ありがとう**; おはよう $\rightarrow$ **お早う** |
