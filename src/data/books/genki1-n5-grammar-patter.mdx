# Japanese Grammar

## Lesson 1: New Friends (第1課)

### 1. XはYです

Basic equational sentence pattern meaning "X is Y / As for X, it is Y." Japanese speakers frequently omit the topic/subject (`がくせいです。` — "(I) am a student") whenever it is clear from context.

- **Topic particle `は`:** Written with hiragana `は` (`ha`) following classical orthography, but pronounced `wa` when marking a topic (and in `こんにちは`, `こんばんは`).
- **No articles or plurals:** Japanese nouns stand alone without "a/an/the" or plural `-s`, so `がくせいです` can mean "am a student" or "are students" depending on context.
- **Ex:** `がくせいです。` — (I) am a student.
- **Ex:** `せんこうはにほんごです。` — (My) major is the Japanese language.
- **Ex:** `わたしはソラ・キムです。` — I am Sora Kim.
- **Ex:** `やましたさんはせんせいです。` — Mr. Yamashita is a teacher.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんはアメリカじんです。` — Mary is an American.

### 2. Question Sentences

Add the question particle `か` at the end of a statement to turn it into a yes/no or question-word question (it is not customary to write a `?` in standard Japanese).

- **Question words:** `なん / なに` (what), `なんじ` (what time), `なんさい` (how old), `なんねんせい` (what year in school), `なんばん` (what number).
- **`なん` vs. `なに`:** Use `なん` immediately before `です` (`なんですか`) or counters like `じ`, `さい`, `ねんせい`, `ばん`; use `なに` before particles or in `なにじん` (what nationality).
- **Ex:** `りゅうがくせいですか。` — (Are you) an international student?
- **Ex:** `せんこうはなんですか。 — （せんこうは）えいごです。` — What is your major? — (My major) is English.
- **Ex:** `いまなんじですか。 — （いま）くじです。` — What time is it now? — It is nine o'clock.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんはなんさいですか。 — じゅうきゅうさいです。` — How old are you, Mary? — I'm nineteen years old.
- **Ex:** `でんわばんごうはなんばんですか。 — 867-5309です。` — What is your telephone number? — It is 867-5309.

### 3. Noun₁ の Noun₂

The particle `の` connects two nouns, where `Noun₂` is the **main idea** and `Noun₁` restricts, specifies, or shows possession (`Takeshi's`). A `Noun₁ の Noun₂` phrase acts like one big noun.

- **Ex:** `たけしさんのでんわばんごう` — Takeshi's phone number
- **Ex:** `だいがくのせんせい` — a college professor
- **Ex:** `にほんごのがくせい` — a student of the Japanese language
- **Ex:** `にほんのだいがく` — a college in Japan
- **Ex:** `たけしさんのおかあさんはこうこうのせんせいです。` — Takeshi's mother is a high school teacher.

## Lesson 2: Shopping (第2課)

### 1. これ / それ / あれ / どれ

Standalone demonstrative pronouns ("this one / that one / that one over there / which one?") used directly before `は` or `が`.

- **Distance:** `これ` (near speaker), `それ` (near listener), `あれ` (far from both), `どれ` (which one).
- **Question-word particle rule:** `どれ` cannot be followed by `は`; always use `が` (`どれですか` / `どれがあなたのペンですか`).
- **Politeness rule:** Do not use `これ / それ / あれ` to introduce people within earshot (`*これはメアリーさんです`).
- **Ex:** `これはいくらですか。 — それはさんぜんえんです。` — How much is this? — That is 3,000 yen.
- **Ex:** `あれはわたしのペンです。` — That one over there is my pen.
- **Ex:** `どれですか。` — Which one is it (that you are talking about)?

### 2. この / その / あの / どの + Noun

Demonstrative modifiers ("this / that / that... over there / which [Noun]") that **must** be followed by a noun before connecting to `は` or `が`. Unlike `これ/それ/あれ`, `この/その/あの` can politely refer to people (`あのがくせい`).

- **Question-word rule:** `どの + Noun` also requires `が`, never `は` (`どのがくせいがにほんじんですか`).
- **Ex:** `このとけいはいくらですか。` — How much is this watch?
- **Ex:** `そのとけいはさんぜんえんです。` — That watch (in your hand) is 3,000 yen.
- **Ex:** `あのがくせいはりゅうがくせいです。` — That student over there is an international student.
- **Ex:** `どのがくせいがにほんじんですか。` — Which student is Japanese?

### 3. ここ / そこ / あそこ / どこ

Place demonstratives: `ここ` (here, near me), `そこ` (there, near you), `あそこ` (over there), `どこ` (where).

- **Ex:** `すみません。ゆうびんきょくはどこですか。 — （ゆうびんきょくは）あそこです。` — Excuse me. Where is the post office? — (The post office is) right over there.

### 4. だれの Noun

Use `だれ` ("who") + `の` (`だれの + Noun`) to ask "whose [Noun]?"

- **Ex:** `これはだれのかばんですか。 — それはソラさんのかばんです。` — Whose bag is this? — That is Sora's bag.

### 5. Noun も

The particle `も` ("too / also") replaces `は` directly after the item that shares the same property (`AはXです。BもXです`). It cannot be used when one person has two attributes (e.g., dual citizenship: say `パットさんはアメリカじんでもあります`, not `*パットもアメリカじんです`).

- **Ex:** `たけしさんはにほんじんです。ゆいさんもにほんじんです。` — Takeshi is a Japanese person. Yui is Japanese, too.

### 6. Noun じゃないです

Negate `XはYです` (where `Y` is a noun) by replacing `です` with `じゃないです` (colloquial), `じゃありません` (more conservative), or `ではありません` (formal/written). `です` and `じゃないです` cannot stand alone without a noun (`いいえ、にほんじんじゃないです`, never `*いいえ、じゃないです`).

- **Note:** Adjectives (`たかい`, `おいしい`) cannot be negated with `じゃないです` (taught in Lesson 5).
- **Ex:** `やまださんはがくせいじゃないです。` — Mr. Yamada is not a student.
- **Ex:** `にほんじんですか。 — いいえ、にほんじんじゃないです。` — Are you Japanese? — No, I am not Japanese.

### 7. 〜ね / 〜よ

Sentence-final particles expressing the speaker's stance toward the listener:

- **`〜ね` ("right? / isn't it?"):** Seeks listener confirmation or agreement.
- **`〜よ` ("I tell you / you know"):** Assures the listener of information the speaker is confident about.
- **Ex:** `リーさんのせんこうはぶんがくですね。` — Ms. Lee, your major is literature, right?
- **Ex:** `とんかつはさかなじゃないですよ。` — Let me assure you. "Tonkatsu" is not fish.
- **Ex:** `スミスさんはイギリスじんですよ。` — (In case you're wondering,) Mr. Smith is British.

## Lesson 3: Making a Date (第3課)

### 1. Verb Conjugation (Present Tense Affirmative & Negative)

Japanese verbs are sorted into three conjugation classes: **ru-verbs**, **u-verbs**, and **irregular verbs**. Dictionary forms are used in dictionary entries and casual contexts, while present-tense polite forms replace the ending with `-ます` (affirmative) and `-ません` (negative).

- **Ru-verbs:** Base ends with `e` or `i` + `る` (`食べる` = `tabe + ru`). Drop `る` and add `ます` / `ません` (`食べます` / `食べません`, `寝ます` / `寝ません`, `起きます` / `起きません`, `見ます` / `見ません`).
- **U-verbs:** Consonant-final base + `u` (`飲む` = `nom + u`). Replace `u` with `imasu` / `imasen` (`飲みます`, `読みます`, `話します`, `聞きます`, `行きます`).
- **Trap `-eru` / `-iru` u-verbs:** Verbs ending in `-aru`, `-oru`, or `-uru` are always u-verbs, while some `-eru`/`-iru` verbs like `帰る` (`kaer + u` → `帰ります`, NOT `*帰ます`) are also u-verbs. Memorize each verb as a set (e.g., `行く — 行きます`).
- **Irregular verbs:** `する` → `します / しません`, `勉強する` → `勉強します / 勉強しません`, `くる` → `きます / きません`.

### 2. Verb Types & Present Tense Meaning

Action verbs describing basic human activities express either **(1) habitual/regular actions** or **(2) future actions/plans** when used in the present tense.

- **Ex:** `私はよくテレビを見ます。` — I often watch TV.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんはときどき朝ご飯を食べません。` — Mary sometimes doesn't eat breakfast.
- **Ex:** `私はあした京都に行きます。` — I will go to Kyoto tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `ソラさんは今日うちに帰りません。` — Sora will not return home today.

### 3. Core Particles (`を`, `で`, `に`, `へ`)

Nouns in sentences are followed by particles indicating their grammatical relation to the verb (in casual speech, particles are sometimes dropped).

- **`を` (pronounced *o*):** Marks the direct object directly involved in or affected by the verb.
- **`で`:** Marks the place where an action/event takes place.
- **`に`:** Marks (1) the goal toward which things move (with verbs like `行く`, `来る`, `帰る`), and (2) the specific time at which an event takes place. For approximate time, substitute `ごろ` or `ごろに` for `に`.
- **`へ` (pronounced *e*):** Marks the goal of movement and can replace directional `に` with `行く`, `来る`, `帰る`, but **cannot** replace `に` for time references.
- **Ex:** `コーヒーを飲みます。` — I drink coffee.
- **Ex:** `図書館で本を読みます。` — I will read books in the library.
- **Ex:** `今日学校に行きません。` — I will not go to school today.
- **Ex:** `十一時ごろ／十一時ごろに寝ます。` — I will go to bed at about eleven.
- **Ex:** `うちへ帰ります。` — I will return home.

### 4. Time Reference Rules with `に`

Whether a time expression takes the particle `に` depends on the category of the time word.

- **Requires `に`:** Days of the week (`日曜日に`) and numerical time expressions (`十時四十五分に`, `九月に`).
- **Never takes `に`:** Relative time expressions (`今日`, `あした`, `きのう`), regular intervals (`毎日`, `毎晩`), and the question word `いつ`.
- **Optional `に`:** Parts of the day (`朝(に)`, `夜(に)`) and `週末(に)`, depending on style and emphasis.
- **Ex:** `十時四十五分に起きます。` — I get up at 10:45.
- **Ex:** `毎晩テレビを見ます。` — I watch TV every evening.
- **Ex:** `いつ行きますか。` — When will you go?
- **Ex:** `週末（に）何をしますか。` — What will you do on weekends?

### 5. `〜ませんか` (Extending Invitations)

Use the negative present verb + `か` (`〜ませんか`) to extend an invitation ("Won't you...? / What do you say to...?"). Its affirmative counterpart (`〜ますか`) is only a plain question, not an invitation. Polite refusal often uses `うーん、ちょっと…`.

- **Ex:** `昼ご飯を食べませんか。` — What do you say to having lunch with me?
- **Ex:** `テニスをしませんか。` — Will you play tennis with me?
- **Ex:** `うーん、ちょっと。` — Um, it's slightly (inconvenient for me at this moment).

### 6. Frequency Adverbs

Frequency adverbs describe how often an action occurs. Affirmative adverbs pair with affirmative verbs, while negative-polarity adverbs require a negative verb ending.

- **Affirmative adverbs:** `毎日` (every day), `よく` (often), `ときどき` (sometimes).
- **Negative-requiring adverbs:** `ぜんぜん〜ません` (never; not at all) and `あまり〜ません` (not often; not very much).
- **Ex:** `私はときどき図書館に行きます。` — I sometimes go to a library.
- **Ex:** `私はぜんぜんテレビを見ません。` — I do not watch TV at all.
- **Ex:** `たけしさんはあまり勉強しません。` — Takeshi does not study much.

### 7. Japanese Word Order

Japanese sentences are fairly flexible in element order, as particles mark each noun's role, though the verb always comes at the end.

- **Typical word order:** `[Topic は] + [Time に] + [Frequency] + [Place で] + [Object を] + [Verb]`
- **Ex:** `私は今日図書館で日本語を勉強します。` — I will study Japanese in the library today.
- **Ex:** `私はよく七時ごろうちへ帰ります。` — I often go back home at around seven.

### 8. The Topic Particle `は`

The particle `は` presents the topic of one's utterance ("As for X..."). A topic does not need to be the grammatical subject—time expressions (`週末は`, `今日は`) or broached objects (`晩ご飯は？`) can also be topicalized with `は` to direct the listener's attention.

- **Ex:** `メアリーさん、週末はたいてい何をしますか。` — Mary, what do you usually do on the weekend?
- **Ex:** `今日は京都に行きます。` — I'm going to Kyoto today.
- **Ex:** `晩ご飯は？ — 食べません。` — How about dinner? — I will not eat.

## Lesson 4: The First Date (第4課)

### 1. Existence & Possession (`Xがあります / います`)

Use `あります` (for non-living things, possessions, and scheduled events) and `います` (for people and living, moving things) to express "there is/are X."

- **Location & existence:** `[Place に] + [Thing が あります / Person が います]`. Notice the place takes `に` (not `で`), except when `あります` means an **event taking place** (`京都でお祭りがあります`).
- **Possession:** `[Thing が あります / ありません]` means "to have / not have" (`テレビがありません`). Contrast with `テレビじゃありません` ("It isn't a TV").
- **Colloquial negative of `ある`:** `ないです` (never `*あらないです`).
- **Note on `います/あります` vs. `です`:** `います/あります` describe existence or location (`あそこに留学生がいます`), whereas `です` describes an attribute (`メアリーさんは留学生です`).
- **Ex:** `あそこにマクドナルドがあります。` — There's a McDonald's over there.
- **Ex:** `火曜日にテストがあります。` — There will be an exam on Tuesday.
- **Ex:** `あした京都でお祭りがあります。` — There will be a festival in Kyoto tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `日本人の友だちがいます。` — I have a Japanese friend.

### 2. Describing Where Things Are (Spatial Nouns)

Describe relative locations using `XはYの[location word]です`.

- **Location words:** `みぎ` (right), `ひだり` (left), `まえ` (front / across from), `うしろ` (behind), `うら` (back/hidden side), `なか` (inside), `うえ` (on/above), `した` (under/beneath), `ちかく` / `そば` (near), `となり` (next to, same category side-by-side) vs. `よこ` (beside/by), `XとYのあいだ` (between X and Y).
- **With action verbs:** Follow the location phrase with `で` when an action occurs there (`モスバーガーの前で待ちました`).
- **Ex:** `銀行は図書館のとなりです。` — The bank is next to the library.
- **Ex:** `レストランはスーパーと病院の間です。` — The restaurant is between the supermarket and the hospital.
- **Ex:** `私はモスバーガーの前でメアリーさんを待ちました。` — I waited for Mary in front of the Mos Burger place.

### 3. Past Tense of `です`

The past tense of `XはYです` is `〜でした` (affirmative) and `〜じゃなかったです` (negative; more conservative `〜じゃありませんでした`, formal written `〜ではありませんでした`).

- **Ex:** `山下先生はさくら大学の学生でした。` — Mr. Yamashita was a student at Sakura University.
- **Ex:** `あれは日本の映画じゃなかったです。` — That was not a Japanese movie.

### 4. Past Tense of Verbs

Past-tense polite verbs end in `-ました` (affirmative) and `-ませんでした` (negative). All Lesson 3 conjugation classes (`ru`-verbs, `u`-verbs, irregular verbs) apply identically before `-ました / -ませんでした`.

- **Ex:** `メアリーさんは九時ごろうちに帰りました。` — Mary returned home at about nine.
- **Ex:** `私はきのう日本語を勉強しませんでした。` — I did not study Japanese yesterday.

### 5. The Particle `も` ("Too / Also")

Use `も` when two or more items share the same predicate or activity.

- **Particle replacement rules:** `は`, `が`, and `を` are replaced by `も`; other particles like `に` and `で` are kept and followed by `も` (`にも`, `でも`).
- **Ex:** `山下先生もきのう京都に行きました。` — Professor Yamashita went to Kyoto yesterday, too.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんはかばんも買いました。` — Mary bought a bag, too.
- **Ex:** `大阪にも行きました。` — I went to Osaka, too.
- **Ex:** `カフェでも本を読みました。` — He read a book at a cafe, too.

### 6. Duration of Activity (`〜時間`)

Express the duration of an activity with a bare duration noun (no particle `に`!).

- **Approximate & half-hours:** Add `半` immediately after `〜時間` (`七時間半`), and `ぐらい` at the end for "about" (`一時間半ぐらい`). (Contrast with `ごろ` for clock times.)
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんはそこでたけしさんを一時間待ちました。` — Mary waited for Takeshi there for an hour.
- **Ex:** `私はきのう日本語を三時間ぐらい勉強しました。` — I studied Japanese for about three hours yesterday.
- **Ex:** `きのう七時間半寝ました。` — (I) slept for seven and a half hours last night.

### 7. Quantity Placement (`たくさん`)

Quantity words like `たくさん` ("many / a lot") can appear either **before the noun** or **after the particle** (`を` or `が`).

- **Ex:** `京都で写真をたくさん撮りました。` — I took many pictures in Kyoto.
- **Ex:** `野菜がたくさんあります。` — There are a lot of vegetables.

### 8. The Particle `と`

`と` has two core functions in Lesson 4: **(1)** connecting two nouns ("A and B", nouns only—not verbs or sentences), and **(2)** meaning "together with (a person)". (Note: "with [an instrument]" uses `で`, taught in Lesson 10.)

- **Ex:** `日本語と英語を話します。` — I speak Japanese and English.
- **Ex:** `京都と大阪に行きました。` — I went to Kyoto and Osaka.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんはソラさんと韓国に行きます。` — Mary will go to Korea with Sora.

## Lesson 5: A Trip to Okinawa (第5課)

### 1. Adjectives (Present Tense)

Japanese has two adjective classes: **`い`-adjectives** and **`な`-adjectives**, each with its own conjugation pattern.

- **`い`-adjectives:** Affirmative adds `です` (`さむいです`). Negative replaces final `い` with `くないです` or `くありません` (`さむくないです` — never `*さむいじゃないです`).
- **`な`-adjectives:** Affirmative adds `です` without `な` (`元気です`). Negative replaces `です` with `じゃないです` / `じゃありません` / `ではありません` (`元気じゃないです`).
- **Irregular `いい` (good):** Changes `い` to `よ` in all non-dictionary forms (`いいです` → `よくないです` / `よくありません`, `かっこよくないです`). Note: `かわいい` is regular (`かわいくないです`).
- **Degree adverbs:** Place `すごく` (extremely), `とても` (very), or `ちょっと` (a little) before adjectives.
- **Ex:** `その本はおもしろいですか。 — いいえ、あまりおもしろくないです。` — Is that book interesting? — No, it is not very interesting.
- **Ex:** `沖縄の海はとてもきれいです。` — The sea is very beautiful in Okinawa.
- **Ex:** `この部屋はちょっと暑いです。` — This room is a little hot.

### 2. Adjectives (Past Tense)

- **`い`-adjectives:** Affirmative replaces final `い` with `かったです` (`さむかったです`, never `*さむいでした`). Negative replaces `くないです` with `くなかったです` (`さむくなかったです` / `さむくありませんでした`). Irregular `いい` → `よかったです` / `よくなかったです`.
- **`な`-adjectives:** Affirmative uses `〜でした` (`元気でした`); negative uses `〜じゃなかったです` (`元気じゃなかったです`).
- **Ex:** `テストは難しかったですか。 — いいえ、ぜんぜん難しくなかったです。` — Was the exam difficult? — No, it was not difficult at all.
- **Ex:** `その町はにぎやかでしたか。 — いいえ、にぎやかじゃなかったです。` — Was the town lively? — No, it was not lively.

### 3. Adjectives (Noun Modification)

Both adjective types can directly precede and modify nouns.

- **`い`-adjectives:** Place the dictionary form directly before the noun (`おもしろい映画`).
- **`な`-adjectives:** Restore `な` before the noun (`きれいな写真`).
- **Ex:** `きのう、おもしろい映画を見ました。` — I saw an interesting movie yesterday.
- **Ex:** `京都できれいな写真を撮りました。` — I took a beautiful picture in Kyoto.
- **Ex:** `ここはとてもにぎやかな町です。` — This is a very vibrant city.

### 4. Likes & Dislikes (`好き(な) / きらい(な)`)

`好き(な)` (like) and `きらい(な)` (dislike) are `な`-adjectives taking the pattern `Xは Yが 好き／きらいです`.

- **Intensifiers:** Use `大好きです` (like very much) and `大きらいです` (hate) rather than `とても`.
- **Neutral:** `好きでもきらいでもないです` ("neither like nor dislike").
- **Nuances:** For romantic/familial affection, `〜のことが好き` is used (`メアリーさんのことが好きです`). When contrasting two items, `は` replaces `が` (`野菜は好きですが、肉はきらいです`).
- **Ex:** `ロバートさんは日本語のクラスが好きです。` — Robert likes his Japanese classes.
- **Ex:** `たけしさんはコーヒーが大好きです。` — Takeshi likes coffee a lot.
- **Ex:** `これは私の好きな本です。` — This is my favorite book.

### 5. `〜ましょう / 〜ましょうか` ("Let's... / Shall we...")

Replace `-ます` with `-ましょう` ("Let's...") or `-ましょうか` ("Shall we...?") to suggest a plan of action.

- **Ex:** `一緒に図書館で勉強しましょう。` — Let's study in the library together.
- **Ex:** `あそこでコーヒーを飲みましょうか。` — Shall we drink coffee over there?

### 6. Counting Flat Objects (`〜枚`)

Use specific counters depending on the shape/category of items. Number words typically come **after** the direct object particle (`を`), before the verb. The counter `枚 (まい)` is used for flat objects like T-shirts, paper, and tickets.

- **Ex:** `リーさんはTシャツを三枚買いました。` — Lee bought three T-shirts.

## Lesson 6: A Day in Robert's Life (第6課)

### 1. Verb `て`-form Conjugation

The `て`-form is used for requests, connecting actions, permission, and prohibition.

- **`Ru`-verbs:** Drop `る` + `て` (`食べる` → `食べて`).
- **`U`-verbs (5 sub-rules):**
  - `う / つ / る` → `って` (`会う` → `会って`, `待つ` → `待って`, `とる` → `とって`; includes `-iru/-eru` u-verbs like `帰る`, `入る`, `知る`, `いる`, `切る`, `走る`).
  - `む / ぶ / ぬ` → `んで` (`読む` → `読んで`, `遊ぶ` → `遊んで`, `死ぬ` → `死んで`).
  - `く` → `いて` (`書く` → `書いて`), **Exception:** `行く` → `行って`.
  - `ぐ` → `いで` (`泳ぐ` → `泳いで`).
  - `す` → `して` (`話す` → `話して`).
- **Irregular verbs:** `する` → `して`, `くる` → `きて`. Note that `u`-verb `て`-forms differ completely from their `-ます` stems (`書きます` vs. `書いて`).

### 2. `〜てください` (Polite Requests)

Combine `て`-form + `ください` to make a polite request ("please do..."). With close friends or family, a bare `て`-form (`窓を開けて。`) can be used casually.

- **Ex:** `教科書を読んでください。` — Please read the textbook.
- **Ex:** `すみません。ちょっと教えてください。` — Excuse me. Please teach me a little. (= Tell me, I need your advice.)

### 3. Describing Two or More Activities (`〜て、〜`)

Use the `て`-form to link sequential actions ("did A and then did B"), or to describe the **manner/situation** of the main verb. The tense of the final verb sets the tense for the entire sentence. (Never use `と` to join verbs.)

- **Ex:** `図書館に行って、本を借ります。` — I will go to the library and check out some books.
- **Ex:** `今日は、六時に起きて、勉強しました。` — Today I got up at six and studied.
- **Ex:** `バスに乗って、会社に行きます。` — I go to work by bus.
- **Ex:** `教科書を忘れて、すみません。` — I am sorry for not bringing in the textbook.

### 4. `〜てもいいです` (Permission)

`Verb て-form + もいいです` means "you may do..." (`〜てもいいですか` asks "May I...?"). In casual speech `も` can be dropped (`食べていいです`). Grant permission politely with `はい、いいですよ。／どうぞ。`.

- **Ex:** `教科書を見てもいいですよ。` — You may use the textbook.
- **Ex:** `トイレに行ってもいいですか。 — はい、いいですよ。／どうぞ。` — May I go to the bathroom? — You may. / Please.

### 5. `〜てはいけません` (Prohibition)

`Verb て-form + はいけません` states a strong prohibition ("you must not do..."), common in rules and regulations. (Unlike `〜てもいいです`, `は` cannot be dropped.) Use `〜ないでください` (Lesson 8) for softer refusals unless you are in a position of authority.

- **Ex:** `ここで写真を撮ってはいけません。` — You must not take pictures here.

### 6. `〜から` (Giving Reasons)

A clause ending with `から` ("because") explains the reason or cause of a situation or proposal. The explanation clause can also precede the main clause (explored further in Lesson 9).

- **Ex:** `私は今晩勉強します。あしたテストがありますから。` — I will study this evening. (Because) we will have an exam tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `バスに乗りましょう。タクシーは高いですから。` — Let's go by bus. (Because) taxis are expensive.

### 7. `〜ましょうか` (Offering Assistance)

In addition to "Shall we...?", `〜ましょうか` is used to offer help ("Let me do... / Shall I...?").

- **Ex:** `（私が）やりましょうか。` — I'll do it.
- **Ex:** `荷物を持ちましょうか。` — Shall I carry your bag?

## Lesson 7: Family Picture (第7課)

### 1. `〜ている` (Action in Progress & Habitual/Occupation)

Japanese verbs fall into three semantic classes: **(1) activity verbs** (`食べる`, `読む`), **(2) change verbs** (`死ぬ`, `起きる`), and **(3) continuous state verbs** (`ある`, `いる`, which do not take `〜ている`). With activity verbs, `〜ている` expresses an **action currently in progress**, or a **habitual action / occupation**. (`いる` conjugates as a `ru`-verb, and applies to both living and inanimate subjects.)

- **Ex:** `ソラさんは今勉強しています。` — Sora is studying right now.
- **Ex:** `たけしさんは英語の本を読んでいます。` — Takeshi is reading a book in English.
- **Ex:** `私は英語を教えています。` — I teach English. / I am teaching English (right now).
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんは毎日日本語を勉強しています。` — Mary studies Japanese every day.

### 2. `〜ている` (Result of a Change / Current State)

With **change verbs** (verbs describing instantaneous transitions like `結婚する`, `座る`, `持つ`, `知る`, `太る`, `やせる`, `着る`, `起きる`, `住む`, `行く`, `来る`), `〜ている` describes the **resulting state** that persists now, NOT an action in progress.

- **Important watch-outs:** `中国に行っています` means "is in China (having gone there)", NOT "is going to China." The negative of `知っています` is `知りません` (without `ている`).
- **Ex:** `山下先生は結婚しています。` — Professor Yamashita is married.
- **Ex:** `ゆいさんは窓の近くに座っています。` — Yui is seated near the window.
- **Ex:** `ソラさんはお金をたくさん持っています。` — Sora has a lot of money.
- **Ex:** `山下先生は母を知っています。` — Professor Yamashita knows my mother.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんはTシャツを着ています。` — Mary is wearing/wears a T-shirt.

### 3. Describing Physical Attributes

Use the double-subject pattern `Aさんは [Body Part] が [Adjective] です` to describe physical features, rather than `Aさんの [Body Part] は...`.

- **Idiomatic collocations:** `背が高い` (is tall), `背が低い` (is short), `頭がいい` (is bright/smart).
- **Ex:** `トムさんは髪が長いです。` — Tom has long hair.

### 4. Adjective & Noun `て`-forms for Joining Sentences

Connect multiple adjectives or nouns smoothly in one sentence; the final predicate determines the overall tense.

- **`い`-adjectives:** Replace final `い` with `くて` (`安い` → `安くて`, irregular `いい` → `よくて`).
- **`な`-adjectives & `Noun + です`:** Add `で` (`元気` → `元気で`, `日本人です` → `日本人で`).
- **Ex:** `あの店の食べ物は安くて、おいしいです。` — The food at that restaurant is inexpensive and delicious.
- **Ex:** `ホテルはきれいで、よかったです。` — The hotel was clean, and we were happy.
- **Ex:** `山下先生は日本人で、五十歳ぐらいです。` — Professor Yamashita is a Japanese and he is about fifty years old.

### 5. `[Verb Stem] + に + [行く / 来る / 帰る]` (Purpose of Movement)

Express the purpose of going/coming/returning using the verb stem (the part before `-ます`: `食べ`, `読み`) + `に` + movement verb.

- **Ex:** `デパートにかばんを買いに行きました。` — I went to a department store to buy a bag.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんは日本に日本語を勉強しに来ました。` — Mary has come to Japan to study Japanese.

### 6. Counting People (`〜人`)

The counter for people is `人 (にん)`, with irregular readings for 1 and 2 people: `ひとり` (一人), `ふたり` (二人), `さんにん` (三人), `よにん` (四人), `ごにん` (五人), `ろくにん` (六人), `しちにん / ななにん` (七人), `はちにん` (八人), `きゅうにん` (九人), `じゅうにん` (十人), `なんにん` (何人).

## Lesson 8: Barbecue (第8課)

### 1. Short Forms (Present Tense Affirmative & Negative)

Short forms (plain/informal forms) are used among close friends/family and inside embedded clauses (`〜と思います`, `〜と言っていました`, `〜のが好き`).

- **Present Affirmative:** Verbs = dictionary form (`読む`); `い`-adj = drop `です` (`かわいい`); `な`-adj & Nouns = replace `です` with `だ` (`静かだ`, `学生だ`).
- **Present Negative:**
  - `Ru`-verbs: drop `る` + `ない` (`食べる` → `食べない`).
  - `U`-verbs: change final `-u` to `-anai` (`書く` → `書かない`). **Verbs ending in `う` take `わ`** (`会う` → `会わない`, `買う` → `買わない`).
  - Irregular: `する` → `しない`, `くる` → `こない`, `ある` → `ない` (exception!).
  - `い`-adj: drop `です` (`かわいくない`, `いい` → `よくない`).
  - `な`-adj & Nouns: drop `です` (`静かじゃない`, `学生じゃない`).

### 2. Short Forms in Informal Speech

Close friends and family use short forms to show intimacy, while long forms maintain polite distance. Never use short forms toward social superiors (e.g., professors) even if they use short forms with you.

- **Casual rules:** Drop question particle `か` and use rising intonation (`今日、学校に行く？`); drop sentence-final `だ` after nouns/`な`-adjectives unless followed by `ね` or `よ` (`メアリーさんは二年生だよ`); use `うん` / `ううん` instead of `はい` / `いいえ`; particles are often dropped.
- **Ex:** `今日、学校に行く？ — ううん、行かない。` — Are you going to school today? — No, I'm not.
- **Ex:** `どんな音楽を聞く？` — What kind of music do you listen to?

### 3. Quoted Thoughts (`[Short Form] + と思います`)

Use `Short Form + と思います` to express opinions ("I think that..."). For negative opinions, put the negation **inside** the quote (`〜ないと思います`) rather than negating `思います`.

- **Ex:** `（私は）たけしさんはメアリーさんが好きだと思います。` — I think Takeshi likes Mary.
- **Ex:** `（私は）メアリーさんはたけしさんが好きじゃないと思います。` — I don't think Mary likes Takeshi.

### 4. Quoted Speech (`[Short Form] + と言っていました`)

Report what someone else said using `Short Form + と言っていました` ("They said..."). Japanese preserves the original tense of the speaker's utterance inside the quote (`あした試験があると言っていました` rather than shifting to past).

- **Ex:** `ヤスミンさんは、あした試験があると言っていました。` — Yasmin said that there would be an exam tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `山下先生は結婚していないと言っていました。` — Professor Yamashita said that he is not married.

### 5. `〜ないでください` ("Please don't...")

Attach `でください` to the negative short form of a verb to politely request someone not to do something. Prefer `〜ないでください` over `〜てはいけません` when declining a permission request (`〜てもいいですか`).

- **Ex:** `ここで写真を撮らないでください。` — Please don't take pictures here.
- **Ex:** `この部屋に入ってもいいですか。 — 入らないでください。` — May I enter this room? — Please don't.

### 6. Nominalizing Verbs (`Verb [Short Form] + のが好き / きらい / 上手 / 下手です`)

Adding `の` to a verb's short form turns the action into a noun phrase ("doing..."). Never use the `て`-form before `のが`. Alternatives for skill: `〜がとくいです` (comfortable/good at) and `〜がにがてです` (uncomfortable/poor at).

- **Ex:** `（私は）日本語を勉強するのが好きです。` — I like studying the Japanese language.
- **Ex:** `ロバートさんは料理を作るのが上手です。` — Robert is good at cooking meals.
- **Ex:** `たけしさんは英語を話すのが下手です。` — Takeshi is not a good speaker of English.

### 7. The Subject Particle `が` (Identification & Question Words)

While `は` marks an already-established topic, `が` spotlights the **new or emphasized subject** (answering "who?" or "which?"). Question words like `だれ`, `何`, and `どの〜` in subject position **always** take `が` (never `は`), as does the noun answering them.

- **Ex:** `ロバートさんが沖縄に行きました。` — ROBERT went to Okinawa.
- **Ex:** `だれが沖縄に行きましたか。` — Who went to Okinawa?
- **Ex:** `どのクラスがおもしろいですか。 — 日本語のクラスがおもしろいです。` — Which class is (the most) interesting? — Japanese class is.

### 8. `何か` ("Something / Anything?") and `何も〜ません` ("Not... anything")

Use `何か` in positive statements and questions, and `何も + negative verb` in negative statements. Particles `は`, `が`, and `を` are usually omitted after `何か` and `何も`.

- **Ex:** `猫が何か持ってきました。` — The cat has brought something.
- **Ex:** `猫は何か食べましたか。` — Did the cat eat anything?
- **Ex:** `いいえ、猫は何も食べませんでした。` — No, the cat did not eat anything.

## Lesson 9: Kabuki (第9課)

### 1. Past Tense Short Forms (`〜た / 〜なかった`)

Only three rules generate all past-tense short forms:

- **Verbs (affirmative):** Replace `て / で` in the `て`-form with `た / だ` (`食べて` → `食べた`, `読んで` → `読んだ`).
- **`い`-adjectives (affirmative):** Drop `です` from the past long form (`かわいかった`).
- **`な`-adjectives & Nouns (affirmative):** Replace `でした` with `だった` (`静かだった`, `学生だった`).
- **All categories (negative):** Replace final `い` in the present negative short form (`〜ない`) with `かった` (`読まなかった`, `かわいくなかった`, `静かじゃなかった`, `学生じゃなかった`).

### 2. Past Tense Short Forms in Informal Speech

Use past short forms directly in casual conversation. Unlike present-tense `だ`, past-tense `だった` is **not** dropped at the end of casual sentences.

- **Ex:** `けさ、朝ご飯を食べた？ — うん、食べた。／ううん、食べなかった。` — Did you have breakfast this morning? — Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.
- **Ex:** `先週は忙しかった？ — ううん、忙しくなかった。ひまだった。` — Were you busy last week? — No, I wasn't. I had free time.

### 3. Past Tense Quoted Thoughts (`[Past Short Form] + と思います`)

Report thoughts about past events using past short forms before `と思います`.

- **Ex:** `トムさんが食べたと思います。` — I think Tom ate it.
- **Ex:** `去年の冬はあまり寒くなかったと思います。` — I don't think last winter was very cold.

### 4. Past Tense Quoted Speech (`[Past Short Form] + と言っていました`)

Report past-tense utterances using past short forms before `と言っていました`. Remember there is no "sequence of tense" shift: if the original speaker used present tense (`晩ご飯を食べています`), keep present tense inside the quote (`ヤスミンさんは晩ご飯を食べていると言っていました` — "Yasmin said that she was having dinner").

- **Ex:** `ヤスミンさんは、昼ご飯を食べなかったと言っていました。` — Yasmin said that she didn't have lunch.

### 5. Qualifying Nouns with Verb & Adjective Clauses (Relative Clauses)

Adjectives and short-form verb clauses can directly precede a noun to qualify/describe it ("a person who..."). In Lesson 9, `〜ている + Noun` is featured for describing people by their current actions/states.

- **Ex:** `あそこで本を読んでいる人です。` — Yui is the one who is reading a book over there.
- **Ex:** `あそこで写真を撮っている学生はロバートさんです。` — The student taking pictures over there is Robert.

### 6. `もう〜ました` ("Have already...") vs. `まだ〜ていません` ("Have not... yet")

Use `もう + past tense` for completed actions with present relevance ("have already..."), and `まだ + 〜ていません` (NOT past negative!) for actions not yet completed ("have not... yet").

- **Ex:** `もう宿題をしました。` — I have already done the homework.
- **Ex:** `まだ宿題をしていません。` — I have not done the homework yet.

### 7. `[Explanation] から、[Situation]`

Put the `〜から` reason clause first to form a single sentence: `[Explanation] から、[Situation]` ("Because [explanation], [situation]" / "[Explanation]; therefore, [situation]"). Both short and polite forms can precede `から` when the main clause is polite, though polite `〜から` is forbidden if the main clause ends in a short form.

- **Ex:** `あした試験があるから、今晩勉強します。` — I will study this evening, because we will have an exam tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `寒かったから、出かけませんでした。` — We didn't go out, because it was cold.
- **Ex:** `かぶきのチケットがありますから、一緒に見に行きましょう。` — Let's go to see Kabuki. I have tickets.

## Lesson 10: Winter Vacation Plans (第10課)

### 1. Comparison between Two Items (`AのほうがBより〜`)

Compare two items using `Aのほうが Bより [Property]` ("A is more [property] than B"). In speech, `Bより` and `Aのほうが` can switch order—listen for `のほうが` and `より` rather than relying on word order!

- **Question pattern:** `AとBと どちらのほうが／どっちのほうが [Property]ですか` (`どちら` / `どっち` alone are also used; `どっち` is slightly more colloquial).
- **Ex:** `中国のほうが日本より大きいです。` — China is larger than Japan.
- **Ex:** `日本とカナダとどちらのほうが寒いですか。 — カナダのほうが寒いです。` — Which is colder, Japan or Canada? — Canada is colder.

### 2. Superlative Comparison among Three or More Items (`〜の中で X がいちばん〜`)

Compare three or more items using `{AとBとC / Category X} の中で Aがいちばん [Property]` ("Among..., A is the most..."). Never use `のほう` or `どっち` for 3+ items; use normal question words (`どれ` for a listed set, `何` for a general category, `だれ`, `いつ`, `どこ`).

- **Ex:** `カナダとフランスと日本の中で、どこがいちばん寒いですか。 — カナダがいちばん寒いと思います。` — Among Canada, France, and Japan, which country has the coldest climate? — Canada is the coldest, I think.
- **Ex:** `季節の中でいつがいちばん好きですか。 — 秋がいちばん好きです。` — What season do you like best? — I like fall the most.

### 3. Pronoun `の` ("One") & Possessive `の`

Replace a repeated noun after an adjective (`赤いセーター` → `赤いの`) or after `Noun₁ の` (`メアリーさんのかばん` → `メアリーさんの`) with `の` ("one" / "'s"). Note: `の` for "one" mainly refers to things, not people (`人` is used for people like `頭がいい人`).

- **Ex:** `私は黒いセーターを持っています。赤いのも持っています。` — I have a black sweater. I have a red one, too.
- **Ex:** `ケーキがたくさんあります。好きなのを持っていってください。` — We have a lot of cakes. Take the ones you like.
- **Ex:** `これはソラさんのかばんですか。 — いいえ、それはメアリーさんのです。` — Is this Sora's bag? — No, that is Mary's.

### 4. `〜つもりだ` (Intention / Plan)

Attach `つもりです / つもりだ` to a present-tense short-form verb (affirmative or negative) to state future intentions. Past tense (`つもりでした`) expresses an unrealized past intention ("was planning to..., but...").

- **Ex:** `（私は）週末にたけしさんとテニスをするつもりです。` — I intend to play tennis with Takeshi this weekend.
- **Ex:** `山下先生はあした大学に来ないつもりです。` — Professor Yamashita does not intend to come to school tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `お寺を見に行くつもりでしたが、天気がよくなかったから、行きませんでした。` — We were planning to visit a temple, but we didn't, because the weather was not good.

### 5. `[Adjective / Noun] + なる` ("To become...")

Use `なる` ("to become") to express absolute or relative change.

- **`い`-adjectives:** Replace final `い` with `く` + `なる` (`暖かい` → `暖かくなる`, never `*暖かいになる`).
- **`な`-adjectives & Nouns:** Add `に` + `なる` (`静かになる`, `会社員になる`).
- **Ex:** `日本語の勉強が楽しくなりました。` — Studying the Japanese language is fun now.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんは前より日本語が上手になりました。` — Mary has become better in Japanese than before.

### 6. Indefinite Expressions (`どこか / だれか` & `どこも / だれも`)

Question words + `か` mean "some-/any-?", while question words + `も + negative` mean "not... any-".

- **Forms:** `何か` / `何も` (something / nothing), `だれか` / `だれも` (someone / no one), `どこか` / `どこも` (somewhere / nowhere).
- **Particle rule:** Omit `は`, `が`, `を`, but **keep** `に`, `へ`, `で` (`どこかへ`, `だれかに`, `どこへも`, `だれにも`).
- **Ex:** `どこかへ行きましたか。 — いいえ、どこへも行きませんでした。` — Did you go anywhere? — No, I didn't go anywhere.
- **Ex:** `だれかに会いましたか。 — いいえ、だれにも会いませんでした。` — Did you see anybody? — No, I didn't see anybody.

### 7. The Particle `で` (Means of Transportation & Instrument)

Use `で` with nouns denoting transportation, languages, or tools/instruments ("by", "with", "in").

- **Ex:** `はしでご飯を食べます。` — We eat our meals with chopsticks.
- **Ex:** `日本語で話しましょう。` — Let's talk in Japanese.
- **Ex:** `バスで駅まで行きました。` — I went to the station by bus.

### 8. Expression Notes (`〜てくる` & trailing `〜が / 〜けど`)

- **`〜てくる`:** Verb `て`-form + `くる` describes doing an action somewhere else and returning (`買ってきます` — "I'll buy it and come back").
- **Trailing `が / けど`:** Ending a sentence with `が` or `けど` treats the statement as shared background and politely yields the floor to the listener (`バスツアーがありますが……`).

## Lesson 11: After the Vacation (第11課)

### 1. Desires (`〜たいです` vs. `〜たがっています`)

Use `[Verb Stem] + たいです` to express your own hopes/wishes ("I want to...") or ask someone directly.

- **Grammar of `たい`:** Conjugates as an `い`-adjective (`会いたくないです`, `買いたかったです`). Direct object takes `を` or `が` (`映画を見たいです` / `映画が見たいです`). Long-held wishes use `〜たいと思っています`.
- **Others' wishes:** Do not use `〜たいです` directly for third parties; use quotation (`〜たいと言っていました`) or observation (`Verb Stem + たがっています`, which conjugates as a `u`-verb and takes **`を` only**, not `が`).
- **Ex:** `今度の週末は、映画を見たいです。` — I want to see a film this weekend.
- **Ex:** `あの人には会いたくないです。` — I don't want to see that person.
- **Ex:** `留学したいと思っています。` — I've wanted to study abroad.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんは着物を着たがっています。` — (It seems) Mary wants to wear a kimono.

### 2. Representative Actions (`〜たり〜たりする`)

Unlike `て`-form (which implies an exhaustive, ordered sequence), `[Short Past] + り、[Short Past] + り + する` lists representative example actions ("do such things as A and B") in no fixed order. Tense and larger sentence structure attach to the final `する`.

- **Ex:** `週末は、勉強したり、友だちと話したりしました。` — I have had the experience of studying and talking with my friends, among other things, over the weekend.
- **Ex:** `踊ったり、音楽を聞いたりするのが好きです。` — I like dancing, listening to music, and so forth.

### 3. Past Experience (`[Past Short Form] + ことがある`)

`[Past Affirmative Short Form] + ことがある / あります` means "have the experience of doing..." (even once in one's life). Answer `〜ことがありますか` with `はい、あります` or `はい、行ったことがあります` (never `*はい、ことがあります`). Contrast with `まだ〜ていません` (not yet done, but expected) vs. `〜たことがありません` (never done).

- **Ex:** `富士山に登ったことがあります。` — I have the experience of climbing Mt. Fuji.
- **Ex:** `たけしさんは授業を休んだことがありません。` — Takeshi has never been absent from classes (in his life).

### 4. Non-Exhaustive Noun Listing (`Noun A や Noun B`)

`や` connects nouns as representative examples ("A and B, among other things"), unlike `と` which implies an exhaustive list.

- **Ex:** `京都や奈良に行きました。` — I went to Kyoto and Nara (for example, and may have visited other places as well).

## Lesson 12: Feeling Ill (第12課)

### 1. Explanatory Predicate (`〜んです / 〜のです`)

Attach `んです` (written/formal `のです`, casual `んだ` / questions `の`) to a **short form** to provide or ask for an explanation connecting a fact to the current context. When following a present-tense affirmative **noun or `な`-adjective**, insert `な` (`静かなんです`, `学生なんです`).

- **Asking for clarification:** Common with `どうして〜んですか` ("Why...?") and `どうしたんですか` ("What happened?"). Answer `どうしたんですか` with subject particle `が` (`うちの猫が死んだんです`).
- **Ex:** `バスが来なかったんです。` — (As it happens,) the bus didn't come.
- **Ex:** `あしたテストがあるんです。` — I have an exam tomorrow. (So I can't go out tonight.)
- **Ex:** `どうして彼と別れたんですか。` — Why did you break up with your boyfriend?
- **Ex:** `かわいいノートですね。 — ええ。日本で買ったんです。` — That's a cute notebook. — You bet. I bought it in Japan.

### 2. Excess (`[Verb Stem / Adjective Stem] + すぎる`)

Attach `すぎる` (a `ru`-verb) to a verb stem (`起き` + `すぎる`) or adjective stem (drop `い` or `な`: `高すぎる`, `静かすぎる`) to mean "too much / excessively." Because `すぎる` implies an undesirable excess, use `とても` / `すごく` for positive praise instead of `親切すぎます`. (Noun form: `すぎ`, e.g., `食べすぎです`.)

- **Ex:** `早く起きすぎました。` — I got up too early.
- **Ex:** `この本は高すぎます。` — This book is too expensive.
- **Ex:** `この町は静かすぎます。` — This town is too quiet.

### 3. Giving Advice (`〜たほうがいいです / 〜ないほうがいいです`)

Give advice ("it is better to / not to...") using short forms before `ほうがいいです`. Use **past affirmative** (`〜た`) for affirmative advice and **present negative** (`〜ない`) for negative advice.

- **Ex:** `もっと野菜を食べたほうがいいですよ。` — You'd better eat more vegetables.
- **Ex:** `授業を休まないほうがいいですよ。` — It is better not to skip classes.

### 4. Formal/Polite Reason Clause (`〜ので`)

`[Short Form] + ので` gives a reason ("because..."), sounding slightly softer and more formal than `から` and widely used in written Japanese. Just like `んです`, insert `な` after present-tense affirmative nouns and `な`-adjectives (`いじわるなので`, `日曜日なので`).

- **Ex:** `いつも日本語で話すので、日本語が上手になりました。` — My Japanese has improved, because I always speak Japanese.
- **Ex:** `宿題がたくさんあったので、きのうの夜、寝ませんでした。` — I did not sleep last night, because I had a lot of homework.
- **Ex:** `今日は日曜日なので、銀行は休みです。` — Banks are closed, because today is a Sunday.

### 5. Obligation / Necessity (`〜なければいけません / 〜なきゃいけません`)

Express "must / have to" by taking the negative short form, dropping final `い`, and adding `ければいけません` (more formal/written) or `きゃいけません` (colloquial/spoken). Past tense uses `いけませんでした`; casual speech shortens to `〜なきゃいけない` or just `〜なきゃ`. (Variants: `〜なければなりません`, `〜ないと／なくてはいけません`.)

- **Ex:** `試験があるから、勉強しなければいけません／なきゃいけません。` — I have to study, because there will be an exam.
- **Ex:** `けさは、六時に起きなきゃいけませんでした。` — I had to get up at six this morning.
- **Ex:** `彼女が来るから、部屋を掃除しなきゃ（いけない）。` — I must clean the room, because my girlfriend is coming.

### 6. Tentative Questions & Guesses (`〜でしょうか / 〜でしょう`)

Attach `でしょうか` to short forms (drop `だ` after present affirmative nouns/`な`-adjectives: `野菜でしょうか`, never `*野菜だでしょうか`) to ask polite, tentative questions without putting the listener on the spot. Non-interrogative `でしょう` (often with `たぶん`) expresses a guess ("probably"); casual rising `でしょ？` seeks confirmation ("..., right?").

- **Ex:** `あした、雨が降るでしょうか。 — 降ると思います。` — Would it rain tomorrow? — I think it will.
- **Ex:** `トマトは野菜でしょうか。` — Is a tomato a vegetable?
- **Ex:** `たぶんあしたは寒くないでしょう。` — I bet it will not be cold tomorrow.
