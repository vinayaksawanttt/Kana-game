# Minna no Nihongo II Grammar Notes (Lessons 26–50)

## Lesson 26

### 1. 〜んです (Explanatory / Confirming / Softening Tone)
Attach **んです** (formal spoken form of **のです**) to the **plain form** of verbs, adjectives, and nouns. Note that **な-adjectives** and **nouns** in the present affirmative take **な** before **んです** (`きれいな んです`, `びょうきな んです`).

- **Confirming what you see or hear (`〜んですか`):** Used when the speaker notices something and asks for confirmation or further details.
  - **Ex:** `渡辺さんは 時々 大阪弁を 使いますね。大阪に 住んで いたんですか。` — Ms. Watanabe, you sometimes use Osaka dialect, don't you? Did you use to live in Osaka?
  - **Ex:** `どこで 買ったんですか。` — Where did you buy it?
- **Asking for & giving reasons (`どうして 〜んですか` / `〜んです`):** Explains a reason, background, or excuse—especially when declining an invitation.
  - **Ex:** `どうして 遅れたんですか。 ……バスが 来なかったんです。` — Why were you late? ...The bus didn't come.
  - **Ex:** `わたしは 運動会に 行きません。用事が あるんです。` — I'm not going to the sports day. I have something to do.
- **Asking "What happened?" (`どう したんですか`):** Used to ask about an unusual situation or problem.
  - **Ex:** `どう したんですか。 ……頭が 痛いんです。` — What's wrong? ...I have a headache.

### 2. 〜んですが、〜 (Setting the Stage / Context)
Using **〜んですが、** introduces background context before making a request, asking a question, or seeking advice, making the utterance sound softer and more polite.

- **Ex:** `あしたから 旅行なんです。` — (Context) I'm going on a trip starting tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `ごみを 捨てたいんですが、どこに 出したら いいですか。` — I'd like to throw away some garbage; where should I put it out?

### 3. 〜て いただけませんか (Polite Request)
`[Verb て-form] + いただけませんか` is a very polite way to ask someone to do something for you (more polite than `〜て ください` or `〜て くれませんか`). Often paired with `〜んですが、`.

- **Ex:** `生け花を 習いたいんですが、いい 先生を 紹介して いただけませんか。` — I'd like to learn flower arrangement; could you please introduce a good teacher to me?
- **Ex:** `日本語で レポートを 書いたんですが、ちょっと 見て いただけませんか。` — I wrote a report in Japanese; could you take a quick look at it?

### 4. 〜たら いいですか (Asking for Advice / Instructions)
`[Question word] + [Verb た-form] + ら いいですか` is used to ask what one should do, how to do something, or which option to choose.

- **Ex:** `ごみは どこに 出したら いいですか。` — Where should I put out the garbage?
- **Ex:** `国会議事堂を 見学したいんですが、どう したら いいですか。` — I'd like to tour the National Diet Building; what should I do?
- **Ex:** `市役所に 聞いたら いいと 思いますよ。` — I think you should ask at city hall.

## Lesson 27

### 1. Potential Verbs (可能動詞)
Potential verbs express ability or possibility.
- **Group I:** Change the `-i` vowel before `ます` to `-e` (`書きます` → `書けます`, `話します` → `話せます`).
- **Group II:** Replace `ます` with `られます` (`食べます` → `食べられます`, `寝ます` → `寝られます`).
- **Group III:** `来ます` → `来られます`, `します` → `できます`.
- Potential verbs conjugate as Group II verbs.
- **Ex:** `わたしは 日本語が 少し 話せます。` — I can speak a little Japanese.
- **Ex:** `日本語の 新聞が 読めますか。` — Can you read Japanese newspapers?

### 2. Particle が with Potential Verbs
With potential verbs, the object of a transitive action (normally marked with `を`) is marked with `が`. Other particles (`へ`, `に`, `で`, `から`, etc.) remain unchanged.
- **Ex:** `一人で 着物が 着られます。` — I can put on a kimono by myself.
- **Ex:** `カードで 払えます。` — You can pay by card.

### 3. 見えます and 聞こえます
`見えます` (to be visible) and `聞こえます` (to be audible) describe what naturally enters one's field of vision or hearing without intentional effort, whereas `見られます` and `聞けます` express the ability or opportunity to watch/listen intentionally.
- **Ex:** `山が はっきり 見えます。` — The mountain can be seen clearly.
- **Ex:** `鳥の 声が 聞こえますね。` — You can hear the birds singing, can't you?

### 4. できます (to be built / made / completed)
`できます` means something comes into existence, is built, completed, or made ready.
- **Ex:** `駅の 前に 大きい スーパーが できました。` — A large supermarket has been built in front of the station.
- **Ex:** `すぐ できます。` — It will be ready right away.

### 5. ～しか ～ません (only)
`しか` attaches to nouns or quantifiers and is always followed by a negative verb, meaning "only" or "nothing but" (often with a nuance of insufficiency). It replaces `が` or `を`.
- **Ex:** `わたしの 会社は 1週間しか 休めません。` — At my company, I can only take one week off.
- **Ex:** `100円しか ありませんから、コーヒーが 買えません。` — Because I only have 100 yen, I can't buy coffee.

### 6. Contrastive は
`は` is used to contrast two items, replacing `が` or `を` (or following `に`, `で`, `へ`, etc.).
- **Ex:** `小さい 鳥や 魚は 飼えますが、犬や 猫は 飼えません。` — You can keep small birds and fish, but you can't keep dogs or cats.

## Lesson 28

### 1. V1 (ます-stem) ながら V2 (Simultaneous Actions)
Indicates that a person performs two actions at the same time. `V2` is the main action and carries the tense, while `V1` is the accompanying background action. Both verbs share the same subject.
- **Ex:** `音楽を聞きながら食事します。` — I eat while listening to music.
- **Ex:** `彼は働きながら大学で勉強しています。` — He is studying at university while working.

### 2. V-ています (Habitual Actions)
In addition to ongoing actions and resulting states, `V-ています` expresses actions that are repeated regularly or habitually (e.g., daily routines, current lifestyle habits). Past habits use `V-ていました`.
- **Ex:** `毎朝ジョギングをしています。` — I go jogging every morning.
- **Ex:** `休みの日はいつも何をしていますか。……たいてい絵をかいています。` — What do you usually do on your days off? — I usually draw pictures.

### 3. Plain Form + し、Plain Form + し、(それに) ～ (Listing Reasons / Qualities)
Used to list multiple qualities, states, or reasons in a parallel manner. `も` often replaces `が` or `を` to emphasize "and also". When explaining a reason, `から` is often added at the end, or the conclusion is left implied with `……`. `それに` ("moreover / besides") can introduce an additional point.
- **Ex:** `地下鉄は速いし、安いし、地下鉄で行きましょう。` — The subway is fast and cheap, so let's go by subway.
- **Ex:** `ワット先生は熱心だし、おもしろいし、それに経験もあります。` — Mr. Watt is enthusiastic, interesting, and moreover has experience.

### 4. それで (Therefore / So / That's Why)
A conjunction that connects a reason or situation just mentioned to a natural result or action that follows.
- **Ex:** `ここはコーヒーもおいしいし、食事もできるし……。―それで人気があるんですね。` — Here the coffee is delicious and you can also eat... — So that's why it's popular.

## Lesson 29

### 1. Vて-form います (Resultant State)
Describes the state resulting from an action or change, typically used with intransitive verbs (`自動詞`) such as `開きます`, `閉まります`, `つきます`, `消えます`, `壊れます`, `割れます`, `折れます`, `破れます`, `汚れます`, `付きます`, `外れます`, `止まります`, `掛かります`. The subject is marked by `が`.
- **Ex:** `窓が 閉まって います。` — The window is closed.
- **Ex:** `会議室の かぎが 掛かって いますね。` — The meeting room is locked, isn't it?

### 2. Nは Vて-form います (Topicalized Resultant State)
When the speaker refers to a specific item already known in the context (often with `この`, `その`, `あの`), it becomes the topic marked by `は` instead of `が`.
- **Ex:** `この 袋は 破れて いますから、捨てましょう。` — This bag is torn, so let's throw it away.
- **Ex:** `その 傘は 折れて いますよ。` — That umbrella is broken.

### 3. Vて-form しまいます / しまいました (Completion)
Used to emphasize that an action has been completely finished (`て しまいました`), or will be finished completely in the near future (`て しまいます`). Often paired with `もう` or `全部`.
- **Ex:** `シュミットさんが 持って 来た ワインは みんなで 飲んで しまいました。` — We drank up all the wine Mr. Schmidt brought.
- **Ex:** `この メールを 書いて しまいますから、お先に どうぞ。` — I'll finish writing this email, so please go ahead.

### 4. Vて-form しまいました (Regret / Unintentional Action)
Expresses the speaker's regret, embarrassment, or dismay over an unintentional, unfortunate, or irreversible event. In conversation, `〜て しまったんです` is frequently used to explain what went wrong.
- **Ex:** `電車に 傘を 忘れて しまいました。` — I accidentally left my umbrella on the train.
- **Ex:** `どこかで 財布を 落として しまったんです。` — I accidentally dropped my wallet somewhere.

### 5. ありました (Found Something)
The past tense `ありました` is used when something that was lost, missing, or being searched for has been found.
- **Ex:** `ありましたよ。` — Here it is! / We found it!

### 6. どこかで / どこかに (Indefinite Place)
Adding `か` to an interrogative such as `どこ` creates an indefinite pronoun meaning "somewhere". Particles like `で` or `に` follow `どこか`.
- **Ex:** `どこかで 財布を 落として しまったんです。` — I dropped my wallet somewhere.

## Lesson 30

### 1. Vて-form あります (Resultant State of an Intentional Action)
`[Place] に [Object] が [Transitive Verb て-form] あります` expresses the state resulting from someone's intentional action. Unlike `Vて います` with intransitive verbs (Lesson 29), `Vて あります` uses transitive verbs and implies that someone did the action for a purpose. When the object is the topic, `は` replaces `が`.
- **Ex:** `交番に　町の　地図が　はって　あります。` — A map of the town is posted at the police box.
- **Ex:** `セロテープは　あの　引き出しに　しまって　ありますよ。` — The cellophane tape is put away in that drawer.

### 2. Vて-form おきます (Preparation, Completion, or Leaving As-Is)
`Vて おきます` has three main uses:
1. Doing something in advance to prepare for a future event.
2. Completing a follow-up action after finishing something so it is ready for the next use.
3. Leaving something in its current state (`そのままに して おきます`).
- **Ex:** `旅行の　まえに、インターネットで　いろいろ　調べて　おきます。` — Before the trip, I will look up various things on the Internet in advance.
- **Ex:** `はさみを　使ったら、元の　所に　戻して　おいて　ください。` — After using the scissors, please put them back in their original place.
- **Ex:** `そのままに　して　おいて　ください。` — Please leave it as it is.

### 3. まだ Vて います (Still Doing)
`まだ Vて います` indicates that an action or state is still continuing at the present time.
- **Ex:** `まだ　使って　いますから。` — Because I am still using it.

### 4. Vないと (Colloquial Obligation: "I'd better...")
In casual conversation, `Vない-form + と` (short for `～ないと いけません`) expresses a sense of necessity or obligation.
- **Ex:** `わたしも　準備して　おかないと。` — I'd better prepare one too.

## Lesson 31

### 1. Verb Volitional Form (意向形)
The volitional form is the casual equivalent of ～ましょう.
- **Group I:** Change the final `-i` sound before `ます` to the corresponding `-o` sound + `う` (e.g., `かいます` → `かおう`, `あるきます` → `あるこう`, `いそぎます` → `いそごう`, `まちます` → `まとう`, `やすみます` → `やすもう`, `のります` → `のろう`).
- **Group II:** Replace `ます` with `よう` (e.g., `かえます` → `かえよう`, `みます` → `みよう`, `ねます` → `ねよう`).
- **Group III:** `きます` → `こよう`, `します` → `しよう`, `きゅうけいします` → `きゅうけいしよう`.
- **Ex:** `いっしょに 行こう。` — Let's go together.
- **Ex:** `疲れたね。ちょっと 休まない？ ……うん、そう しよう。` — You're tired, aren't you? Shall we take a short rest? ...Yeah, let's do that.

### 2. V(volitional) と 思って います
Used to express a speaker's intention or something they have been thinking of doing. `と思っています` (continuative form) indicates an ongoing intention held from the past up to now.
- **Ex:** `将来 自分の 会社を 作ろうと 思って います。` — I'm thinking of starting my own company in the future.
- **Ex:** `お正月は 何を しますか。 ……家族と 温泉に 行こうと 思って います。` — What are you going to do for New Year's? ...I'm thinking of going to a hot spring with my family.

### 3. V(dictionary / nai) つもりです
Used to express a firm plan or intention to do (or not to do) something. Compared to `～ようと思っています`, `つもりです` expresses a stronger, more decided intention.
- **Ex:** `来月 車を 買う つもりです。` — I plan to buy a car next month.
- **Ex:** `大学院の 試験を 受けますから、ことしは 帰らない つもりです。` — Since I'm taking the graduate school entrance exam, I don't plan to go back this year.

### 4. V(dictionary) / N + の + 予定です
Used to state a fixed schedule, itinerary, or official plan.
- **Ex:** `来週の 金曜日に 帰る 予定です。` — I'm scheduled to return next Friday.
- **Ex:** `来週は 出張の 予定です。` — Next week is scheduled for a business trip.

### 5. まだ Vて いません
Indicates that an action has not yet been completed up to the present moment. Often paired with `もう Vましたか` ("Have you already...?") and followed by an intention (`～ようと思っています`).
- **Ex:** `レポートは もう できましたか。 ……いいえ、まだ 書いて いません。金曜日までに まとめようと 思って います。` — Have you finished your report yet? ...No, I haven't written it yet. I'm thinking of pulling it together by Friday.

## Lesson 32

### 1. Vた形／Vない形 ＋ ほうがいいです (Giving advice / recommendation)
Used to give advice or recommend doing or not doing something. Because it can sound strong, it is often softened with `よ` when speaking directly to someone.
- **Ex:** `毎日運動したほうがいいです。` — You should exercise every day.
- **Ex:** `現金で持って行かないほうがいいですよ。` — You shouldn't take it in cash.

### 2. Plain form ＋ でしょう (Conjecture / probability)
Expresses the speaker's conjecture or prediction based on certain information or reasoning. When used as a question (`～でしょうか`), it asks for the listener's conjecture. Note that な-adjectives and nouns drop `だ` before `でしょう`.
- **Ex:** `あしたは雪が降るでしょう。` — It will probably snow tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `日本の経済はどうなるでしょうか。` — What do you think will happen to the Japanese economy?

### 3. Plain form ＋ かもしれません (Possibility)
Expresses the possibility that something is true or might happen (lower certainty than `でしょう`). Like `でしょう`, な-adjectives and nouns drop `だ` before `かもしれません`.
- **Ex:** `約束の時間に間に合わないかもしれません。` — I might not make it in time for the appointment.
- **Ex:** `故障かもしれません。` — It might be a breakdown.

### 4. きっと / たぶん / もしかしたら (Adverbs of certainty)
Adverbs commonly paired with `でしょう` and `かもしれません`: `きっと` (surely/certainly, with `でしょう`), `たぶん` (probably, with `でしょう`), and `もしかしたら` (perhaps/possibly, with `かもしれません`).
- **Ex:** `よく勉強していましたから、きっと合格するでしょう。` — Because he studied hard, he will surely pass.

## Lesson 33

### 1. 命令形 (Imperative Form)
Used to give a direct order or strong command (mostly by men, or in emergencies, sports cheering, road signs, and quoted instructions).
- **Group I:** Change the final `-i` sound before `ます` to the `-e` sound (e.g., `いいます` → `いえ`, `急ぎます` → `急げ`).
- **Group II:** Replace `ます` with `ろ` (e.g., `やめます` → `やめろ`, `止めます` → `止めろ`).
- **Group III:** `きます` → `こい`, `します` → `しろ`.
- **Ex:** `急げ。` — Hurry up.
- **Ex:** `だめだ。もう走れない。……頑張れ。あと500メートルだ。` — It's no good. I can't run anymore. ... Hang in there! Only 500 meters left.

### 2. 禁止形 (Prohibitive Form: V-dictionary + な)
Used to strongly forbid someone from doing something ("Do not ~"). Formed by attaching `な` to the dictionary form of any verb.
- **Ex:** `触るな。` — Don't touch.
- **Ex:** `もう時間がない。……まだ1分ある。あきらめるな。` — There's no more time. ... There's still one minute. Don't give up.

### 3. ～と書いてあります / ～と読みます
Used to state what is written on a sign/notice or how kanji/words are read.
- **Ex:** `あそこに「入るな」と書いてありますよ。` — It says "Do not enter" over there.
- **Ex:** `あの漢字は何と読むんですか。……「きんえん」です。` — How do you read that kanji? ... It's "kin'en".

### 4. Xは Yという意味です / どういう意味ですか
Used to explain or ask the meaning of a word, kanji, or symbol. The explanatory clause `Y` takes the plain form before `という`.
- **Ex:** `立入禁止は入るなという意味です。` — "Tachiiri kinshi" means "Do not enter."
- **Ex:** `このマークはどういう意味ですか。……洗濯機で洗えるという意味です。` — What does this mark mean? ... It means it can be washed in a washing machine.

### 5. Plain Form + と言っていました (Reported Speech)
Used to relay what someone else said. `～と言っていました` uses the past continuous form for statements someone made previously.
- **Ex:** `ミラーさんは来週大阪へ出張すると言っていました。` — Mr. Miller said that he will go on a business trip to Osaka next week.
- **Ex:** `今出かけています。30分ぐらいで戻ると言っていました。` — He is out right now. He said he would be back in about 30 minutes.

### 6. Plain Form + と伝えていただけませんか (Relaying a Message)
Used to politely ask someone to pass on a message to a third party. Nouns and na-adjectives take `だ` before `と`.
- **Ex:** `すみませんが、渡辺さんにあしたのパーティーは6時からだと伝えていただけませんか。` — Excuse me, could you please tell Ms. Watanabe that tomorrow's party starts at 6 o'clock?

## Lesson 34

### 1. Vた / Nの とおりに、〜 (Just as... / According to...)
Used to indicate doing something in the exact same way as a preceding action, instruction, or standard. Attach `とおりに` directly to the plain past (`た`-form) of a verb, or connect it to a noun with `の`.
- **Ex:** `先生が 言った とおりに、書きます。` — I write it just as the teacher said.
- **Ex:** `説明書の とおりに、組み立てて ください。` — Please assemble it according to the instruction manual.

### 2. Vた / Nの あとで、〜 (After...)
Indicates that the action in the main clause happens after the event or action in the first clause. Attach `あとで` to the `た`-form of a verb or after `Noun + の`. Unlike `〜てから` (which emphasizes sequential order or prerequisite), `〜あとで` simply states that one event follows another in time.
- **Ex:** `ごはんを 食べた あとで、歯を 磨きます。` — After eating a meal, I brush my teeth.
- **Ex:** `仕事の あとで、飲みに 行きませんか。` — Would you like to go for a drink after work?

### 3. Vて / Vないで (Doing / Without doing...)
`Vて` and `Vないで` describe the accompanying state or manner in which the main action is performed (e.g., wearing or not wearing something, adding or omitting something).
- **Ex:** `コーヒーは 砂糖を 入れないで 飲みます。` — I drink coffee without putting sugar in it.
- **Ex:** `白い ネクタイを して 行きます。` — I go wearing a white tie.

### 4. Vないで、〜 (Without doing X, do Y instead)
When contrasting two mutually exclusive actions, `Vないで` means "instead of doing X, do Y" or "without doing X, Y."
- **Ex:** `最近 エレベーターに 乗らないで、階段を 使って いるんです。` — Lately I've been using the stairs instead of taking the elevator.

## Lesson 35

### 1. Conditional Form (条件形: ～ば / ～ければ / ～なら)

- **Group I verbs:** Change the final `-i` syllable before `ます` to the corresponding `-e` syllable and add `ば` (e.g., `あいます` → `あえば`, `ききます` → `きけば`, `いそぎます` → `いそげば`, `だします` → `だせば`, `まちます` → `まてば`, `よびます` → `よべば`, `のみます` → `のめば`, `ふります` → `ふれば`).
- **Group II verbs:** Replace `ます` with `れば` (e.g., `はれます` → `はれれば`, `みます` → `みれば`).
- **Group III verbs:** `きます` → `くれば`, `します` → `すれば`.
- **Negative verbs:** Replace the `い` of the `ない`-form with `ければ` (`～なければ`, e.g., `おきない` → `おきなければ`, `ない` → `なければ`).
- **い-adjectives:** Replace `い` with `ければ` (e.g., `たかい` → `たかければ`; `いい` → `よければ`).
- **な-adjectives & Nouns:** Attach `なら` directly to the stem or noun (e.g., `きれいなら`, `まじめなら`, `あめなら`, `むりょうなら`).
- **Ex:** `春に なれば、桜が 咲きます。` — When spring comes, the cherry blossoms bloom.
- **Ex:** `天気が よければ、向こうに 島が 見えます。` — If the weather is good, you can see the island over there.

### 2. Conditional Sentences (`V1ば / Aければ / Nなら, V2`)

Expresses a condition under which the result in the main clause occurs, or gives advice/instructions depending on a condition.
- **Ex:** `その ボタンを 押せば、開きますよ。` — If you press that button, it will open.
- **Ex:** `もう 少し 物価が 安ければ、もっと いいと 思います。` — If prices were a little lower, I think it would be even better.
- **Ex:** `無理なら、金曜日までに 出して ください。` — If that's impossible, please submit it by Friday.

### 3. Topic-Based Recommendation (`N なら、～が いいです`)

Used to pick up a topic mentioned by the listener (or raised in context) and offer advice, an opinion, or a recommendation about it.
- **Ex:** `北海道旅行なら、6月が いいです。` — If it's a trip to Hokkaido, June is good.
- **Ex:** `2、3日なら、箱根か 日光が いいと 思います。` — If it's for 2 or 3 days, I think Hakone or Nikko is good.

### 4. Asking for Instructions (`疑問詞 + Vば いいですか`)

Used when asking someone what to do or how to proceed in a given situation.
- **Ex:** `本を 借りたいんですが、どう すれば いいですか。` — I'd like to borrow a book; what should I do?
- **Ex:** `日本の マンガに ついて 調べたいんですが、どこへ 行けば いいですか。` — I'd like to research Japanese manga; where should I go?

## Lesson 36

### 1. V (dictionary form / ない-form) ように、～ (So that / In order that)
Expresses a purpose or goal where the preceding clause describes a state or potential action (often using potential verbs, わかる, 見える, 聞こえる, or negative forms like 忘れない, 遅れない) that the subject wants to bring about or prevent through the action in the main clause. Unlike ために, ように is used with non-volitional verbs or potential verbs and when the subjects of the two clauses differ.
- **Ex:** `速く 泳げるように、毎日 練習して います。` — I practice every day so that I can swim fast.
- **Ex:** `忘れないように、付けて あるんです。` — I marked it so that I won't forget.

### 2. V (dictionary form / ない-form) ように なります (Become able to / Come to)
Indicates a change in state or ability over time—something that could not be done before becomes possible (using a potential verb), or a new habit/situation develops. With ない-form, it means something ceases to happen or is no longer the case.
- **Ex:** `やっと 自転車に 乗れるように なりました。` — I finally became able to ride a bicycle.
- **Ex:** `初めは 食べられませんでしたが、今は 何でも 食べられるように なりました。` — At first I couldn't eat it, but now I can eat anything.

### 3. V (dictionary form / ない-form) ように して います / して ください (Make an effort to / Try to)
`ように して います` expresses an ongoing habitual effort to do (or not do) something. `ように して ください` is used to ask or advise someone to make an effort to do (or avoid doing) something.
- **Ex:** `毎日 日記を 書くように して います。` — I try to write in my diary every day.
- **Ex:** `絶対に 遅れないように して ください。` — Please make sure not to be late.

## Lesson 37

### 1. Passive Form of Verbs (動詞の受身形)
Verbs conjugate into the passive form depending on their group:
- **Group I**: Change the `i`-sound before `ます` to the `a`-sound and add `れます` (e.g., `いいます` → `いわれます`, `かきます` → `かかれます`, `ふみます` → `ふまれます`).
- **Group II**: Replace `ます` with `られます` (e.g., `ほめます` → `ほめられます`, `みます` → `みられます`).
- **Group III**: `きます` → `こられます`, `します` → `されます`.
- **Ex:** `法隆寺は607年に建てられました。` — Horyuji Temple was built in 607.

### 2. Direct Passive (`N1 は N2 に V受身`)
Indicates that N1 receives an action performed by N2.
- **Ex:** `子どものとき、よく母にしかられました。` — When I was a child, I was often scolded by my mother.

### 3. Possessive / Adversative Passive (`N1 は N2 に N3 を V受身`)
Used when someone (N2) performs an action on something belonging to N1 (N3), often implying N1 is troubled or inconvenienced.
- **Ex:** `ラッシュの電車で足を踏まれました。` — My foot was stepped on in the rush-hour train.
- **Ex:** `だれかに傘をまちがえられたんです。` — Someone took my umbrella by mistake.

### 4. Inanimate Subject Passive (`N が/は V受身`)
Used when an inanimate thing or event is the topic/subject, such as historical events, discoveries, or general practices, without specifying the agent.
- **Ex:** `ことしの世界子ども会議はどこで開かれますか。` — Where will this year's World Children's Conference be held?
- **Ex:** `ブラジルでは何語が使われていますか。` — What language is spoken in Brazil?

### 5. Materials (`N1 は N2 から/で 作られます・造られます`)
`で` is used when the raw material remains visible/recognizable in the final product, whereas `から` is used when the raw material is transformed and no longer recognizable.
- **Ex:** `ビールは麦から造られます。` — Beer is made from barley.

## Lesson 38

### 1. V（辞書形）のは Adj です (Nominalizing a verb as topic)
Attaching the nominalizer `の` to the dictionary form of a verb turns the verb phrase into a noun phrase ("doing ~"). Followed by `は` and an adjective (such as `楽しい`, `おもしろい`, `難しい`, `大変`, `危ない`, `気持ちがいい`, `無理`), it states an evaluation or characteristic of that action.
- **Ex:** `絵をかくのは楽しいです。` — Drawing pictures is fun.
- **Ex:** `始めるのは簡単ですが、続けるのは難しいですね。` — Starting is easy, but continuing is hard, isn't it?

### 2. V（辞書形）のが Adj です (Nominalizing a verb with preference/ability/speed)
When expressing likes, dislikes, skill, or speed with adjectives such as `好き`, `嫌い`, `上手`, `下手`, `速い`, `遅い`, the nominalized verb phrase takes the particle `が`.
- **Ex:** `わたしは星を見るのが好きです。` — I like watching the stars.
- **Ex:** `夫は花を育てるのが上手なんです。` — My husband is good at growing flowers.

### 3. V（辞書形）のを 忘れました (Forgetting to do something)
`V（辞書形）のを 忘れました` means "forgot to do ~". The nominalizer `の` turns the action into the direct object of `忘れる` marked by `を`.
- **Ex:** `財布を持って来るのを忘れました。` — I forgot to bring my wallet.
- **Ex:** `回覧にはんこを押すのを忘れないでください。` — Please don't forget to put your stamp on the circular.

### 4. V（普通形）のを 知っていますか (Knowing a fact or event)
Using the plain form of a verb (or adjective) before `の` with `知っていますか` / `知りませんでした` asks or states whether someone knows about an event or state.
- **Ex:** `宮崎さんに赤ちゃんが生まれたのを知っていますか。` — Did you know that a baby was born to Miyazaki-san?
- **Ex:** `富士山は7月と8月しか登れないのを知っていますか。` — Did you know that Mt. Fuji can only be climbed in July and August?

### 5. 〜のは 〜です (Cleft sentence for emphasis)
A cleft sentence places the presupposed action/state before `のは` and the focused element (time, place, person, or thing) before `です`. Note that when a noun is modified before `のは`, the `の` itself stands in for that noun/person/thing (e.g., `いちばん大切なのは家族の健康です`).
- **Ex:** `わたしが日本へ来たのは去年の3月です。` — It was in March last year that I came to Japan.
- **Ex:** `彼女に初めて会ったのは小学校の教室です。` — It was in an elementary school classroom that I first met her.

### 6. Distinguishing の and こと
While both `の` and `こと` nominalize verbs, `こと` is required in fixed patterns like `〜ことができます`, `〜たことがあります`, and `趣味は〜ことです`, whereas `の` is required before perception/knowledge/forgetting verbs (`〜のを忘れる`, `〜のを知っている`) and in cleft sentences (`〜のは〜です`).
- **Ex:** `鈴木さんはベトナム語を話すことができます。` — Suzuki-san can speak Vietnamese.
- **Ex:** `課長に連絡したのはおとといです。` — It was the day before yesterday that I contacted the section chief.

## Lesson 39

### 1. て-form for Cause / Reason (Emotional & Non-controllable Results)
The て-form of verbs (including ～なくて), い-adjectives (～くて), and な-adjectives (～で) can express a cause or reason. Unlike から or ので, the result in the main clause is limited to emotional reactions (e.g. びっくりする, 安心する, がっかりする, 寂しい, うれしい, 悲しい, 恥ずかしい, 心配だ) or non-controllable/potential states (e.g. わからない, できない, 食べられない, 眠れない). It cannot be used with volitional requests, commands, or invitations.
- **Ex:** `ニュースを聞いて、びっくりしました。` — I was surprised to hear the news.
- **Ex:** `家族に会えなくて、寂しいです。` — I'm lonely because I can't see my family.
- **Ex:** `話が複雑で、よくわかりませんでした。` — The story was complicated, so I didn't understand it well.

### 2. Noun + で (Cause / Reason)
A noun followed by the particle で indicates the cause or reason for an event, especially natural disasters, accidents, illnesses, or unexpected occurrences (地震, 台風, 事故, 火事, 病気, etc.). The predicate is typically an intransitive or non-volitional verb.
- **Ex:** `地震でビルが倒れました。` — A building collapsed because of the earthquake.
- **Ex:** `事故でバスが遅れたんです。` — The bus was delayed because of an accident.

### 3. ～ので (Reason / Cause)
ので connects a reason clause to a result clause. It attaches to the plain form of verbs and い-adjectives, and to な-adjectives and nouns using な (e.g. 暇なので, 雨なので). Compared to から, ので sounds softer, more objective, and more polite, and is preferred when asking permission or making excuses.
- **Ex:** `体の調子が悪いので、病院へ行きます。` — Because I am not feeling well, I am going to the hospital.
- **Ex:** `きょうは妻の誕生日なので、花を買って帰ります。` — Since today is my wife's birthday, I'll buy flowers on my way home.
- **Ex:** `気分が悪いので、帰ってもいいですか。` — Since I feel unwell, may I go home?

### 4. 途中で (On the way) and ～てしまう (Regret / Unintended Completion)
途中で means "on the way" or "in the middle of" an action/event (used with Verb dictionary form + 途中で or Noun + の + 途中で). In the dialogue, it is paired with ～てしまう (here in polite explanatory form ～てしまったんです / ～てしまって), which expresses regret or that something happened unintentionally.
- **Ex:** `来る途中で事故があって、バスが遅れてしまったんです。` — On the way here there was an accident, and the bus ended up being delayed.
- **Ex:** `ケータイをうちに忘れてしまって……。` — I accidentally left my mobile phone at home...

## Lesson 40

### 1. Embedded Questions with Question Words (`[Question word] ... か`)
A question containing an interrogative word (e.g., 何時、どこ、どうやって、いつ、だれ、どれ、どんな) can be embedded as a clause inside a larger sentence by using the **plain form** of the predicate followed by **か**. Note that `だ` is dropped after na-adjectives and nouns.

- **Ex:** `JL107便は何時に到着するか、調べてください。` — Please check what time Flight JL107 arrives.
- **Ex:** `わたしたちが初めて会ったのはいつか、覚えていますか。` — Do you remember when we first met?
- **Ex:** `この服はどちらが表か、わかりません。` — I don't know which side of these clothes is the front.

### 2. Embedded Yes/No Questions (`... かどうか`)
A question without an interrogative word (a yes/no question) is embedded using the **plain form** followed by **かどうか** ("whether or not"). As with `〜か`, `だ` is omitted after na-adjectives and nouns.

- **Ex:** `台風9号は東京へ来るかどうか、まだわかりません。` — I still don't know whether Typhoon No. 9 will come to Tokyo or not.
- **Ex:** `忘年会に出席できるかどうか、メールで返事をください。` — Please reply by email whether you can attend the year-end party or not.
- **Ex:** `その話はほんとうかどうか、確かめてください。` — Please confirm whether that story is true or not.

### 3. `Vて みます` (Try doing something)
Attaching `みます` to the te-form of a verb expresses doing something as a trial or to see what happens / what it is like. It conjugates like a Group 2 (ichidan) verb (`みてみます`, `みてみました`, `みてみてもいいですか`, `みてみたいです`).

- **Ex:** `この服を着てみてもいいですか。` — May I try on these clothes?
- **Ex:** `できるかどうか、やってみます。` — I'll try doing it to see if I can.
- **Ex:** `長崎へ行ったことがないので、ぜひ一度行ってみたいです。` — Since I've never been to Nagasaki, I'd definitely like to try going once.

## Lesson 41

### 1. `いただきます` / `くださいます` / `やります` (Polite & Hierarchical Giving/Receiving)
- `いただきます` is the humble/polite equivalent of `もらいます`, used when receiving something from a person of higher status (e.g., teacher, boss).
- `くださいます` is the respectful equivalent of `くれます`, used when someone of higher status gives something to the speaker (or speaker's in-group).
- `やります` is used instead of `あげます` when giving something to someone of lower status (such as one's own child or grandchild), animals, or plants.
- **Ex:** `わたしは ワット先生に 本を いただきました。` — I received a book from Mr. Watt.
- **Ex:** `部長の 奥さんが わたしに お土産を くださいました。` — The department manager's wife gave me a souvenir.
- **Ex:** `わたしは 息子に お菓子を やりました。` — I gave sweets to my son.

### 2. `Vて いただきます` / `Vて くださいます` / `Vて やります` (Giving and Receiving Actions)
- Attaching `いただきます`, `くださいます`, or `やります` to the `て`-form of a verb expresses doing or receiving a favor with the same status/politeness distinctions as above.
- **Ex:** `わたしは 先生に 漢字の まちがいを 直して いただきました。` — The teacher corrected my kanji mistakes for me.
- **Ex:** `部長の 奥さんが お茶を 教えて くださいました。` — The department manager's wife taught me tea ceremony.
- **Ex:** `わたしは 息子に 紙飛行機を 作って やりました。` — I made a paper airplane for my son.

### 3. `Vて くださいませんか` (Polite Requests)
- `Vて くださいませんか` is a softer, more polite way to make a request than `Vて ください`.
- **Ex:** `新しい コピー機の 使い方が よく わからないんですが、ちょっと 教えて くださいませんか。` — I don't really understand how to use the new copy machine; could you please show me?

### 4. `N に` (Purpose / Occasion for Giving)
- `に` marks the occasion or purpose for which something is given (such as `結婚の お祝いに` as a wedding gift).
- **Ex:** `結婚の お祝いに 田中さんが くださいました。` — Mr. Tanaka gave it to me as a wedding present.

## Lesson 42

### 1. V dictionary form / N の + ために (Purpose: "in order to / for")
Expresses a clear purpose or goal (intentional action by a person or group), or "for the sake of" a person/group. With a verb, use the dictionary form (`Vる + ために`); with a noun, use `N のために`.
- **Ex:** `将来自分の店を持つために、貯金しています。` — I am saving money in order to have my own shop in the future.
- **Ex:** `健康のために、何かしていますか。` — Are you doing anything for your health?
- **Ex:** `ベートーベンがある女の人のために、作った曲です。` — It's a piece Beethoven composed for a certain woman.

### 2. V dictionary form + のに / N に + 使います・いいです・便利です・役に立ちます・かかります・必要です
Expresses the purpose, use, suitability, or cost/time required for an action or noun. When a verb precedes `に`, it is nominalized with `の` (`Vる + のに`). When a noun is used directly, `の` is omitted (`N + に`).
- **Ex:** `ワインを開けるのに使います。` — It's used for opening wine.
- **Ex:** `この靴は山を歩くのにいいです。` — These shoes are good for walking in the mountains.
- **Ex:** `この橋を造るのに何年かかりましたか。` — How many years did it take to build this bridge?
- **Ex:** `2、3日の出張にいいかばんがありますか。` — Do you have a good bag for a 2- or 3-day business trip?

### 3. Comparison: ために vs. のに
Both indicate purpose, but `ために` is used for a person's conscious intention/goal or for the benefit of someone/something, whereas `のに` is used to describe the function, suitability, convenience, or required time/cost of a tool, place, or action.
- **Ex:** `引っ越しのために、車を借ります。` — I will rent a car for moving. (intentional purpose)
- **Ex:** `パソコンを直すのに5時間かかりました。` — It took 5 hours to fix the computer. (required time)

### 1. 〜場合は、〜 (In the event that / If 〜)
Used to state a hypothetical situation or condition and the action to be taken in that case.
- **Form:**
  - Verb (plain form) + 場合は
  - い-adjective (plain form) + 場合は

### 1. `V (dictionary form) ところです` — Just about to do something
Used with adverbs like `ちょうど` (just), `今から` (from now), or `これから` (from now on) to express that an action is on the verge of starting.

Used with `今` (now) to express that an action is currently in progress.

Used with `たった今` (just a moment ago), `今` (now), or `ちょうど` (just) to emphasize that an action has very recently been completed.

## Lesson 47

### 1. 〜そうです (Hearsay: "I hear that...")
Used to report information heard or read from another source without adding personal judgment. Attached to the plain form of verbs and i-adjectives, and to `〜だ` for na-adjectives and nouns.
- **Ex:** `新聞で読んだんですが、1月に日本語のスピーチ大会があるそうですよ。` — I read in the newspaper that there is a Japanese speech contest in January.
- **Ex:** `ワット先生は厳しい先生だそうですね。` — I hear Mr. Watt is a strict teacher.

### 2. 〜によると、〜そうです ("According to...")
Indicates the source of the reported information (e.g., weather forecast, newspaper, someone's letter).
- **Ex:** `天気予報によると、あしたは寒くなるそうです。` — According to the weather forecast, it is going to get cold tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `昼のニュースによると、失敗したそうです。` — According to the noon news, it failed.

### 3. 〜ようです (Conjecture: "It seems that...")
Expresses the speaker's subjective conjecture or inference based on sensory evidence, observation, or situation. Attached to the plain form of verbs and i-adjectives, `〜な` for na-adjectives, and `〜の` for nouns. Often used with `どうも` ("somehow / it really seems").
- **Ex:** `隣の部屋にだれかいるようです。` — It seems someone is in the next room.
- **Ex:** `どうも恋人ができたようですね。` — It seems she's got a boyfriend.
- **Ex:** `事故のようですね。` — It seems to be an accident.

### 4. 〜がします ("To sense a smell / taste / sound / voice")
Used with nouns representing sensory perceptions (`におい`, `味`, `音`, `声`) to indicate that one perceives them.
- **Ex:** `にぎやかな声がしますね。` — We can hear lively voices, can't we?
- **Ex:** `変なにおいがしますね。` — There's a strange smell, isn't there?

### 5. Contrast: 〜そうです (Appearance, L43) vs. 〜そうです (Hearsay, L47)
- **Appearance (L43):** Attaches to verb stem / adj stem (`〜そう`, `いい → よさそう`, `ない → なさそう`). Describes how something looks at first glance.
- **Hearsay (L47):** Attaches to plain form (`〜そう`, `いい → いいそう`, `ない → ないそう`, `N/na-adj + だそう`). Reports what one heard.
- **Ex:** `カタログで見ると、新しい掃除機はよさそうですが、使った人の話によると、あまり便利じゃないそうです。` — Looking at the catalog, the new vacuum cleaner looks good, but according to people who used it, it's not very convenient.

## Lesson 48

### 1. Causative Verb Conjugation (使役形)
- **Group I**: Change the `-i` vowel before `ます` to `-a` and add `せます` (e.g., `てつだいます` → `てつだわせます`, `かきます` → `かかせます`, `いそぎます` → `いそがせます`).
- **Group II**: Replace `ます` with `させます` (e.g., `たべます` → `たべさせます`, `います` → `いさせます`).
- **Group III**: `きます` → `こさせます`, `します` → `させます`.
- **Ex:** `毎日子どもたちを1キロ走らせています。` — They make the children run 1 kilometer every day.

### 2. `N₁ (person) を V (intransitive) causative`
Used with intransitive verbs (`行く`, `走る`, `立つ`, `歩く`, `泳ぐ`, `通う`, `留学する`, etc.). The person caused to perform the action is marked with `を`.
- **Ex:** `息子をイギリスへ留学させます。` — I will let my son study abroad in Britain.

### 3. `N₁ (person) に N₂ を V (transitive) causative`
Used with transitive verbs that already take a direct object marked with `を`. To avoid using `を` twice, the person caused to perform the action is marked with `に`.
- **Ex:** `娘にピアノを習わせます。` — I will have my daughter learn the piano.

### 4. Compulsion vs. Permission (Make vs. Let)
The causative form expresses either making/forcing someone do something (compulsion) or allowing/letting someone do something they wish to do (permission, often with `〜たいと言ったので`).
- **Ex:** `柔道をしたいと言ったので、柔道教室に行かせています。` — Since he said he wanted to do judo, we are letting him go to a judo class.

### 5. `V (causative て-form) + いただけませんか`
Politely asks for permission to do something oneself ("Could you please let me...?").
- **Ex:** `来月7日から10日ほど休みを取らせていただけませんか。` — Could you please let me take about 10 days off from the 7th of next month?

## Lesson 49

### 1. Respectful Expressions (尊敬語 — Sonkeigo)
Honorific/respectful language is used to show respect toward the listener or the person being spoken about by elevating their actions, states, or belongings. It is never used for one's own actions or those of one's in-group (such as family members when speaking to outsiders).

### 2. Honorific Verb Form (`〜れます / 〜られます`)
Uses the same conjugation as passive verbs (Group I: `u`-row → `a`-row + `れます`; Group II: `ます` → `られます`; Group III: `きます` → `こられます`, `します` → `されます`). It is a general respectful form, though slightly less formal than `お〜になります` or special honorific verbs.
- **Ex:** `課長は帰られました。` — The section chief went home.
- **Ex:** `この本は読まれましたか。` — Have you read this book?

### 3. `お + V-ます-stem + になります`
A higher-level respectful pattern formed by attaching `お` to the `ます`-stem of a verb followed by `になります`. Note: Cannot be used with Group III verbs (`きます`, `します`), single-syllable `ます`-stems (e.g. `みます`, `ねます`, `います`), or verbs that already have special honorific equivalents.
- **Ex:** `社長はお帰りになりました。` — The company president went home.
- **Ex:** `部長はさっきお出かけになりました。` — The department manager went out a little while ago.

### 4. Special Honorific Verbs (特別な尊敬語)
Certain common verbs have dedicated respectful equivalents:
- `いきます / きます / います` → `いらっしゃいます`
- `たべます / のみます` → `めしあがります`
- `いいます` → `おっしゃいます`
- `しっています` → `ごぞんじです`
- `みます` → `ごらんになります`
- `します` → `なさいます`
- `くれます` → `くださいます`
- **Ex:** `部長はアメリカへ出張なさいます。` — The department manager is going on a business trip to America.
- **Ex:** `お名前は何とおっしゃいますか。` — What is your name?
- **Ex:** `よく映画をご覧になりますか。` — Do you often watch movies?

### 5. `お + V-ます-stem + ください` / `ご + Noun(Group III stem) + ください`
A polite way to make a request or invite someone to do something, softer and more respectful than `〜てください`. Uses `お` for native Japanese verbs (`ます`-stem) and `ご` for Sino-Japanese (`する`-verb) nouns.
- **Ex:** `しばらくお待ちください。` — Please wait a moment.
- **Ex:** `どうぞお入りください。` — Please come in.
- **Ex:** `ぜひご出席ください。` — Please do attend.

## Lesson 50

### 1. 謙譲語 (Humble Expressions) — `お + Vます-stem + します` / `ご + Group III N + します`
Used when the speaker lowers their own actions out of respect for the listener or the person toward whom the action is directed.
- Group I & II verbs (except single-syllable stems like 見ます, 寝ます, 居ます): `お + Vます-stem + します`.
- Group III noun-verbs (`Nします`): `ご + N + します` (mostly kanji compounds).
- Often used with `～ましょうか` to politely offer help (`お持ちしましょうか`).
- **Ex:** `重そうですね。お持ちしましょうか。` — It looks heavy. Shall I carry it for you?
- **Ex:** `今月のスケジュールをお送りします。` — I will send you this month's schedule.
- **Ex:** `江戸東京博物館へご案内します。` — I will guide you to the Edo-Tokyo Museum.

### 2. Special Humble Verbs (特別な謙譲語)
Certain common verbs have dedicated humble equivalents that replace `お～します`:
- `いきます / きます` → `まいります`
- `います` → `おります`
- `たべます / のみます / もらいます` → `いただきます`
- `いいます` → `もうします`
- `します` → `いたします`
- `しっています` → `ぞんじております` / `しりません` → `ぞんじません`
- `みます` → `はいけんします`
- `ききます / (うちへ) いきます` → `うかがいます`
- `あいます` → `おめにかかります`
- **Ex:** `あした3時に伺います。` — I will visit you at 3 o'clock tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `私はアメリカから参りました。` — I came from America.
- **Ex:** `ちょっと切符を拝見します。` — May I see your ticket for a moment?
- **Ex:** `初めまして。ミラーと申します。` — How do you do? My name is Miller.

### 3. Polite / Humble `～ております` and `～ていただきます`
- `Vて + おります` is the humble/polite equivalent of `Vて + います` (used for one's own state, ongoing action, or that of one's in-group).
- `Vて + いただきます` is the humble equivalent of `Vて + もらいます`.
- **Ex:** `IMCに勤めております。` — I work at IMC.
- **Ex:** `ボランティアの佐野さんにこの教室を紹介していただきました。` — Mr. Sano, a volunteer, introduced this classroom to me.

### 4. Polite Copula / Formal Expressions (`～ております`, `心から感謝いたします`)
- `～いたします` can also follow `N` (especially Sino-Japanese nouns like `感謝`, `緊張`, `留学`) to form formal humble statements.
- **Ex:** `応援してくださった皆様に心から感謝いたします。` — I thank everyone who supported me from the bottom of my heart.
