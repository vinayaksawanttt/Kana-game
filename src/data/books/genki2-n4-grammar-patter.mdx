# Japanese Grammar II (Genki II — JLPT N4)

## Lesson 13: Looking for a Part-time Job (第13課)

### 1. Potential Verbs ("Can do")

Potential verbs express ability or possibility ("can do / is able to do"). All potential verbs conjugate as regular `ru`-verbs (short: `書ける / 書けない / 書けた / 書けなかった / 書けて`; long: `書けます / 書けません`).

- **`Ru`-verbs:** Drop final `-ru` and add `-rareru` (`見る` → `見られる`). Colloquially, shorter `ら`-less forms (`見れる`, `出れる`, `こられる` → `これる`) are common in speech, though not yet sanctioned in formal school grammar.
- **`U`-verbs:** Drop final `-u` and add `-eru` (`行く` → `行ける`, `話す` → `話せる`, `待つ` → `待てる`, `死ぬ` → `死ねる`, `読む` → `読める`, `作る` → `作れる`, `泳ぐ` → `泳げる`, `遊ぶ` → `遊べる`, `買う` → `買える`).
- **Irregular verbs:** `くる` → `こられる`, `する` → `できる`.
- **Particles:** Verbs taking `を` can switch to `が` in potential sentences (`漢字が読める` or `漢字を読める`); `仕事をする` → `仕事ができる` (almost always `が`). Other particles (`山に登る` → `山に登れる`) remain unchanged.
- **Alternative `〜ことができる`:** `Verb dictionary form + ことができる` (`ギターを弾くことができます`) keeps the original `を` particle.
- **Ex:** `私は日本語が話せます。` — I can speak Japanese.
- **Ex:** `私は泳げないんです。` — (The truth is) I cannot swim.
- **Ex:** `雨が降ったので、海に行けませんでした。` — We could not go to the beach, because it rained.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんはギターを弾くことができます。` — Mary can play the guitar.

### 2. `〜し` (Listing Multiple Reasons)

Use `[Short Form] + し` to cite two or more reasons (`(reason₁) し、(reason₂) し、(situation)`). Using a single `〜し` implies there are other unstated reasons. With present-tense `な`-adjectives and nouns, include `だ` (`好きだし`, `学生だし`, never `*学生し`). Very polite speech may use long forms before `し`.

- **Ex:** `日本語はおもしろいし、先生はいいし、私は日本語の授業が大好きです。` — I really like my Japanese class, because Japanese is interesting, and our teacher is good.
- **Ex:** `物価が安いし、この町の生活は楽です。` — Life in this city is easygoing. Things are inexpensive, for one thing.
- **Ex:** `山下先生はいい先生です。教えるのが上手だし、親切だし。` — Professor Yamashita is a great teacher. He is good at teaching, and he is kind.

### 3. `〜そうです` ("It looks like..." — Conjecture from Appearance)

Attach `そうです` to adjective bases or verb stems to guess what something is like based on impressions when lacking conclusive evidence (so don't say `*きれいそうです` when you can already see it's pretty).

- **Formation:** Drop `い` from `い`-adjectives (`おいしい` → `おいしそうです`; exception `いい` → `よさそうです`); drop `な` from `な`-adjectives (`元気(な)` → `元気そうです`).
- **Negative:** Replace `ない` with `なさそう` (`難しくなさそうです`, `上手じゃなさそうです`) or use `〜そうじゃないです`.
- **Noun modifier & verb stems:** `そうな + Noun` (`暖かそうなセーター`); `Verb stem + そうです` for imminent events or impressions (`雨が降りそうです`, `このセーターは家で洗えそうです`).
- **Ex:** `このりんごはおいしそうです。` — This apple looks delicious.
- **Ex:** `あしたは天気がよさそうです。` — It looks like the weather will be fine tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `この本は難しくなさそうです。` — This book does not look difficult.
- **Ex:** `暖かそうなセーターを着ています。` — She is wearing a warm-looking sweater.

### 4. `〜てみる` ("Try doing something")

Attach `みる` (always written in hiragana, conjugates as a `ru`-verb) to a verb `て`-form to express doing something tentatively to see what happens ("try doing...").

- **Ex:** `漢字がわからなかったので、日本人の友だちに聞いてみました。` — I did not know the kanji, so I tried asking a Japanese friend of mine.
- **Ex:** `この本、おもしろかったですよ。 — じゃあ、読んでみます。` — This book was interesting. — Okay, I will take a look at it.

### 5. `なら` (Contrast & Limitation)

`X (Noun) なら Y` means predicate `Y` applies specifically to `X` (contrasting `X` with another item, or limiting a broader category to `X`). Particles `に`, `で`, `から` can optionally appear before `なら` (`チリになら`), while `は`, `が`, `を` never appear with `なら`.

- **Ex:** `チリなら行ったことがありますが、ブラジルは行ったことがありません。` — I've been to Chile, but never to Brazil.
- **Ex:** `日本語が読めますか。 — ひらがななら読めます。` — Can you read Japanese? — If it is (written) in hiragana, yes.

### 6. Frequency over a Period (`[Period] に [Frequency]`)

Express how often an event occurs per time period using `(period) に (frequency)`.

- **Ex:** `一週間に何回髪を洗いますか。 — 私は一週間に三回髪を洗います。` — How many times a week do you shampoo? — I shampoo three times a week.

## Lesson 14: Valentine's Day (第14課)

### 1. `ほしい` ("Want [something]")

`ほしい` ("want [a thing]") conjugates as an `い`-adjective (`ほしくない`, `ほしかった`), marking the desired object with `が` (or `は` in negatives). Like `たい`, `嬉しい`, `悲しい`, and `痛い`, `ほしい` is restricted to first-person descriptions (or direct questions).

- **Third-person desires:** Quote/guess (`ほしいと言っています`, `ほしくないでしょう`) or use the `u`-verb `ほしがる` (`ほしがっています`, which takes **`を`** instead of `が`).
- **Action vs. Possession:** For wanting to *do* or *eat* something, use `Verb + たい` (`昼ご飯が食べたい`), not `ほしい`.
- **Ex:** `いい成績がほしいです。` — I want a good grade.
- **Ex:** `子供の時、ポケモンのゲームがほしかったです。` — When I was young, I wanted a Pokémon game.
- **Ex:** `カルロスさんは友だちをほしがっています。` — (I understand that) Carlos wants a friend.

### 2. `〜かもしれません` ("Maybe / Might")

Attach `かもしれません` (casual `かもしれない` or `かも`) to short forms (affirmative/negative, present/past) to express possibility with lower certainty than `でしょう`. Drop `だ` after present-tense affirmative nouns and `な`-adjectives (`インド人かもしれません`, `きらいかもしれません`).

- **Ex:** `あしたは雨が降るかもしれません。` — It may rain tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `田中さんより、鈴木さんのほうが背が高いかもしれない。` — Suzuki is perhaps taller than Tanaka.
- **Ex:** `ロバートさんは、子供の時、いじわるだったかもしれません。` — Robert may have been a bully when he was a kid.
- **Ex:** `ウデイさんはインド人かもしれません。` — Uday may be an Indian.

### 3. Giving & Receiving Nouns (`あげる / くれる / もらう`)

Japanese distinguishes giving/receiving verbs by direction relative to the speaker's inner circle:

- **`あげる` (moving away / staying away):** I give to you/others; you give to others; others give to others. `(giver) は/が (recipient) に あげる`.
- **`くれる` (moving closer):** You give to me; others give to me/you (or to my close family when I identify with the benefit). `(giver) は/が (recipient) に くれる`.
- **`もらう` (receiving):** `(recipient) は/が (giver) に/から もらう`. Not used when the speaker is the giver (`*(あなたは)私から手紙をもらいましたか` is wrong).
- **Ex:** `私はその女の人に花をあげます。` — I will give the woman flowers.
- **Ex:** `両親が（私に）新しい車をくれるかもしれません。` — My parents may give me a new car.
- **Ex:** `私は姉に／姉からマフラーをもらいました。` — I received a winter scarf from my big sister.
- **Ex:** `姉が私にマフラーをくれました。` — My big sister gave me a winter scarf.

### 4. `〜たらどうですか` (Giving Advice / Recommendation)

Attach `たらどうですか` (casual `たらどう` / `たら`) to the affirmative past short form (`飲んだ` + `らどうですか`) to recommend something. Because it can carry a slightly disapproving/critical tone ("why haven't you done it?"), avoid using it unsolicited, and **never** use it for invitations (use `〜ませんか` instead: `うちに来ませんか`).

- **Ex:** `薬を飲んだらどうですか。` — How about taking some medicine?
- **Ex:** `もっと勉強したらどう。` — Why don't you study harder?

### 5. `Number + も` ("As many as") & `Number + しか + Negative` ("Only / As few as")

Add `も` after a number+counter to emphasize a large quantity ("as many as..."), or add `しか` + **negative predicate** to emphasize a small quantity ("only / as few as...").

- **Ex:** `私の母は猫を三匹も飼っています。` — My mother owns three, count them, three cats.
- **Ex:** `きのうのパーティーには学生が二十人も来ました。` — As many as twenty students showed up at the party yesterday.
- **Ex:** `私は日本語の本を一冊しか持っていません。` — I have only one Japanese book.
- **Ex:** `この会社にはパソコンが二台しかありません。` — There are only two computers in this company.

## Lesson 15: A Trip to Nagano (第15課)

### 1. Volitional Form (Casual "Let's..." / "Shall I...?")

The volitional form is the casual equivalent of `〜ましょう` (used with close friends; add `か` for offers/suggestions like `手伝おうか`).

- **`Ru`-verbs:** Drop `-ru` and add `-yoo` (`見る` → `見よう`).
- **`U`-verbs:** Drop `-u` and add `-oo` (`行く` → `行こう`, `話す` → `話そう`, `待つ` → `待とう`, `死ぬ` → `死のう`, `読む` → `読もう`, `作る` → `作ろう`, `泳ぐ` → `泳ごう`, `遊ぶ` → `遊ぼう`, `買う` → `買おう`).
- **Irregular verbs:** `くる` → `こよう`, `する` → `しよう`.
- **Ex:** `あしたは授業がないから、今晩どこかに行こう。 — いいね。そうしよう。` — We won't have any classes tomorrow. Let's go somewhere this evening. — Sounds good. Let's do so.
- **Ex:** `手伝おうか。 — ううん。大丈夫。` — Shall I lend you a hand? — No, I'm doing okay.

### 2. `Volitional Form + と思っています / と思います` (Intention & Resolution)

- **`Volitional + と思っています`:** Expresses a resolution or decision already made ("I've decided to / I'm going to...").
- **`Volitional + と思います`:** Expresses an intention decided on the spot at the moment of speaking.
- **Contrast with `Present Short Form + と思います`:** `働こうと思います` = intention ("I intend to work"); `働くと思います` = prediction ("I think I/they will work").
- **Ex:** `毎日三時間日本語を勉強しようと思っています。` — I've decided to / I'm going to study Japanese for three hours every day.
- **Ex:** `旅行に行こうと思います。` — I will go on a trip. (decision made on the spot)
- **Ex:** `日本の会社で働こうと思います。` — I will/intend to work for a Japanese company.

### 3. `〜ておく` (Doing Something in Advance / Preparation)

`Verb て-form + おく` (often contracted to `〜とく` in speech) means performing an action in preparation for the future.

- **Ex:** `あした試験があるので、今晩勉強しておきます。` — Since there will be an exam tomorrow, I will study (for it) tonight.
- **Ex:** `友だちが来るから、部屋を掃除しておかなきゃいけません。` — I have to clean the room, because my friends are coming.
- **Ex:** `ホテルを予約しとくね。` — I will make a hotel reservation in advance.

### 4. Using Sentences to Qualify Nouns (Complex Relative Clauses)

Any short-form sentence (present/past, affirmative/negative) can precede and modify a noun as a big noun phrase (`きのう買った本`, `彼がくれた本`, `つくえの上にある本`, `日本で買えない本`). When the subject of the qualifying clause appears inside it, mark it with **`が`** (not `は`).

- **Ex:** `これは去年の誕生日に彼女がくれた本です。` — This is a book that my girlfriend gave me on my birthday last year.
- **Ex:** `父が村上春樹が書いた本をくれました。` — My father gave me a book that Haruki Murakami wrote.
- **Ex:** `私が一番感動した映画は「生きる」です。` — The movie I was touched by the most is "To Live."

## Lesson 16: Lost and Found (第16課)

### 1. Giving & Receiving Services (`〜てあげる / 〜てくれる / 〜てもらう`)

`あげる`, `くれる`, and `もらう` follow verb `て`-forms to express doing or receiving favors.

- **`〜てあげる`:** Doing something as a favor for someone else. Keep the main verb's particle for the beneficiary (`妹にお金を貸してあげました`), or use `〜のために` if the verb lacks a beneficiary slot (`ゆいさんのために買い物に行きました`).
- **`〜てくれる`:** Someone does something for me/us (beneficiary is usually omitted; if stated, keeps the main verb's particle or `私のために`).
- **`〜てもらう`:** Subject is the beneficiary who gets/persuades someone (`に`) to do something for them (or acknowledges goodwill: `知らない人に漢字を読んでもらいました`).
- **Ex:** `私は妹にお金を貸してあげました。` — I (generously) lent my sister money.
- **Ex:** `友だちが宿題を手伝ってくれます。` — A friend helps me with my homework.
- **Ex:** `私は友だちに宿題を手伝ってもらいました。` — I got a friend of mine to help me with my homework.

### 2. Polite & Casual Requests (`〜ていただけませんか / 〜てくれませんか / 〜てくれない？`)

Request favors with varying levels of politeness (descending order: `〜ていただけませんか` > `〜てくださいませんか` > `〜てもらえませんか` > `〜てくれませんか` > `〜てもらえない？` > `〜てくれない？` / bare `〜て`).

- **`〜ていただけませんか`:** Polite request to superiors (professors, bosses) or strangers (from `いただける`, potential of humble `いただく`).
- **`〜てくれませんか`:** Polite-casual request, ideal for host families; roughly equal to `〜てください`.
- **`〜てくれない？`:** Very casual request among peers.
- **Ex:** `手伝っていただけませんか。` — Would you lend me a hand?
- **Ex:** `ちょっと待ってくれませんか。` — Will you wait for a second?
- **Ex:** `それ取ってくれない？` — Pick that thing up (and pass it to me), will you?

### 3. Expressing Hope (`〜といいですね / 〜といいんですが`)

Attach `といいですね` (casual `といいね`) to present-tense short forms to wish good luck for **others**, or `といいんですが` (casual `といいんだけど`) to modestly express hope for **yourself** (or indirect requests with `〜てくれるといいんですが`). When hoping to *do* something under your control, use the **potential form** (`大学に行けるといいんですが`, not `*行くといいんですが`).

- **Ex:** `いいアルバイトが見つかるといいですね。` — I hope you find a good part-time job.
- **Ex:** `試験がやさしいといいんですが。` — I am hoping that the exam is easy.
- **Ex:** `大学に行けるといいんですが。` — I am hoping to go to college.

### 4. Temporal Clauses (`[Clause A] 時、[Clause B]`)

`[Sentence A (short form)] 時、[Sentence B]` means "When A, B." Determine Clause A's tense by placing yourself at the time of main event B:

- **Present tense in A:** Event A has **not yet happened** at the time of B (`寝る時、電気を消します`; `チベットに行く時、ビザを取りました` — visa was obtained *before* going), or A is an **ongoing state/action** during B (`さびしい時`, `テレビを見ている時` — never `*行っている時`). `な`-adj take `な`, nouns take `の` before `時` (`病気の時`).
- **Past tense in A:** Event A **already happened** before B (`中国に行った時、ウーロン茶を買います` — buying tea *after* arriving in China; `財布を忘れた時`).
- **Ex:** `チベットに行く時、ビザを取ります。` — When I go to Tibet, I will get a visa.
- **Ex:** `出かける時、ドアにかぎをかけました。` — When I went out, I locked the door.
- **Ex:** `中国に行った時、ウーロン茶を買いました。` — I bought oolong tea when I went to China.

### 5. Apologizing for Actions (`〜てすみませんでした / 〜なくてすみませんでした`)

Apologize for something you did with `Verb て-form + すみませんでした` (casual `ごめん`), or for something you **failed to do** with the negative `て`-form `〜なくてすみませんでした` (`〜ない` → drop `い` + `くて`).

- **Ex:** `デートの約束を忘れてごめん。` — Sorry that I stood you up.
- **Ex:** `宿題を持ってこなくてすみませんでした。` — I'm sorry for not bringing the homework.

## Lesson 17: Grumble and Gossip (第17課)

### 1. `〜そうです` ("I hear that..." — Hearsay Report)

Unlike Lesson 13's visual/impression `そうです`, hearsay `そうです` ("I hear that...") attaches to **complete short-form predicates** (retaining original tense/polarity; nouns and `な`-adjectives keep `だ`: `親切だそうです`, `学生だそうです`). It is invariant (not negated as `*そうじゃないです`), and you can cite the source with `〜によると` ("according to...").

- **Ex:** `日本語の授業は楽しいそうです。` — I've heard that their Japanese class is fun.
- **Ex:** `先生はとても親切だそうです。` — I've heard that their professor is very kind.
- **Ex:** `天気予報によると、台風が来るそうです。` — According to the weather forecast, a typhoon is approaching.

### 2. `〜って` (Informal Hearsay & Quotation)

In casual speech, `〜って` replaces `そうです` at the end of a sentence (`忙しいって`, `あるんだって`), or replaces quotation particle `と` before verbs like `言う` (`何て言ってた？` — note `って` becomes `て` after `ん`).

- **Ex:** `メアリーさん、今日は忙しいって。あした、試験があるんだって。` — Mary says she's busy today. She says she has an exam tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `チョコレートを食べすぎたって言ってた。` — He said he ate too much chocolate.

### 3. Conditional `〜たら` ("If / When")

Add `ら` to any past-tense short form (`読んだら`, `読まなかったら`, `やさしかったら`, `静かだったら`, `休みだったら`) to mean "if / when A, then B."

- **Uses:** (1) Open/possible conditions (`会ったら聞こうと思います`), (2) probable temporal sequence ("once/when A happens, B" — `宿題が終わったら`), and (3) counterfactual/hypothetical wishes (`私が猫だったら`).
- **Constraint:** B can only happen **at or after** the time A becomes true (cannot say `*来週試験があったら、今週末勉強したほうがいい`), and `たら` requires natural dependence (not "even if").
- **Ex:** `日本に行ったら、着物を買います。` — I will buy kimono if and when I go to Japan.
- **Ex:** `天気がよくなかったら、キャンプに行きません。` — We will not go camping, if the weather is not good.
- **Ex:** `百万円あったら、車を買うんですけど。` — If I had a million yen, I would buy a car.

### 4. `〜なくてもいいです` ("Do not need to...")

Take the negative short form, drop final `い`, and add `くてもいいです` (casual `も` optional: `〜なくていい`) to say something is unnecessary ("you don't have to...").

- **Ex:** `靴を脱がなくてもいいです。` — You do not need to take off your shoes.
- **Ex:** `プレゼントは高くなくてもいいです。` — The present does not need to be anything expensive.

### 5. `〜みたいです` ("It looks/acts like...")

Attach `みたいです` directly to nouns ("looks/acts like [Noun]") or to short-form verbs/adjectives (present/past, affirmative/negative) to mean "it appears to be the case that..." (can even be used about yourself when uncertain: `財布を忘れたみたいです`).

- **Ex:** `私の父はカーネルおじさんみたいです。` — My dad looks/acts like Colonel Sanders, the KFC founder.
- **Ex:** `雨が降ったみたいですね。` — It looks like it has rained, doesn't it?
- **Ex:** `あの人はきのうの夜寝なかったみたいです。` — It looks like that person did not sleep last night.

### 6. Sequence of Actions (`〜前に` vs. `〜てから`)

- **`[Verb A (Present Short Form)] + 前に、[Verb B]`:** "B happens before A." Verb A is **always** in the present tense, even when Verb B is past (`日本に来る前に、勉強しました`).
- **`[Verb A て-form] + から、[Verb B]`:** "After A, B" (or "since A happened, state B has held true": `猫が死んでから、とてもさびしいです`).
- **Ex:** `ジーンズを買う前に、はいてみます。` — I try on jeans before I buy them.
- **Ex:** `勉強してから、遊びに行きました。` — I studied and then went out.

## Lesson 18: John's Part-time Job (第18課)

### 1. Transitivity Pairs (Transitive `他動詞` vs. Intransitive `自動詞`)

Many Japanese verbs come in transitive/intransitive pairs: **transitive** verbs take an agent (`が`/`は`) and direct object (`を`), while **intransitive** verbs describe a change happening to the subject (`が`) alone.

- **Key pairs:** `開ける / 開く`, `閉める / 閉まる`, `入れる / 入る`, `出す / 出る`, `つける / つく`, `消す / 消える`, `壊す / 壊れる`, `汚す / 汚れる`, `落とす / 落ちる`, `沸かす / 沸く`.
- **Ex:** `たけしさんが電気をつけました。` — Takeshi turned the light on.
- **Ex:** `電気がつきました。` — The light came on.

### 2. Paired Intransitive Verbs + `ている` (Resultant State)

With `〜ている`, **transitive** activity verbs describe an action currently in progress (`ウデイさんは窓を開けています`), whereas paired **intransitive** change verbs describe the **resulting state** (`窓が開いています` — "The windows are open").

- **Ex:** `電気は消えています。` — The light is off.
- **Ex:** `このパソコンは壊れています。` — This computer is broken.

### 3. `〜てしまう` (Completion & Regret)

`Verb て-form + しまう` has two meanings depending on context: **(1) finishing completely** ("carried out to completion") and **(2) regrettable/unintended occurrence** ("accidentally / unfortunately..."). In speech, `〜てしまう` → `〜ちゃう` and `〜でしまう` → `〜じゃう`. Because it uses the affirmative `て`-form, it is not negated to mean "regrettably didn't."

- **Ex:** `本を読んでしまいました。` — I read the book completely. / I finished reading the book.
- **Ex:** `電車の中にかばんを忘れてしまいました。` — I inadvertently left my bag on the train.
- **Ex:** `宿題をなくしちゃった。` — I lost my homework!

### 4. Habitual/Natural Conditional `〜と` ("Whenever / If")

`[Clause A (Present Short Form)] + と、[Clause B]` expresses an inevitable, natural, or habitual cause-and-effect ("Whenever A happens, B happens"). Clause B can be present or past, often using adjective + `なる` for seasonal/natural changes, and **cannot** express speaker commands/requests/volition (e.g., `*話すとカフェに行きます` is unnatural).

- **Ex:** `私はその人と話すと元気になる。` — Whenever I talk with that person, I feel uplifted.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんが国に帰るとさびしくなります。` — If Mary goes back home, we will be sad and lonely.
- **Ex:** `秋になると木が赤くなります。` — Whenever fall arrives, trees turn red.

### 5. Simultaneous Actions (`〜ながら`)

`[Verb₁ Stem] + ながら、[Verb₂]` means doing two actions simultaneously ("while V₁-ing, V₂"). Both actions **must** be performed by the **same person** (use `〜時` when two different people act simultaneously).

- **Ex:** `私はいつも音楽を聞きながら日本語を勉強します。` — I always study Japanese while listening to music.
- **Ex:** `アルバイトをしながら学校に行くのは大変です。` — It is not easy to go to school while holding a part-time job.

### 6. Past Regret (`〜ばよかったです / 〜なければよかったです`)

Express past regret ("I wish I had... / I should have...") using the `ば`-form + `よかったです`.

- **Affirmative `ば`-form:** Drop final `-u` and add `-eba` (`食べる` → `食べれば`, `行く` → `行けば`, `待つ` → `待てば`, `買う` → `買えば`, `する` → `すれば`, `くる` → `くれば`).
- **Negative `ば`-form:** Drop final `い` from `〜ない` and add `ければ` (`食べない` → `食べなければ`).
- **Ex:** `あの時、「愛している」と言えばよかったです。` — I wish I had told her that I loved her.
- **Ex:** `彼女と別れなければよかったです。` — I should not have broken up with her.

### 7. Expression Note (`〜のおかげで / おかげさまで`)

`Noun のおかげ（で）` expresses gratitude when something turns out well ("thanks to..."). `おかげさまで` is the standard polite reply to `元気ですか` or inquiries about progress.

- **Ex:** `友だちが書いてくれた地図のおかげで道に迷わなかった。` — Thanks to the map my friend drew, I didn't get lost.

## Lesson 19: Meeting the Boss (第19課)

### 1. Honorific Verbs (`尊敬語` — Exalting the Subject)

Honorific language elevates the person performing an action (social superiors or people you don't know well—**never** use honorific verbs for your own actions!).

- **Special Honorific Verbs (`u`-verbs with irregular `-ます` stems ending in `-います`):**
  - `いる / 行く / 来る` → `いらっしゃる` (`いらっしゃいます`; also compounds like `連れていらっしゃる`)
  - `食べる / 飲む` → `召し上がる` (`召し上がります`)
  - `くれる` → `くださる` (`くださいます`)
  - `する` → `なさる` (`なさいます`; also compounds like `勉強なさる`)
  - `寝る` → `お休みになる`
  - `見る` → `ご覧になる`
  - `言う` → `おっしゃる` (`おっしゃいます`)
  - `〜ている` → `〜ていらっしゃる` (`〜ていらっしゃいます`)
- **General Honorific Pattern:** `お + [Verb Stem] + になる` (`お帰りになりました`, `お読みになったことがありますか`). Post-predicate expressions like `〜ことがあります` and `〜ください` remain unchanged.
- **Ex:** `先生は今日学校にいらっしゃいません。` — The professor will (graciously) not go to/come to/be at the school.
- **Ex:** `何を召し上がりますか。` — What will you (graciously) eat/drink?
- **Ex:** `先生はもうお帰りになりました。` — The professor has already (graciously) gone home.

### 2. Respectful Advice / Courteous Commands (`お + [Verb Stem] + ください`)

`お + [Verb Stem] + ください` (or `ご + [Compound する Noun] + ください`, e.g., `ご注意ください`, `ご覧ください`, `お召し上がりください`, `お休みください`) gives courteous instructions for the listener's own benefit (like in announcements/stores). Do **not** use it to ask favors for yourself (use `〜ていただけませんか` instead).

- **Ex:** `切符をお取りください。` — Please take a ticket.
- **Ex:** `説明をお読みください。` — Please read the instruction.

### 3. Specific Gratitude (`〜てくれてありがとう / 〜てくださってありがとうございました`)

Thank someone for a specific action using `Verb て-form + くれてありがとう` (casual/peers) or `〜てくださってありがとうございました` (polite/superiors; use `ありがとうございます` for ongoing kindness). Use `〜ていてくれてありがとう` for "thank you for being..."

- **Ex:** `手伝ってくれてありがとう。` — Thank you for helping me out.
- **Ex:** `推薦状を書いてくださってありがとうございました。` — Thank you for writing a letter of recommendation for me.

### 4. Relief / Gladness (`〜てよかったです / 〜なくてよかったです`)

Express relief or gladness about a past/present fact using `Verb て-form + よかったです` ("I'm glad that...") or negative `〜なくてよかったです` ("I'm glad I didn't...").

- **Ex:** `日本語を勉強してよかったです。` — I'm glad that I have studied Japanese.
- **Ex:** `約束の時間に遅れなくてよかったです。` — I'm glad that I was not late for the appointment.

### 5. Logical Expectation (`〜はずです`)

`[Short Form] + はずです` expresses a confident expectation based on evidence, duty, or common sense ("is supposed to be / must be"). Keep `な` after `な`-adjectives (`元気なはずです`) and `の` after nouns (`日本人のはずです`). Past tense `はずでした` describes something that was supposed to happen but didn't. Strong negative disbelief uses `〜はずがありません / はずがない`.

- **Ex:** `今日は日曜日だから、銀行は閉まっているはずです。` — Banks must be closed, because today is a Sunday.
- **Ex:** `レポートの締め切りはあしたじゃないはずです。` — I believe that the paper is not due tomorrow.
- **Ex:** `先週電話をもらうはずでしたが、電話がありませんでした。` — I was supposed to receive a phone call last week, but I didn't.

## Lesson 20: Mary Goes Shopping (第20課)

### 1. Extra-Modest Expressions (`丁寧語・丁重語`)

Extra-modest expressions lower your own actions (or your in-group's/company's actions) to show heightened deference to the **listener** (like adding "sir/ma'am" in formal business/interviews/announcements, often paired with `わたくし`). **Never** use them for the listener's actions!

- **Core vocabulary:** `いる` → `おります` (`おる`), `行く/来る` → `まいります` (`まいる`), `言う` → `申します` (`申す`), `する` → `いたします` (`いたす`), `食べる/飲む` → `いただきます` (`いただく`), `ある` → `ございます` (`ござる`), `〜ている` → `〜ております`, `〜です` → `〜でございます`.
- **Ex:** `私は来年も日本におります。` — I will be in Japan next year, too, sir/ma'am.
- **Ex:** `私は木村たけしと申します。` — My name is Takeshi Kimura.
- **Ex:** `お手洗いは二階でございます。` — The bathroom is on the second floor.

### 2. Humble Expressions (`謙譲語` — Demoting the Actor to Respect the Recipient)

Humble expressions lower the actor ("I" or an in-group member) to show respect to the **person receiving or affected by the action** (contrasting with Lesson 19's `お + stem + になる` which elevates the actor).

- **Pattern:** `お + [Verb Stem] + する` (`お会いしました`, `お貸しする`, `お送りします`).
- **`する` compound verbs:** Prefix `ご` or `お` + `する` (`ご紹介する`, `ご案内する`, `ご説明する`, `お電話する`).
- **Special humble verbs:** `もらう` → `いただく`, `あげる` → `さしあげる` (note: avoid `〜てさしあげる`, prefer `お + stem + する` like `お見せしました`), `行く(visit) / 聞く(ask)` → `うかがう`.
- **Ex:** `私はきのう社長のご主人にお会いしました。` — I (humbly) met husband of the company president yesterday.
- **Ex:** `私は先生にこの本をいただきました。` — I (humbly) received this book from my professor.
- **Ex:** `私は部長のお宅にうかがいました。` — I (humbly) visited my department manager's house.

### 3. `〜ないで` ("Without doing X")

Attach `で` to the present negative short form (`〜ない`) to mean "without doing X" (works for both present and past main verbs).

- **Ex:** `きのうの夜は、寝ないで、勉強しました。` — Last night, I studied without getting any sleep.
- **Ex:** `辞書を使わないで、新聞を読みます。` — I read newspapers without using dictionaries.

### 4. Embedded Questions within Larger Sentences (`〜か / 〜かどうか`)

Embed a question inside a larger sentence using short forms:

- **With question words (`何`, `どこ`, `だれ`):** End with `か` (`きのう何を食べたか覚えていません`). Drop `だ` after present affirmative nouns/`な`-adjectives (`だれが一番上手か`).
- **Yes/No questions:** End with `かどうか` ("whether or not"; casual speech often uses just `か`).
- **Embedded `〜んです` questions:** Drop `だ` and change `ん` to `の` (`どうして来なかったのかわかりません`). Subject `は` often shifts to `が` inside embedded clauses.
- **Ex:** `メアリーさんがどこに住んでいるか知っていますか。` — Do you know where Mary lives?
- **Ex:** `週末、旅行に行くかどうか決めましょう。` — Let's decide whether we will go on a trip this weekend.
- **Ex:** `あの人が学生かどうか分かりません。` — I don't know if that person is a student.

### 5. `[Name] という [Item]` ("An [item] called [name]")

Introduce an unfamiliar name of a person, place, or thing using `(name) という (item)`.

- **Ex:** `ポチという犬（を飼っていました。）` — (I used to have) a dog called "Pochi."
- **Ex:** `「花」という歌（を知っていますか。）` — (Do you know) a song called "Hana"?

### 6. Ease & Difficulty (`[Verb Stem] + やすい / にくい`)

Attach `やすい` ("easy to...") or `にくい` ("hard to...") to a verb stem; both conjugate as `い`-adjectives (`読みやすかったです`). Often focuses on psychological ease/usability rather than statistical difficulty (`漢字は覚えにくい` vs. `漢字を覚えるのは難しい`). Note: standalone `やすい (安い)` means "cheap," whereas "easy" is `やさしい`.

- **Ex:** `この電子レンジは使いやすいです。` — This microwave oven is easy to use.
- **Ex:** `骨が多いので、魚は食べにくいです。` — Fish are hard to eat, because they have many bones.
- **Ex:** `この町はとても住みやすいです。` — This town is quite livable.

## Lesson 21: Burglar (第21課)

### 1. Passive Sentences (`受身` — Suffering / Affective & Direct Passive)

In Japanese, passive sentences often express being **inconvenienced/adversely affected** ("affective passive": `(victim) は (villain) に (action) られる`), though neutral/favorable passives (`誘われました`, `尊敬されています`) and inanimate **direct passives** (agent marked with `によって` in formal writing: `この絵はピカソによって描かれました`) also exist. All passive verbs conjugate as `ru`-verbs.

- **Formation:** `Ru`-verbs drop `-ru` + `-rareru` (`食べる` → `食べられる`); `U`-verbs drop `-u` + `-areru` (`行く` → `行かれる`, `読む` → `読まれる`, `買う` → `買われる` with intervening `w`); Irregular `くる` → `こられる`, `する` → `される`. (Note: `u`-verb passives `読まれる` differ from potentials `読める`!)
- **Constraint:** Cannot use passive for adjectives or negated verbs (`*天気に悪くされた`).
- **Ex:** `私はとなりの人にたばこを吸われました。` — I was annoyed with the person sitting next to me for smoking.
- **Ex:** `山下先生はだれかにパスワードを盗まれたそうです。` — I hear that Professor Yamashita had his password stolen by someone.
- **Ex:** `私はその人にデートに誘われました。` — I was asked out by that person for a date.

### 2. Intentional Resultant State (`〜が + [Transitive Verb て-form] + ある`)

`[Noun が] + [Transitive Verb て-form] + ある` describes a current state that was **intentionally arranged on purpose** by someone (often paired with prior `〜ておきました`). Contrast with intransitive `〜ている` (`窓が閉まっている`, neutral state without implying human intent) vs. `〜てある` (`窓が閉めてある`, kept closed on purpose).

- **Ex:** `寒いので、ヒーターがつけてあります。` — The heater is on, because it is cold.
- **Ex:** `テーブルの上に花が置いてあります。` — Flowers are on the table.
- **Ex:** `レストランの予約がしてあります。` — A restaurant reservation has been made.

### 3. Temporal "While / During" (`〜間に` vs. `〜間`)

- **`A 間に B`:** Event B (short duration) happens **at some point during** ongoing event A (`〜ている`, state verb like `いる`, or noun `留守の間`). Verb A is always in the present tense.
- **`A 間 B` (without `に`):** Event B lasts **throughout the entire duration** of A.
- **Ex:** `お風呂に入っている間に電話がありました。` — There was a phone call while I was taking a bath.
- **Ex:** `両親が日本にいる間に京都に連れていきたいです。` — I want to take my parents to Kyoto while they are in Japan.
- **Ex:** `ルームメイトが買い物をしている間、私は本を読んで待ちました。` — I waited, reading a book, while my roommate was shopping.

### 4. `Adjective + する` ("To make something [Adjective]")

Combine adjectives with `する` ("to make something [adj]"): `い`-adj drops `い` + `くする` (`冷たくする`, `いい` → `よくする`); `な`-adj adds `にする` (`簡単にする`, `静かにする`).

- **Ex:** `次の試験はやさしくしてください。` — Please make the next exam easier.
- **Ex:** `部屋をきれいにしました。` — I cleaned the room.
- **Ex:** `静かにしてください。` — Please be quiet!

### 5. `[Person に] + [Verb て-form] + ほしい` ("I want [someone] to do...")

Express what you want another person to do using `(私は) [Person に] + [Verb て-form] + ほしい`. Negative forms: `〜てほしくないです` or `〜ないでほしいです`. For third-party wishes, quote or use `ほしがっています`.

- **Summary of "want":** `Verb stem + たい` (want to do), `Noun がほしい` (want something), `Verb て + ほしい` (want someone to do).
- **Ex:** `私は病気の友だちに元気になってほしいです。` — I want my sick friend to get well.
- **Ex:** `私は日本人の友だちに英語で話さないでほしいです。` — I don't want my Japanese friends to speak in English.

## Lesson 22: Education in Japan (第22課)

### 1. Causative Sentences (`使役` — "Make / Let someone do")

Causative verbs express either **making** someone do something or **letting** someone do something (`(director) は/が (cast) に [causative verb]`). All causatives conjugate as `ru`-verbs.

- **Formation:** `Ru`-verbs drop `-ru` + `-saseru` (`食べる` → `食べさせる`); `U`-verbs drop `-u` + `-aseru` (`行く` → `行かせる`, `買う` → `買わせる` with `w`); Irregular `くる` → `こさせる`, `する` → `させる`.
- **Particle `を` vs. `に` for the cast:** Use **`を`** for reflexive/involuntary actions (`子供を泣かせてしまいました`, `みんなを笑わせました`) or when an intransitive verb lacks a direct object `を` (`私をトイレに行かせました`, `私をそこに座らせました`). If the verb already takes `を`, use `に` (`子供に本を読ませました`).
- **Ex:** `先生は学生に会話を覚えさせました。` — The professor made the students memorize the dialogue.
- **Ex:** `お父さんは子供に野菜を食べさせました。` — The father made/let his child eat vegetables.

### 2. `Causative + てあげる / てくれる / てもらう / てください` ("Let / Allow someone to do")

Combining a causative verb with giving/receiving helpers (`〜させてあげる / くれる / もらう`) unambiguously indicates **permission ("let/allow")**. Use `Causative + てください` to politely ask permission or volunteer to do something.

- **Ex:** `先生は私に英語を話させてくれませんでした。` — The professor did not allow me to speak in English.
- **Ex:** `私は自分の子供に好きなことをさせてあげるつもりです。` — I think I will let my children do what they love.
- **Ex:** `私にこの仕事をやらせてください。` — Please let me do this job.

### 3. Commands (`[Verb Stem] + なさい` & Imperative Form)

- **`[Verb Stem] + なさい`:** A firm command from a superior/parent/teacher or in exam instructions ("Do...!"). Negative instructions use `〜のをやめなさい` ("Stop doing...").
- **Imperative forms (direct/aggressive, or cheering/signs like `止まれ`, `がんばれ`):** `Ru`-verbs change `-ru` to `-ro` (`やめろ`; exception `くれる` → `くれ`); `U`-verbs change `-u` to `-e` (`死ね`); Irregular `する` → `しろ`, `くる` → `こい`. Negative imperative: `[Dictionary Form] + な` (`あきらめるな`).
- **Ex:** `十時までに帰りなさい。` — Come home by 10 o'clock.
- **Ex:** `文句を言うのをやめなさい。` — Stop complaining.

### 4. Conditional `〜ば` ("If A, then B")

`A ば B` states a condition where `A` normally guarantees a good or expected result in `B` (often used for advice with `大丈夫です` or `いいんです`). Also works with `い`-adjectives (`おもしろければ / おもしろくなければ`), negative predicates (`元気じゃなければ`), and `な`-adjectives/nouns (`なら` or `であれば`).

- **Ex:** `車があれば、いろいろな所に行けます。` — If you have a car, you can go to various places.
- **Ex:** `この薬を飲めば大丈夫です。` — You will be okay, if you take this medicine.
- **Ex:** `先生に聞けばいいんです。` — All you have to do is ask the teacher.

### 5. Contrast / "Despite" (`〜のに`)

`A (Short Form) のに B` connects two surprising or contradictory facts ("Even though A, B" / "Despite A, B"). Insert `な` after present-tense affirmative nouns and `な`-adjectives (`親切なのに`, `大きい問題なのに`). Because `のに` connects two **facts**, Clause B cannot be a request, command, or suggestion (use `〜が / 〜けど` instead).

- **Ex:** `この会社はお金があるのに、給料は安いです。` — This company is rich, but its workers' salaries are low.
- **Ex:** `八時間も勉強したのに、試験ができなかった。` — I studied for eight hours, but I couldn't do well on the examination.
- **Ex:** `大きい問題なのに、あの人はだれにも相談しません。` — It is a big issue, but he does not consult with anybody.

### 6. Simile & Resemblance (`[Noun A] のように / [Noun A] のような [Noun B]`)

Use `Noun A のように` before verbs/adjectives ("just like A") and `Noun A のような Noun B` before nouns ("a B like A"). (Casual `みたい` works similarly without `の`: `ぬいぐるみみたいに`, `マザー・テレサみたいな人`.)

- **Ex:** `メアリーさんは魚のように上手に泳げます。` — Mary can swim very well, just like a fish.
- **Ex:** `私は鎌倉のような町が好きです。` — I like towns like Kamakura.

## Lesson 23: Good-bye (第23課)

### 1. Causative-Passive Sentences (`使役受身` — "Be forced/made to do")

Causative-passive expresses being forced, harassed, or talked into doing something you didn't want to do (`(puppet) は (puppet master) に [causative-passive]`). All causative-passives conjugate as `ru`-verbs.

- **Formation:**
  - `Ru`-verbs: drop `-ru` + `-sase-rare-ru` (`食べる` → `食べさせられる`).
  - `U`-verbs ending in `す`: drop `-u` + `-ase-rare-ru` (`話す` → `話させられる`).
  - All other `u`-verbs (shortened `-asare-ru` form, much more common than `-aserareru`): drop `-u` + `-asare-ru` (`行く` → `行かされる`, `待つ` → `待たされる`, `読む` → `読まされる`, `取る` → `取らされる`, `泳ぐ` → `泳がされる`, `遊ぶ` → `遊ばされる`, `買う` → `買わされる` with `w`).
  - Irregular: `する` → `させられる`, `くる` → `こさせられる`.
- **Ex:** `歌を歌わされました。` — I was forced to sing.
- **Ex:** `いつも野菜を食べさせられます。` — I am (always) made to eat vegetables.
- **Ex:** `私は彼女に車を洗わされました。` — I was tricked by my girlfriend into washing her car.

### 2. Concessive Conditional `〜ても / 〜でも` ("Even if / Even though")

Add `も` to the `て`-form of verbs (`買っても / 買わなくても`) and `い`-adjectives (`悲しくても / 悲しくなくても`), or add `でも` to `な`-adjectives and nouns (`元気でも / 元気じゃなくても`, `学生でも / 学生じゃなくても` — never `*元気だっても`). With question words (`だれに聞いても`, `いつ行っても`), it means "no matter who/when..."

- **Ex:** `雨が降っても、バーベキューをします。` — We will have a barbecue, even if it rains.
- **Ex:** `子供でも、わかります。` — Even a child will get it.
- **Ex:** `私は、かぜをひいていても、毎日、授業に行きました。` — I went to class every day, even when I had a cold.
- **Ex:** `いつ行っても、東京はにぎやかです。` — No matter when you visit, Tokyo is lively.

### 3. `〜ことにする` ("Decide to do")

Attach `ことにする` to present-tense short-form verbs (affirmative or negative) to express a personal decision (`〜ことにしました`). Volitional `〜ことにしよう` suggests a decision after deliberation.

- **Ex:** `車を買うことにしました。` — We have decided to buy a car.
- **Ex:** `あの人がかわいそうだから、あまり文句を言わないことにします。` — I will not make too many complaints.
- **Ex:** `今年の夏はベトナムに行くことにしよう。` — Let's take the plunge. Let's go to Vietnam this summer.

### 4. `〜ことにしている` ("Make it a rule/habit to do")

Attach `ことにしている` to present-tense short-form verbs to describe a regular personal habit or rule you have decided and stuck to.

- **Ex:** `毎日十一時までに寝ることにしています。` — I make it a rule to go to bed by eleven every night.
- **Ex:** `絶対にお酒を飲まないことにしています。` — I have made this firm decision not to drink and have strictly followed it.

### 5. `[Verb Present Affirmative Short Form] + まで` ("Until A happens")

`[Verb A (Present Affirmative Short Form)] + まで、B` means "B continues until change A occurs." Verb A is **always** in the present affirmative, even when B is in the past (`慣れるまで、大変でした`). If A and B have different subjects, mark A's subject with `が` (`赤ちゃんが寝るまで`).

- **Ex:** `晴れるまで、カフェでコーヒーを飲みながら、待ちます。` — I will wait in the cafe, drinking coffee, till it clears up.
- **Ex:** `日本語が上手になるまで、国に帰りません。` — I will not go back to my country, till I become fluent in Japanese.
- **Ex:** `日本の生活に慣れるまで、大変でした。` — It was tough until I got used to living in Japan.

### 6. `[Verb Stem] + 方（かた）` ("How to do / Way of doing")

Attach `方 (かた)` to a verb stem to form a noun meaning "how to do... / way of doing..." (`泳ぎ方`, `考え方`). Objects/goals that normally take `を` or `に` shift to **`の`** (or `への` for `に`) before `〜方` (`漢字の読み方`, `空港の行き方 / 空港への行き方`, `日本語の勉強の仕方`).

- **Ex:** `この野菜の食べ方はいろいろあります。` — There are many ways to eat this vegetable.
- **Ex:** `すみません。この漢字の書き方を教えていただけませんか。` — Excuse me, can you tell me how to write this kanji?
